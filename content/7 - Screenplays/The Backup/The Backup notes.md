Questions:
- Why wasn't there a press conference after failure of Apollo 1?
	- While there was a formal **press conference on March 21, 1966**, to announce the crew, the primary reason for the perceived lack of high-profile media coverage _immediately following_ the mission's failure was that it never actually launched. [[1](https://sma.nasa.gov/sma-disciplines/accllp/apollo-1), [2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HOqXHOMDKqQ&t=176)]
	- The three astronauts—**Gus Grissom**, **Ed White**, and **Roger Chaffee**—were killed in a cabin fire during a routine "plugs-out" ground test on January 27, 1967. [[1](https://twit.tv/posts/tech/remembering-nasas-saddest-moments-what-apollo-1-challenger-and-columbia-taught-us), [2](https://www.bbc.com/future/article/20170125-the-fire-may-have-saved-the-apollo-programme)]
	- **Mission Classification:** Because the fire occurred during a pre-launch test rather than a live flight, there was no "post-flight" press conference like those held for successful missions (e.g., Apollo 11).
