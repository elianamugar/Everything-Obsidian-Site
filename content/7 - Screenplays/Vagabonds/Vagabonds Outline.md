**Act 1** - Exposition[^1]
- **15 YEARS LEFT**
- Establish Scout's pace of life, routines
	- Needs to unpack, but does it slowly over time.
		- Only goes to pubs to listen to music because her music system is still packed.
- Establish Caroline's pace of life, routines
	- Night job as bartender.
	- Normally goes for stationed people at pub.
- Caroline's routine breaks - Ed "fires" her, leaves job in two weeks.
- Scout and Caroline finally talk to each other.
	- Caroline slips her phone number into Scout's jacket before Scot arrives.
- Scout finishes unpacking, has no reason to show up at the pub anymore.
- Caroline's day job is established - library
	- Scout looks for a book there.
	- Caroline tries to figure out Scout. Doesn't get a satisfying answer.
	- **Tomorrow night**: They agree to meet at the pub tomorrow night (after the Ball) so Caroline can lend **her** book to Scout.

**Act 2** - Rising Action[^2]
- **That night**: Scout and Caroline meet at RAF Ball - reveals Scout's name and job.
	- The Commander revealed as Caroline's father.
- **Next day:** Caroline and her father (The Commander) meet at a cafe to discuss the move to London.
- **Same day:**, Scout goes through her routine, but gets hit by a car on the way home.
- Scout doesn't show up to the pub. 
	- Caroline returns home with another stranger.
	- Caroline gets a phone call from the hospital.
- At the hospital, Scout asks what happened to the dog.
	- Caroline takes the phone number for the pound.
	- Caroline gives Scout the book.
	- Caroline lets Scout know about her move to London in two weeks.
	- Establish Scout's dilemma of *home*.
- Establish Scout's inability to let people *in* (both literally into her home and emotionally). 
	- Doesn't let Caroline help her after hospital. Doesn't let Caroline into her home.
- Scout forced to take care of herself. The distraction of work disappears - she's forced to *rest*. Scout's high-functioning depression established.
	- Scout resists rest. She pushes herself past her injuries.
- **One week later**: Caroline shows up to check on Scout. Scout has spiraled - very sickly.
	- Scout still shows little acts of service for Caroline.
	- Caroline wakes up to Scout still overworking herself.
	- Scout wants to go to the pound to see the dog. Caroline says no, until Scout feels better.
- **That night**: Ed and Caroline chat at pub. One more week until the London move.
- **Days later:** Scout is visibly much better, doing architectural sketches.
- **That morning:** Scout calls Caroline to go to the pound.
- **Same day:** They pick up the dog (Shelby).
	- They get to know each other better.

**Act 3** - Climax[^3]
- Caroline moves to London. Needs to unpack and establish her territory.
- Scout's depression, more clear. Thought Shelby would keep her busy, but Scout teeters on the line of neglect.
- **Six months later:** Scout is healthy, ahead of the pack.
	- Forced to move to London.
- Caroline is visiting Bury St Edmunds. Passes by Scout's house. She's no longer there.
- **Two weeks later:** Scout meets Mack, her cousin who lives in London.
- **That night:** They go to karaoke. Mack introduces Scout to his friends. Among them is Caroline (uni friends).
- Scout and Caroline catch up. Establish Mack likes Caroline.
- Caroline invites Scout to her flat. Scout refuses - needs to check on Shelby. Scout invites Caroline to hers instead.
	- They chat at Scout's, but the night ends after conversation.
- **Next day:** Caroline dreams of Scout. Wakes up next to another stranger. Caroline's routine still continues.
- Scout and Caroline grab tea. They *really* get to know each other.
- They go to the park. Digging more into each other.
- Establish Caroline's new week-end project: workaway on a farm.
- They kiss.
- *maybe establish a month without each other?* Scout: I've got some things to reckon with.

	- Scout boutta suicide. Depression left lingering.

[^2]: This act builds tension and suspense as the protagonist encounters obstacles and complications, leading towards a point of no return. It's where the stakes are raised and the audience becomes more invested in the outcome.
[^3]: The climax is the turning point of the story, the moment of highest tension and emotional intensity. It's where the protagonist confronts the central conflict, and the audience anticipates the resolution.
[^4]: Following the climax, this act shows the consequences of the protagonist's actions and how the conflict begins to resolve itself. It provides a sense of relief and release after the intense climax.
[^5]: The final act provides closure, tying up loose ends and bringing the story to a satisfying conclusion. It may also offer a glimpse into the future or the long-term impact of the events.
