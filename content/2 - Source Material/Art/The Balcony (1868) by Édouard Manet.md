# Thoughts
I thought it'd be nice to start a series on dissecting some art pieces, starting with the ones I've noticed that make book covers! This oil painting (*Le Balcon* in its original French) is by Édouard Manet, a famous Parisian French painter known bridging the gap between the Realism and Impressionism movements. He captured modern, urban life while challenging traditional artistic conventions of his time.

What makes this painting so unconventional was how Manet's subjects were depicted. At the time, it was mostly the bourgeois lifestyle that was depicted in paintings such as this. The colors Manet uses, specifically the greens for the balustrade and shutters and the blue of the tie in comparison to the whites on the women's dresses and the general darkness in the background, were perceived by the art world as being "provocative." 

It was hard to find any reasoning behind using *Le Balcon* (1868) online as the cover art for the specific edition I was reading for *Pride and Prejudice* (1813), so much so that Google AI says "extensive searches for Pride and Prejudice covers and editions do not reveal any instances where Manet's 'The Balcony' was used." Here is my evidence that it was used: 
![[Pride and Prejudice cover.png]]

So I guess that means I'll have to draw my own conclusions here. Generally, book covers reflect the themes, tone, and setting of the book they represent (not all the time, but that's for a whole other topic). I can only guess the publishers used this cover because of its bourgeois-ness and the unconventional realism that is also portrayed in Jane Austen's Pride and Prejudice. I can also assume that the publishers thought "hey, these two girls could be Jane and Elizabeth while the man behind them can be Mr. Darcy!" That's what I see, anyway. I reckon the girl looking at the reader/viewer is Elizabeth Bennet and the other is Jane.
