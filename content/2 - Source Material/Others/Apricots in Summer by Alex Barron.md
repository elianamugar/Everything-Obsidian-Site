# Thoughts
- Don't focus on formatting/economy/writer's style of descriptions on first draft.
	- Pay attention to effective use of action lines/description
- Separate day description from action (for dramatic effect).
- You can add description of titles if you have a specific vision for it.
- Some people like to still use DISSOLVE TO: or CLOSE UP: or CUT TO: and other camera directions. It should be ok.
- Economical examples - descriptions/action lines
	- "A foot shakes. Nervous."
	- "Unkempt hair. His clothes haven't been washed in days."
	- "Short, dyed hair, secondhand clothes."
	- "Josh takes some handcuffs. Approaches the fifteen-year-old. Cuffs him."
	- "**FANTASY:** Christian and Josh, on his dorm bed, making out."
	- "<u>Now we realize he's been in here for days.</u>"
	- "Slo motion. The staging, shot choice, and editing should reflect the Physics Olympics scene from earlier."
- Examples of writer's style
	- "A postcard of suburbia."
	- "Christian stands up, heads to the door, goes to open it--
	  --but it's locked."
	- "In a flash, Christian tries to BOLT out of the door--
	  --but Wyatt OVERPOWERS IM. FORCES HIM BACK."
		- compelling use of ALL-CAPS
	- CHRISTIAN: I think it was a defensive response. Like clinging to a life preserver when you're drowning.
	- "A postcard of middle class heterosexuality. A gas stove, a dinner table, two twin beds."
	- "The look back up. The infinite night sky stretches above them."
- Don't be afraid to separate action lines.
  
	**CLOSE ON:** A bookshelf filled with science books: *Schrodinger's Cat, Euclid's Elements, On the Origin of Species*. 
	
	Next to the bookshelf, we find Christian lying asleep in bed.
	
	The sound of the DOOR OPENING. The light flicks on. Then FOOTSTEPS.
	
	A hand comes into frame, gently shaking Christian awake.
- **obdurate** (*adj.*) – stubbornly refusing to change one's opinion or course of action.
	- used as a parenthetical
- Important to make the reading experience of the script AS EQUALLY IMPORTANT as how it'll come across on-screen.
	- "This is too much. Way too much." - description/action line
	- "You can tell he hasn't slept all night."
- There exists a secondary conflict
	1) How to deal with being in a conversion camp (how to escape?).
	2) Should Christian leverage his privilege to be in a better position?
1) Great pacing and intensity. Fantastic economy of lines to still get the emotion across.
