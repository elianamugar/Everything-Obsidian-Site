# Notes
- How do societies behave? Why do we do what we do? What motivates us? What drives us?
- Theories:
	- **Religion:** We're all driven in a war between good and evil.
	- **Biology:** We want to pass on our genes forward. Struggle between men and women.
	- **Race + Culture:** The world is a struggle between different races and cultures for dominance.
	- **Economics:** Driven by money or self-interest.
	- **Liberalism:** Comes from the Enlightenment. History is a progress towards Enlightenment, rationality, truth, and justice.
- **Game Theory**
	- Jiang's argument: It's the best way to understand how humans behave. What drives nations, what drives societies, what drives humans.
	- What is it? 3 components to a game:
		- Players/participants
		- Rules/constraints/boundary conditions (mathematic)
		- Incentives - how do you win this game, what do you get from this game
	- Argument: If you study all 3 components, then you understand how the game works and, therefore, you can predict how the game will turn out.
	- Benefits:
		- You become a better person. The point of education is not to get good grades/get into university/get a good job. It's to make you a better person, a more thoughtful person who can better analyze yourself and the world around you, which allows you to make better decisions and makes you more curious/moral/imaginative.
		- You'll be able to understand the world we live in. This world is stupid (e.g. American involvement in Venezuela 2026). We're trying to figure out *why* this happened and what this will lead to. Requires you to follow the news. This is relevant to your lives. Will look at conflicts between Ukraine-Russia, Israel-Iran, South America, China-Japan, US-China.
		- Once you understand the game, you will have predictive powers. You will be able to understand how the world will develop. You'll be able to analyze your own life and understand how *you* would develop. That means you control sovereignty over your own destiny.
	- This is a lifelong struggle to learn game theory. Prof. Jiang will show us how to look at the world through game theory/how to think critically about the world.
- Game Example: 5 boys and 5 girls want to get married.
	- Players: 5 males, 5 females
		- Ranked according to attractiveness (3 criteria)
			- Genes
			- Wealth
			- Status
