# Thoughts
- Hmm, maybe I'll do an essay comparing and contrasting Sally Rooney's depiction of romance (Normal People) and Celine Song's *Materialists*. I believe Song goes for the idealistic portrayal of romance, while Rooney goes for the realistic (and slightly pessimistic, maybe) portrayal of romance.
- Impressed with Song's steadfast thesis. She's headstrong about it and it shows in this film. That being said... I'm not loving the lack of subtext in this.
- Dakota Johnson's slightly robotic opening (sob)
- My face visibly cringed on DJ's line: "I don't want to hate you because you're poor. But right now I do and it makes me hate myself."
- They were together for five years??!??!?!?!?
- I dunno how to explain it, but I think this film needed some sort of *Wolf of Wall Street* -esque scene (sell me this pen).
- Awesome soundtrack. I know Harry Nilsson when I hear it.
- I also know John Magaro when I hear him LOL.
- When Sophie called DJ a pimp, that was kinda awesome.
- Celine Song tried to put every capitalist zinger she could muster into the dialogue.
- The Sophie L. and Mark P. storyline feels oddly out of place. It doesn't mesh into the story very well, but I can't really pinpoint why.
- The ending of this third act is... something. Something that's not working for me... maybe it's the dialogue.
- Maybe it's also the pacing or structure that wasn't working for me? Something is off by the time the credits roll and I can't figure out what exactly it is.
