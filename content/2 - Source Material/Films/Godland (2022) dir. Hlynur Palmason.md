# Thoughts
- I do love the idea of opening on a black screen with text, then jumping into the character's adventure.
	- Exposition scene -> text over black -> character's adventure begins
- A mesmerizing soundscape *and* landscape.
- The dogs are so cute.
- The first symbolic moment of this film: an Icelander (Ragnar) tries to teach the priest (Lucas) how to ride the horse. Lucas is not great with the horse. Ragnar shows him again. Then, the Ragnar says *Don't ruin a perfectly good horse.* Lucas is able to ride the horse.
	- I think the horse is a perfect symbol for this story's themes of colonialism. It's a naturally wild animal who becomes tamed and domesticated by humans.
- I like watching the process of wet plate photography unfold.
- When the Icelanders talk while Lucas and his interpreter go to the falls, Ragnar tells of a story of a man's nightmare of a woman being raped by multiple men and the man watching. Something about dirty eels in the woman or getting out of the woman? The man wakes up from the nightmare and finds all the dirty eels he can find and kills them all. Symbolic representation of colonialism and revenge?
- The interpreter falls off his horse while crossing the river. He dies.
	- I realize that there was never a name given to the Translator. He remains nameless. Reminds me of the R.F. Kuang quote along the lines of *Every act of translation is a small act of betrayal.* 
- What a compelling way to show the justification of religion in the act of colonialism.
- Now, Lucas is alone with the Icelanders without a Translator. He will have to either adapt and survive, or... not?
- There's a pretty powerful sequence of events that occurs when Lucas and Ragnar climb up a steep mountainside/hill. 
	- Lucas charges on ahead, not being wary of the horse he's trying to drag up. The horse complains. Ragnar tries to help, telling Lucas to get down. Lucas, in defeat says *I can't do this anymore, Ragnar*. Ragnar responds, *What are you saying?* Lucas rests upon the rock in defeat, then looks away to...
	- Cuts to the Translator (who is already dead) looking over a mossy landscape. It's as if he was looking at Lucas from the previous scene, then turns to face the landscape. The Translator powerfully and loudly sings *Where do you come from, waterfall? Which way do you travel home? Will you come to us? Or will you travel with them?* It echoes. ![[Godland 1.png]]
	- While the Translator finishes singing, it cuts to the dead Translator buried underneath the sand and the waves. ![[Godland 2.png]]
- ~44m00s: Lucas finishes taking a picture of some people in the icy part of Iceland. He mutters *Your dog moved less than you*. Lucas goes to get the photo developed, but his whole portable station falls apart. He blames Satan for ruining the photo. ![[Godland 3.png]]
- Lucas looks to be in *horrible* condition.
- Dare I say my favorite scene is when Lucas, at night, says a little prayer about how he's a victim and *he* needs help, even though he's committing this act of colonialism and hating the people he's supposed to love under God's tenets. And to top it all off, he ends the prayer with *I am God*!
- Lucas finds solace in a familiar horse.
- Lucas falls off his horse. Ragnar: *Where are you going?* 
	- It amazes me how the natives still treat Lucas with kindness, despite Lucas's despise towards them.
- Once Lucas makes his way to Iceland, he is forced to reckon with both mother and human nature.
- I love that juxtaposing long shot of nature, then pans to Lucas resting in the grass, then the fiery explosion of a volcano spewing molten lava.
- MIDPOINT REACHED? Start of a new act? Opens on a woman and a girl on the coast. The mother speaks Danish and little Icelandic (prefers Danish). The girl is half Icelandic, half Danish.
