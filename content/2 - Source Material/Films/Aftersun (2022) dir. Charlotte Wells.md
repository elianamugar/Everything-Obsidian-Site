# Thoughts
Watching this to study depiction of depression in film (re: male/father depression). Wells has two distinct ways to show Calum's depression: through the daughter's eyes, through Wells' own imagination which the viewer gets to see. The daughter's vision of her father's depression is through the rave scenes with strobe lights that reveals her father at the same age on the vacation. What the audience gets to see in Calum's depressive moments is what younger Sophie doesn't get to see, which is also Wells' imagined depiction of her father's depression, which is *also* what older Sophie tries to imagine while rewatching the DV footage (filling in the missing pieces, if you will).

At the start of the film, Calum already has a cast on his arm. How did he get that? The answer is purposely ambiguous and left to interpretation, but could be represented as a broken man trying to keep himself together. Theoretically he could've self-harmed or maybe he was too drunk (depression coping mechanism) and hurt himself that way.

Calum puts Sophie to sleep and we're left with a lingering long shot of Calum going outside, smoking, and swaying to his own rhythm. He stares out to the ocean, whilst swaying (mimicking the dancing in the rave scenes maybe, but more similar to Tai-Chi movement).

Sophie calls her mom in the phone booth, and her mom asks her (implicitly) how Calum is doing. Sophie responds innocently, saying he's good, but maybe the mom knows more deeper about Calum's depression hence why she asks Sophie how Calum's doing. Sophie hands the phone to Calum and we hear what Calum says to the mom.
>Calum: Hey. Checking up on me?... That's wonderful. Yeah. I'm so happy for you. All right, love you. Yeah, yeah, yeah. Will do. Okay. Bye.

I think these lines required some direction from Wells, but it's ambiguous what's said on the other side and how Calum feels. The first time I watched it, I thought that Calum and his ex have a healthy relationship and they're still friends and he's genuinely happy. There's another interpretation where maybe he is more depressed upon hearing the news, but masking it.

In the Turkish baths, Calum is seen resting with his eyes closed (Sophie also sees this). It's ambiguous whether he's genuinely resting or depressively tired or meditating.

Calum rests next to Sophie on the beach (Sophie finished her whole spiel about how even if they're not in the same place, they're still under the same sky and the same sun). Sophie points up to the parachute and wants to do it. Calum says she's too young. As they both look up to the parachute, Calum looks away (Sophie doesn't see this), and wipes a tear from his eyes (that's how I interpreted the movement anyway).

Calum breaks his own cast. While doing so, he accidentally cuts himself but doesn't tell Sophie (he masks the pain to keep up the appearance for her). There's a lovely shot in this scene:
![[Aftersun 1.png]]

Calum generally acts like he's running out of time with Sophie and trying to make sure she's equipped for what life will throw at her. It could be interpreted that he's already planning to leave her, or he's concerned with how Sophie will fare *if* he does just disappear (a sort of *just in case of emergency* type scenario). He also has financial burdens, but does not try to show Sophie. He probably can't help but think to himself *Am I a failure?* and *What more of life do I have to live, really?*

>**Calum:** And do you miss it?

Calum crosses the street without caring about almost getting hit by a bus.

Sophie and Calum end the day in the hotel room. Calum brushes his teeth while Sophie lays on the bed.
>C: You okay through there?
>S: I don't know. I guess. I just feel a bit down or something.
>C: What do you mean? 
>S: I don't know. Don't you ever feel like... you've just done a whole amazing day, and then you come home and feel tired and down, and it feels like your bones don't work? They're just tired and everything is tired. Like you're sinking. I don't know. It's weird.
>C: Well, we're here to have a good time, eh? Ready?

After listening to Sophie, Calum stares at the sink (we see through a mirror, and Sophie doesn't see this). He's probably thinking 1) I know the exact feeling she's describing, 2) I don't know what advice to give because I feel the exact way, and 3) Oh God, did I pass it onto her? Then he spits at his reflection in the mirror.

Calum does Tai-Chi in the hotel room. Scattered in the room are self-help pop books and a guide to meditation (taken from screenplay). 

Calum sits in the carpet store alone (Sophie doesn't see this scene), where he previously was with Sophie and he implicitly couldn't afford the carpet he was fixated with. He stares at the carpet he loves, but can't afford. The shopkeeper gives Calum a receipt, indicating that he bought the carpet anyway (indicative of impulse buying to feel a dopamine kick in depressive moods). Calum lays on his new carpet.

The DV footage that plays at the beginning now plays out in this scene, realtime. Sophie asks to interview Calum, saying she's just turned 11 and he's jokingly turning 131 in two days. 
>S: When you were 11, what did you think you would be doing now?
>*Calum stares at her, then tries to hide from the camera.*
>S: Hello? What did you think you would be doing now?
>C: Come on, Soph. Turn that off now, okay?

Sophie lies about turning off the camera, so Calum turns it off. Through the reflection of the TV and the hotel room's mirror, we see Calum trying to keep it together and not cry (Sophie doesn't see this or notice maybe? I think she's looking at him, but she's not reading that he's trying to keep it together). 
>S: Right. Ready? What did you do for your 11th birthday?
>*Calum stares off.*
>C: When I was 11, nobody remembered it was my birthday. And when I told my mom, she was so angry, she grabbed me by the ear, and made my dad drive me to the toy shop, and made me pick something to buy.
>S: That's a bit, um, deep.
>C: It's okay.
>*Calum stares out the window, making sure Sophie can't see his face.*
>S: What did you pick?
>*Calum wipes his eyes.*
>C: (chuckles) I chose a toy phone. It was red.
>S: Good choice.
>C: Thank you.

Calum puts Sophie to bed.
>S: Think you'll ever move back to Scotland?
>C: No.
>S: Why?
>C: There's not enough sun.
>S: Very funny.
>C: Hmm, it's all in the past for me, that's all. And there's this feeling, once you leave where you're from, like, where you grew up, that, um, you don't totally belong there again. Not really. But Edinburgh was never... I never felt like I really did belong there.
>S: I do. It's home.
>C: That's good. I'm glad you feel that way. But you never know where you'll end up, though, eh? You can live wherever you want to live. Hmm. Be whoever you want to be. You have time.

Calum stands on the edge of the balcony, arms out.
![[Aftersun 2.png]]He looks back to see if Sophie can see him. We don't see Sophie, but instead we see a shot of the parachutes flying.

Now that I think about it, since Calum is turning 30 on this trip, he must really feel like a failure. I think there's a certain emotion and existential crisis one goes through when being on the cusp of 30. 29 is a big turning point for most adults, where they reassess their lives and get past the experimental and difficult navigation of the 20s. They take on a certain seriousness and levity with their lives, because that's what they believe (I don't necessarily agree, but I have heard from interviews that this is how people turning 30 feel). All this mixed with Sophie *knowing* about Calum's financial troubles must add up to his depression. Knowing that he's even failed at hiding his troubles and failures from Sophie.

Calum refuses to sing karaoke with Sophie and continues drinking his beer, telling Sophie he's not ready/prepared (probably emotionally). At the end of the karaoke, Calum offers to pay for singing lessons and Sophie says "Don't do that... offering to pay for something you can't afford." Calum ends the night early, but Sophie says she's going to stay out and go back to the room later.

Calum watches the DV footage where Sophie says, "Ladies and gentlemen, the marvelous, wonderful, amazing, one-armed, Calum Aaron Patterson...." Is he trying to see himself through Sophie's eyes? Is he trying to see if she thinks he's a superhero? Maybe he's trying to see if she can see through his cracks, his mask, and his failures. Can she see his depression? He closes the camera, then goes out. He picks up a burnt cigarette off the ground and smokes it. Calum walks into the sea of darkness (the first time I watched this, I genuinely thought he just died right here). We linger on the shot of the sea. Sophie finally gets into their hotel room after being locked out and Calum is there, sleeping naked on the bed. Sophie covers him with a blanket.

The rave scene that's intercut after this is different in terms of soundscape. We hear Calum's erratic breathing and sirens wailing.

Calum sleeps in the bus (is he tired with depression?). They get off the bus for a stop. They do Tai-Chi over the Turkish hills.

The tragedy of Calum is we see him try and get himself better, especially for Sophie's sake, but that's why depression is such a silent killer. Did he see that Sophie can live just fine without him?

Calum has an injury on his shoulder. Again, he doesn't know how he got it and the audience is unsure whether he got it during that sea scene or if it was there before the trip. 

The theme of water in this is apparent. The sea. The murky waters of the mud bath.

Sophie and Calum float alone in the middle of the sea on a little platform.
>C: You know, I want you to know that you can talk to me about anything. As you get older, you know. Whatever parties you go to, boys you meet, drugs you take. 
>S: Dad!
>C: No, I'm serious, Soph. I've done it all and you can too. I just want you to promise me that you'll talk to me about it, okay?
>S: Okay. But I'm never gonna do any of that, anyway.
>C: Well, that's okay, too. But if you do, remember, okay?

![[Aftersun 4.png]]

Two main points that makes this conversation so tragic: 1) Calum does not live. He implicitly commits suicide, so they will never have these conversations that Calum mentions while Sophie grows up. 2) Calum seems to project through Sophie that she can do anything because it's something he wished he'd heard when he was younger. He already suspects that Sophie also has early signs of depression (re: hotel room conversation) so he wants her to be able to survive. And, at this point, if he can survive, then certainly so can she.

Everyone's favorite shot comes up after this! Calum climbs to the top of the steps of an ancient theater while Sophie asks all the other guests at the bottom with her to sing happy birthday to him. Calum looks down, watching this all unfold from the top of the steps. This crossfades/dissolve into Calum sobbing on the hotel room bed.

On the last night, Calum goes to the dance floor (mimicking the "dancing" from earlier on the trip, but also more poignantly the dancing from the rave scenes). Sophie doesn't want to dance, but Calum loves dancing. This is intercut with the strobing rave scenes. Finally, Calum drags Sophie to dance with him. In the strobe rave scenes, we see older Sophie yelling at forever young Calum, but also trying to hug him (but it also looks like a fight?). Older Sophie and forever young Calum continue to dance, hug, and fight all at the same time. Oh god, even watching this frame by frame I'm still hit by its sadness. I think this is aided by the song playing throughout it ([*Oliver Coates' Version of Under Pressure*, originally by David Bowie and Freddie Mercury](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KGy2xrY-x7I&ab_channel=olivercoates)). 
- "I really like how Bowie never gets the chance to finish the lyric. It results in a lack of closure, similar to how Sophie never quite found closure with her father after this being the last time they saw each other. She could never understand what happened after that trip, why she never saw him again, or the pressures and suffering he was going through at the time since she was too young to understand. A really nuanced and effective detail"
- "In an interview, the director, Charlotte Wells revealed that she actually hand drew a map of sorts showing during which points of the song, she wanted an emphasis on the strings or the vocals. What results is a beautiful push and pull not only of the vocals, but also of the father daughter relationship! One of my favourite ending of any film!"
- "'the give love, give love' and the 'this is our last dance' part always wrecks me"

Through DV footage, Calum drops Sophie off at the airport. Calum, through the DV, watches Sophie leave. We move from the TV screen to older Sophie in her apartment, watching the old DV footage. Then we move into the imagined reality, watching Calum put down the DV recorder and walking through double doors into the darkness of the strobing rave.

In the Letterboxd synopsis, it describes Calum as a father with "private melancholy" and I couldn't have put it any better myself.
