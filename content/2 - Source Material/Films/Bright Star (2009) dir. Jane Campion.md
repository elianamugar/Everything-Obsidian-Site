# Thoughts
- It's not until the end credits roll do I realize Keats (Ben Whishaw) is reciting his *Ode to Nightingale* poem. Upon listening to it after his death, it's a pre-written death anthem for himself, or more likely generally his feeling on mortality and relating it to that of his observations of a nightingale. It's quite powerful, the reading of the poem. It emotionally hits even harder when you realize in the film, it was this poem he was working on throughout his time with Fanny. He was telling it to his friend Charles Brown, reciting what he has been working on after a burst of inspiration. *That* is the beauty of poetry – for a brief moment to be recorded into *poeisis* and for it to mean something more than life itself (death in this case). And even more, his lines have lived on past him.
