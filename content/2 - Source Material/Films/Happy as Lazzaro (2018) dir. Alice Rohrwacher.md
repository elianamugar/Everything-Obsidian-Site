# Thoughts
The character of Lazzaro is innocent. He's very happy to do whatever he's told in order to help. There are some interesting themes of exploitation, slavery, and innocence/naivety in this film. All of this can be used to inform the character of Scout.

Lazzaro manages to escape the rural (slightly prison-esque) countryside of Inviolata into the city where he first runs into some radio towers/energy powerplant (his first sight of modern technology beyond Tancredi's Walkman). In this place, lots of migrants are competing to work (selling themselves at the lowest price). He moves onto the next place in search of... Tancredi I think?

Turns out Lazzaro has remained the same age and everyone from Inviolata that left have all ended up in the city. Everyone from Inviolata thought he died and had been eaten by wildlife. A report is read that the marquis of Inviolata got charged for enslavement of the people (they were doing free labor and thought they were constantly in debt).

Lazzaro's knowledge comes in handy at the encampment he lives in with the previous workers of Inviolata – there are edible plants and vegetables there which he points out to his fellow encampment dwellers.
