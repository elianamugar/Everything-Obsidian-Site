# Thoughts
I watched this to get inspiration for [[Lost & Found]] in regards to likeable love triangles (still having a hard time finding this!!). Overall the movie is kind of messy, but it's iconic so I felt a 3.5/5 rating was fair enough. I guess there were three stories that involved a love triangle? But I'm going to focus on the one between Alan Rickman, Emma Thompson, and the secretary and the other one between Keira Knightley, Chiwetel Ejiofor, and Andrew Lincoln. Unfortunately the movie was covering so much that these love triangles were quite shallowly explored. The Rickman-Thompson-secretary affair had no definitive ending, and it just kind of *fizzled*. That's the only way I can describe it. Emma Thompson's emotions throughout it, though, is what will stick with me and I want that kind of energy poured into *Lost & Found*. The secretary just wasn't likable enough for this love triangle to be taken into consideration for the screenplay. Now, the Knightley-Ejiofor-Lincoln love triangle was more of the dynamic I was looking for, but unfortunately it also suffered to being shallowly explored. Both love interests for Knightley's character were charming (in fact they're best friends!) and likable! Unfortunately Knightley and Ejiofor do get married already, so it's already a done deed for Lincoln – a short-lived tale. I'm trying to explore a *what if* scenario if the crux of the love triangle (e.g. Knightley) is only *engaged* and *about to be* married to her fiancé (e.g. Ejiofor) AND there's another possible suitor who happens to be close to the fiancé (e.g. Lincoln). The time crunch! I think the film that will get me closest to this will be Celine Song's upcoming *Materialists (2025)* because in an interview, Song explicitly said she didn't want either of the male love interests to be unlikable – in fact, she wanted them to be equally charming and vulnerable and on an equal playing field (except in socio-economic statuses). This is what I want to explore as well!
