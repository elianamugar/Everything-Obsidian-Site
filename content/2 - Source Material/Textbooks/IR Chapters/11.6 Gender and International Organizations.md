#### 11.6.0 Gender and International Organizations
- int'l orgs and NGOs under scrutiny
- Sandra [[Whitworth]]
	- how int'l orgs are part of complex political and social processes that aid in the construction of assumptions about proper roles of women and men in workforce
		- early case studies: 
			- Int'l Planned Parenthood Federation (IPPF)
			- Int'l Labour Organization (ILO)
		- how such orgs over time understood and organized their programs around shifting views of gender
		- how these understandings came to exist
		- how activists managed to influence their construction and redefinition
	- underlying assumption: such institutions are a reflection of the interests, norms, ideas of hegemonic groups
	- [[Men, Militarism, and UN Peacekeeping by Sandra Whitworth]]
		- gendered understanding to national participation int'l orgs
		- aware of positive global image of UN peacekeepers and Canadian forces
		- widespread faith and belief in the necessity of peacekeeping reinforced [[post-Cold War]], dramatic expansion of peacekeeping missions
			- number and scope increased
			- mandates beyond military and peacekeeping responsibilities
				- monitoring human rights, conducting elections, delivering aid, helping to repatriate refugees, rebuilding state bureaucracies
		- who benefits from peacekeeping operations?
			- who is excluded?
			- what is the effect of peacekeeping operations on the local people?
			- problematizing the impact of peacekeeping ops
				- challenged blanket assertions that UN peacekeepers were selfless, benign soldiers
				- examines militarized masculinity from perspective of peacekeeping missions
		- states and UN tend to dismiss any information that contradicts image of UN peacekeepers as altruistic and benign
			- views negative reports as isolated examples
			- over time, UN and nat'l gov'ts charged with sexual [[exploitation]] and physical violence against those who are supposed to be protected
	- while attention to gender has made concerns more visible within UN, such critiques have had minimal impact on actual UN way of doing business
		- incorporating language of gender in official UN policy ironically had the effect of silencing criticism by ensuring that broader issues (militarized masculinity) don't end up on formal UN agendas
- empirical neglect of women and gender relations
	- growth of transnational women's networks
		- worked with sympathetic actors in states and int'l org bureaucracies to effect policy changes
	- 1990: [[Amnesty International]] added gender to its list of forms of political persecution
		- [[Yugoslavia]] civil war and political pressure
		- rape now considered a war crime under [[Geneva Conventions]] Against War Crimes
			- can be prosecuted by the [[ICC]] (International Criminal Court)
