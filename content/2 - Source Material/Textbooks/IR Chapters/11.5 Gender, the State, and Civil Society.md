#### 11.5.0 Gender, the State, and Civil Society
- J. Ann [[Tickner]]
	- misrepresentations of feminism and the feminist standpoint
		- feminist understandings about how and why gender matters to IR theorizing and global challenges women face
	- IR feminists want to rid the field of "idealistic associations of women with peace, [[Idealism]], and impracticality"
		- "disempowers women and keeps them in their place out of the 'real world' of int'l politics"
		- "women and men to participate in reducing damaging and unequal hierarchical structures (gender and race)"
			- constructed gender hierachies
				- results in devaluation of women's lives, their economic and social contributions to society
				- contributes to conflict, inequality, oppression
- Iris [[Marion Young]]
	- gender matters to the [[security]] state, which directly reflects masculinist understandings
		- "protection" role assumed by males, "loving self-sacrifice" subordinates "those in the protected position"
	- the security state, two faces
		1. facing outward to defend against enemies
		2. facing inward to keep those under protection under necessary control
		- "no state can make any of us completely safe"
	- ideal of "democratic citizenship"
		- [[Marion Young]] rejects "hierarchy of protector and protected"
			- prefers "defender" role that women and men customarily perform
		- on global level, avoids [[authoritarian]], masculinist orientation of the security state
			- in favor of "respect and political equality" among everyone globally
- [[War, Women and Power by Marie Berry]]
	- [[Marie Berry]] compares [[Rwanda]] and [[Bosnia]]
		- "perpetrators employed rape as a deliberate strategy of violence with ethnic, class and gender dimensions"
		- [[Rwanda]]
			- aim to emasculate and humiliate [[Tutsi]] men by abusing their women, considered their property in patrilineal society
		- [[Bosnia]]
			- physical and psychological effects on rape victims
				- faced stigma from their communities
			- rape was "a political act designed to carry out an officially orchestrated policy"
	- displacement of persons and consequent fragmentation of social networks
		- "destruction of previous patterns of life"
			- opened doors for women in post-conflict years, displaced women creating new social networks
		- "war can serve as a period of rapid social change that reconfigures gendered power relations by precipitating interrelated demographic, economic, and cultural shifts"
- Hilary [[Matfess]]
	- women are victims and important agents in their own right
	- study of [[Boko Haram]]'s insurgency in [[Nigeria]]
		- "women throughout the region have organized and advanced their own interests"
			- victimization in sexual violence and other forms against them in masculinist space
		- not all women were abducted and coerced into Boko Haram
			- some joined willingly and found ways to exercise their agency
- Erica [[Chenoweth]] and Maria [[J. Stephan]]
	- explore nonviolent civil resistance
		- far more effective than armed resistance in overthrowing regimes and achieving other social objectives
		- civil resistance as "a form of collective action that seeks to affect the political, social, or economic status quo without using violence or the threat of violence against people to do so"
			- strikes, protests, demonstrations, boycotts, alternative institution building
		- [[Gandhi]] as a prime example, developed concept of civil resistance against [[UK]] [[Colonialism]] in [[India]]
