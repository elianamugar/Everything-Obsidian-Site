#### 8.3.0 The Divergence of British and American Scholarship
- how [[The English School]] emerges as a distinct, societal-based, rationalist image
	- divergence [[post-WWI]] between [[UK]] and [[America]] scholarships on politics (general and int'l)
	- [[UK]]
		- wedded to strengths in classical understandings and more traditional methods of analysis
		- didn't embrace [[behavioralism]] movement that was taking over American [[political science]] in 1950s – 1960s
	- classical scholarship
		- influenced by historically based understandings
			- [[Philosophy]], [[Law]], institutional studies
		- challenged by [[behavioralism]] movement that emerged in American social sciences by 1930s
		- gained steam [[Post-WWII]] and established a prominent position in IR by 1960s, when [[Realism]] occupied center stage
- debate in the interwar period (1919 – 1939)
	- [[Realism]] preferred to focus on realities of power and interest
	- others wished to change that by turning to greater reliance on int'l law and institutions as substitute for reality of [[Realpolitik]] (power politics)
	- [[behavioralism]] movement in [[America]] to make social sciences more scientific rises
- late 1960s: [[Hedley Bull]]'s critique of American scholarship's direction
	- argues for "classical approach" vs. scientific approach, claiming scientific approach is "positively harmful"
	- calls for continued reliance on judgment
	- skeptical about scientific claims made by Americans wedded to logical/mathematical proofs or verified by strict procedures
	- rejects "models" by the "scientific school"
		- "a connection with reality it does not have"
		- "distorting the model itself by importing additional assumptions about the world in the guise of logical axioms"
		- "distorted and impoverished by a fetish for measurement"
	- warns against dogmatism that can be found among modelers
	- calls for rigor and precision in theory work
		- accommodated within classical approach
		- be a coherent, precise, orderly body of knowledge
		- be consistent with philosophical foundations of science
	- disliked arrogance of practitioners of scientific approach
		- "by cutting themselves off from history and philosophy, have deprived themselves of the means of self-criticism"
	- observes consequences: callow and brash view of their subject
- [[Hedley Bull]]'s critiques shared with [[The English School]]
	- pointed directly at American scholars
		- reflective of divergence between [[UK]] and [[America]] methodological and epistemological approaches in [[IR]]
- [[America]]: [[Positivism]], quantitative approaches
	- earlier convergence in Anglo-American approaches around history, law, and institutional studies
- [[UK]]: traditional, societal focus
	- open to historical [[Sociology]] and [[constructivism]] understandings
