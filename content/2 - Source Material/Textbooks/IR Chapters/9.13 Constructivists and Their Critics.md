#### 9.13.1 Liberal and Realist Critiques
- early constructivist work critiquing [[Epistemology]] and [[Ontology]] of [[neorealism]] and [[neoliberalism]]
	- [[neoliberal institutionalism]] response
		- [[Keohane]]: a research program is lacking and until then, it can be shown via empirical studies that can "illuminate important issues in world politics, they will remain on the margins of the field, largely invisible to the preponderance of empirical researchers" #quote 
			- reflective approaches/interpretive understandings are good at pointing out limitations of rational actor and game-theoretic approaches to int'l institutions rather than developing theories of their own
	- [[neorealism]] response
		- [[Mearsheimer]] re: [[social constructivism]], [[critical theory]], [[postmodernism]]
			- they have little to say on
				- feasibility of the int'l system evolving into a more peaceful world
				- mechanisms as to how this would occur
				- little empirical support for their assertions
				- inability to explain why particular discourses rise and fall
	- since then, constructivism has studied many empirical case studies
	- clash between interpretive understandings and [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]] will always contrast because of underlying [[Epistemology]] and [[Ontology]] differences
#### 9.13.2 Debates within Constructivism and Postmodern Challenges
- who is a constructivist?
- is constructivism a middle path between causal theory and postmodernism?
	- does it include postmodernism?
		- difference between [[Wendt]] ([[Positivism]] [[Epistemology]]) and Andreas [[Behnke]] ([[postmodernism]])
			- [[Wendt]]: commitment to science as a way to gain knowledge of the world and treats states as actors with intentions
			- [[Behnke]]: Wendt isn't far removed from [[Kenneth Waltz]]'s [[neorealism]]
- what analytic purposes should the state agent be given?
	- should scientific methods be used?
		- [[Positivism]] [[Epistemology]]: a socially constructed int'l system contains patterns that are subject to generalization and falsifiable hypothesis
			- [[Wendt]], [[Martha Finnemore]], [[Michael Barnett]]
		- [[postmodernism]]: is such work really that different from the [[Methodologies]] and [[Epistemology]] of [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]]?
			- role of ethics?
			- skeptical about these distinctions
			- is [[anarchy]] a fundamental organizing principle of the int'l system?
				- does enough authority (legitimated power) exist in int'l orgs, int'l law, and firms and institutions that it's questionable whether the world is really as anarchic as it's usually portrayed?
- [[agent-structure]] debate unresolved
	- how important is agency?
		- whether states or non-state actors (humans) act in their own right
	- how important is structure?
		- external/exogenous factors that facilitate/constrain agents
	- what impact do agents have on these structures?
		- human construction structures or subject to interpretation by humans
	- clash between [[neorealism]] and [[neoliberalism]] –> [[constructivism]]
		- [[postmodernism]] and [[post-structuralism]] join debate
			- [[post-structuralism]]: "what matters is neither structures nor agents, but the role of discursive practices"
				- social "realities" are a function of *linguistic* construction, our understanding of cultural meanings found in language interactions, verbal/non-verbal communications we employ
	- [[Emanuel Adler]]: agent-structure debate will never be fully resolved
		- but we have a better understanding of how agents and structures relate in the theoretical formulations offered by constructivism
- summary of [[constructivism]]
	- leading perspective in IR
	- evolved from critique of realism and liberalism –> research program that's provided important insights into world politics
		- caused other perspectives/approaches to take into account constructivism concepts and arguments
