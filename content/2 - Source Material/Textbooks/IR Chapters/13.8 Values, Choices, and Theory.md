#### 13.8.0 Values, Choices, and Theory
- normative theory in [[The English School]], [[classical realism]], [[constructivism]]
	- divorcing norms from inquiry
		- at a minimum, difficult to achieve
	- traditional debate
		- over the role of normative theory in the four images
			- [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]] –> [[Positivism]]
				- explanatory theory and normative theory occupy separate realms
- normative theory relevant to each of the images to the extent that one finds allowance for the exercise of human will
	- how much can political leaders, heads of int'l orgs, directors of transnational/NGOs affect the course of events?
		- if decision makers are driven internally (psychological theories) or severely constrained by external environment, normative theory plays a reduced role in decision-making
		- if humans have some control over their affairs (IR) and causal theories take this effect of the will into account and exclude [[determinism]] inevitabilities, why has normative part been neglected in IR theory?
- central argument: the image, set of assumptions, and interpretive understandings one holds re: IR affects the sense one makes of "facts" and types of explanations/predictions one offers
	- important to come to understanding of biases/perspectives associated w/any particular image/interpretive understanding of IR and world politics
		- such knowledge is NOT just a function only of prior assumptions, preferences, values
	- when what we see as facts contradicts the image/understanding we hold, it's the image/understanding that should be altered/overturned to accommodate new info
- knowledge of IR is imperfect
	- various biases color our vision
	- world has a way of breaking down preconceptions when they're fallacious
	- scientific skepticism about claims to truth
		- forces testing of various propositions/hypotheses with historical, interpretive, other empirical data
	- scientific knowledge enhances our knowledge of the world
		- painfully slow, but persistent
		- natural/social realms
