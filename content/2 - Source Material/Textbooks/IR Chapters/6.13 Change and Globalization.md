#### 6.13.0 Change and Globalization
- [[Liberalism]]: while a great deal of [[IR]] involves continuity, **change is also a constant**
	- [[USSR]] collapsed
	- [[America]]'s domination of int'l economy eroded
	- radical [[Islam]], the power of [[Religion]] in general, and int'l terrorism wasn't on [[IR]]'s radar 20 years ago
	- [[China]] is rising
	- [[Russia]] is resurgent
- how to explain these shifts in the first place?
	- reference to shifts in global distribution of power
- [[Liberalism]] is most open (and expectant) image discussed in this book of **change**
	- due to emphasis on [[voluntarism]] (on [[determinism]]-[[voluntarism]] spectrum)
- [[Liberalism]]'s emphasis on political economy (literature on [[integration]] and [[interdependence]])
	- deals with impact of globalization?
		- 1970s: rise of [[interdependence]] concept
		- 1990s: rise of [[Globalization]] concept
			- continues to have currency
	- scholars try not to replace one concept with the other
		- [[Globalization]] concerns the way aspects of social life have been reorganized due to velocity and intensity of transnational flows encouraged by *liberalization* of trade and [[deregulation]] of financial markets
			- not simply about linkages between societies and states
			- [[John Ruggie]]: [[Globalization]] is to [[interdependence]] as Federal Express is to the exchange of letters between separate post offices
- [[Liberalism]] perspective on world politics has most affinity with [[Globalization]]
	- except for [[Economic Structuralism]]
	- [[Transnationalism]] and [[interdependence]] are component elements of definitions of [[Globalization]]
	- [[Liberalism]] has been long concerned with meeting human desires for material well-being and the achievement of civil/political rights
	- technological advances helped int'l transportation of goods and ideas
	- [[integration]], [[regime theory]], [[neoliberal institutionalism]] all studied how collaborative efforts ([[IMF]], World Bank) can support global markets and upgrade common interest in other areas (i.e. human rights)
		- requires establishment of legal and institutional arrangements to stabilize markets and assist spread of [[Liberalism]] [[democracy]] worldwide
- 2008: global financial crisis
	1. to what extent does [[Globalization]] threaten [[democratic peace theory]]?
		- [[Realism]]: [[interdependence]] didn't keep major states from launching [[WWI]]
			- since 1970s: flow of trade and finance hanged the operation of world's economy
				- increasingly creates one large market with a single division of labor
			- there are winners and losers, resultant political tensions within and between states
			- could [[Globalization]] worsen political tensions, far from creating harmony among states?
				- in line with [[Realism]]'s expectations
	2. will [[global governance]] continue to expand or retract in an age of contested [[Globalization]], at times expressed in the resurgence of [[nationalism]]
		- to what extent are the norms, institutions, processes designed to enhance int'l collaboration sufficient to manage the challenge of [[Globalization]] and the **collective action problem** it poses for states?
			- [[Liberalism]] concerned over possible answers to these questions
				- awareness that current levels of efficiency and effectiveness of int'l institutions aren't enough to sustain their viability
				- what's lacking: sufficient degrees of legitimacy that are required to sustain any institution over time
