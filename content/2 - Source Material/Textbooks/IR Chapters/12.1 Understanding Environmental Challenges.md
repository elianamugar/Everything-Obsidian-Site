#### 12.1.0 Understanding Environmental Challenges
- [[rationalism]]
	- in [[Realism]], [[Liberalism]], [[The English School]]
		- looks scientifically for causes of undesirable outcomes
		- seeks remedies that will reduce/eliminate adverse effects
- carbon emissions into the atmosphere
	- causally related to 
		- acid rain
		- ozone layer depletion in the upper atmosphere
		- global climate change
	1. exhausts from automobiles, trucks, buses, ships, aircraft, smokestacks, power plants burning fossil fuels and other sources combine with water in the atmosphere to form carbonic and other acids
		- rains on forests and contributes to death of trees
		- greater acidity in lakes and streams
			- affects fish and other wildlife
	2. chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) from spray cans, refrigerants, other sources rise to upper atmosphere and deplete stratospheric ozone layer that protects humans/other wildlife from skin cancer-causing exposure to direct sunlight
	3. CO2, methane, and other "greenhouse" gases trap heat from sun and cause global warming/climate change
- [[J. D. Thompson]]
	- study of organizations
	- uncertainties can make decision-making difficult for practitioners
		- application to global warming
			- increasing certainty about devastating outcomes if we fail to reduce carbon emissions/find productive ways to remove carbon from the atmosphere
				- emissions from
					- industrial smokestacks
					- automobiles and trucks
					- aircraft
					- ships
					- livestock raised for slaughter
					- melting tundra
					- forest and other fires
				- clearing forests removes recycling effect of trees and other plant life that take in and store carbon from the atmosphere and emit oxygen as part of their photosynthesis process
			- uncertainty among atmospheric scientists about relative importance of different causes and effectiveness of diverse remedies (e.g. cutting emissions, massive tree-planting)
				- is developing technologies that directly remove carbon from the atmosphere the answer?
				- to maximize the reduction of overall emissions, where should our emphasis be?
			- uncertainty about possible remedies adds to difficulty in forming global political consensus 
				- many proposed remedies are costly
				- numerous int'l conferences on climate change, but progress has been very slow
				- asymmetry in levels of development among states
					- adds to difficulties of achieving consensus
					- [[Third World]] are less inclined to submit to constraints on carbon emissions
						- global change caused by earlier industrialization by [[First World]]
						- ongoing economic production
						- continuing large amounts of carbon emissions
			- certainty on ozone-layer depletion caused by CFC emissions
				- political consensus in late-1980s on remedies
					- [[Montreal Protocol]]
					- [[Vienna Convention]]
				- aims to eliminate/substantially reduce CFC emissions
			- uncertainty of increased acidification of precipitation
				- carbon emissions blamed for increased amounts of acidification in lower atmosphere
					- causes acid rain
				- replacing fossil fuels –> electricity (renewable sources: solar, wind, geothermal)
					- takes decades to implement
	- decision-making matrix
		- [[12.1 Understanding Environmental Challenges#Figure 12.1 Thompson's Decision-Making Matrix]]
		- sheds light on atmospheric environmental problems analytically on degrees of uncertainty
- primary problem in dealing with many global environmental global challenges
	- need for scientific consensus to achieve a plan of action
	- many countries have made economic development their primary goal
		- countries w/large youth population with few prospects, job creation is a concern
		- owners and managers of capital (OMC) have financial interest in economic growth
	- long-term effects of global environmental degradation recognized
		- challenges of here and now are more immediate and greater concern to policy makers
