#### 11.4.0 Gender, War, and Security Studies
- critical of masculinist approaches to conflict that emphasizes power and [[balance of power]] politics, [[coercive diplomacy]], [[unilateralism]], and use of force
	- strong do what they will and weak do what they must mindset
	- arms races and use of force in warfare are masculinist constructs
	- perpetrators of guerrilla, terrorist, politically motivated violence in IR tend to be men/boys
		- women usually consigned to home front, not battlefront
- [[Elshtain]]'s [[Women and War (1987) by Jean Bethke Elshtain]]
	- examines war from conventional and unconventional perspectives
	- contemporary IR research on [[War]]
	- [[Hegel]]'s Just Warriors/Beautiful Souls dichotomy
		- Western men are seen fit to plan, conduct, narrate wars
		- women viewed as too soft and motherly
	- historical perspectives on war, peace, armed civic virtue all the way to [[Ancient World]] [[Greece]]
	- women absent from historical accounts
		- show how women made sense of war
- [[Joshua Goldstein]]'s [[War and Gender by Joshua Goldstein]]
	- summarize empirical studies ranging all levels of analysis
	- despite political, economic, cultural differences, gender roles in war are consistent across all known human societies
		- historically, when faced with the prospect of war, the response has been in a gender-based way to assemble fighters who were exclusively male
		- war is a diverse, multifaceted enterprise, just as gender norms outside war show similar diversity
			- diversity vanishes in connection between war and gender
	- applies all 3 strands of feminist thought previously outlined
- sexual orientation
	- homophobic prejudices are ignored in an all-male setting
		- institutional culture emphasizing masculine and discounting feminine
	- more recent decades, women allowed to assume combat roles in [[America]]'s military units
	- roles women and men play in society intersect with sexuality
		- military re: LGBTQ+ members serving in armed forces
			- transgenders particularly have been marginalized in society and the LGBTQ+ community (differentiated on sexual orientation, not the genders they identify with)
- concept of [[security]] under scrutiny
	- feminism: "Security for whom?"
		- does it make sense to continue to view the patriarchal state as the mainstay of security?
		- is the security of individuals or groups adequately understood in terms of being members of a given national community?
		- is achieving security to be found in the traditional [[Realism]] conception?
	- "What is meant by security?"
		- is it limited to deterring or preventing an outside power from attacking the state of which one is a citizen?
		- denial of basic human rights, widespread poverty, environmental degradation, gender inequality viewed as human security issues applicable to men, women, children
		- challenging entire discourse of security in line with [[critical theory]], [[postmodernism]], expansive notion of security in [[Liberalism]]
		- struggle over meaning of security and peace in relation to masculinity/femininity
