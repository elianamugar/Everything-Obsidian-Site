#### 5.7.0 Realists and International Cooperation
- [[classical realism]] has more faith than [[structural realism]] in ability of int'l orgs to make a contribution to int'l stability/peace
	- [[Grotius]] influence, similar to [[The English School]]
- starting point: application of [[Game Theory]]
	- assumption of unified, rational state actors
- starting point: systemic distribution of power and implications of [[hegemonic power]]
	- theory of [[hegemonic stability]]
		- dominant power assumes leadership (for entire globe maybe)
			- e.g. 19th and early 20th c.: [[UK]] seen as offering leadership in int'l monetary matters 
				- [[gold standard]] associated with int'l exchange of money was managed by [[Bank of England]]
				- [[Post-WWII]], leadership assumed by [[America]]
		- absence of [[hegemonic power]]/leadership may result in chaos and instability
			- e.g. 1930s: [[America]] unwilling to assume leadership of world economy and [[UK]] (weakened) was unable assume leadership
				- competitive depreciation of currencies
				- emergence of trade barriers
				- drastic reduction in volume of trade
- stability is enhanced by a concentration of power in int'l politics
	- virtue in inequality among states
	- [[hegemonic power]] benefits, but also less powerful states that find a more stable world advantageous
- decline of [[hegemonic power]] and consequent fragmentation of power in int'l politics –> disorder
	- breakdown of previously constructed int'l agreements
	- leadership provided by hegemonic states facilitates achievement of collaboration among states
- 1980s – 2000s: theoretical and empirical controversy mirrored by public debate whether [[America]] was a [[hegemonic power]] in decline
	- [[Paul Kennedy]]
		- historian, sparked debate
		- examines rise and fall of great powers over 500 years
		- debate influenced by discussion among [[structural realism]] on how [[America]] might be able to adapt to [[hegemonic power]] decline and how stability in int'l economic system could be sustained after [[hegemony]]
