#### 7.1.0 The Capitalist World-System
- [[dependency]] –> [[capitalist world-system]] perspective
	- [[Economic Structuralism]]
	- differs from [[dependency]]
		1. [[capitalist world-system]] advocates concerned with lack of [[Third World]] development and wish to understand the economic, political, social development of regions globally
			- developed and underdeveloped states (winners and losers) are all examined to explain the global existence of ***uneven development***
		2. goal: to understand fate of various parts of the world at various times in history within the larger context of a developing world political economy
			- e.g. [[Latin American]] isn't unique
				- its experience is an integral part of the [[capitalist world-system]]
			- [[Third World]] underdevelopment and [[exploitation]] are central to maintaining present structure of dominance in [[capitalist world-system]]
			- priority: to understand this global system from a historical perspective
				- fats of particular societies/regions can be understood
- Immanuel [[Wallerstein]]'s writings
	- [[Economic Structuralism]], catalyst for extensive research
	- attempting to understand the origins and dynamics of modern world-economy and existence of worldwide uneven development
	- aspire to a historically based theory of global development
		- [[world-system theory]]
	- analyzes emergence of [[Capitalism]] in [[Europe]]
		- development into [[capitalist world-system]] that contains a *core*, *periphery*, *semiperiphery*
			- different understanding of [[Globalization]] than [[Liberalism]]
		- *core*: historically engaged in most advanced economic activities
			- banking, manufacturing, technologically advanced agriculture, shipbuilding
		- *periphery*: provided raw materials to fuel *core*'s economic expansion
			- minerals, timber
			- unskilled labor repressed
			- denied advance technology in areas that might make them more competitive with *core* states
		- *semiperiphery*: involved in a mix of production activities, some associated with *core*, some with *periphery*
			- serves as other functions, like an outlet for investment when wages in *core* economies become too high
		- over time, world regions may gravitate between *core*, *periphery*, and *semiperiphery* status
		- class structure varies in each zone, depending on how the dominant class relates to world-economy
			- division of labor requires increases in inequality between regions
				- contrary to [[Liberalism]]'s [[Economics]] notion of specialization based on comparative advantage
			- states in *periphery* are weak
				- unable to control their fates
			- states in *core* are economically, political, militarily dominant
				- basic function of state: to ensure the continuation of [[Capitalism]]'s mode of production
	- [[Wallerstein]]'s debt to [[Marx]] and other [[Economic Structuralism]] scholars
		- topics: cause of [[War]] among states, factors leading to rise and fall of *core* powers
			- discussed in context of creation and expansion of [[Capitalism]] as a historical world-system
			- focus on economic processes and how they influence political and security considerations
#### 7.1.1 System
- [[Wallerstein]] and other [[Economic Structuralism]] scholars
	- to understand the development of global economic, political, social processes, we must pay attention on the development of [[Capitalism]]
		- [[Capitalism]] is a systemwide/global phenomenon
		- examine capitalism as an integrated, historically expanding system that transcends any political/geographic boundaries
			- shouldn't concentrate on individual states/national economies then extrapolate from their experiences
	- understanding [[Capitalism]] as a truly integrated world-system
		- we can better understand the fate of particular countries
		- emphasis on the system is key to understanding
			- similar to [[Realism]]
	- do [[Economic structuralists]] operating from the world-system perspective share the [[Realism]] as to what constitutes the int'l system?
		- parallels in [[Wallerstein]]'s work
			1. [[Realism]] acknowledges [[Wallerstein]] attempts to develop a system-level theory
				- though he emphasizes [[Economics]] over [[Politics]]
			2. [[Wallerstein]] recognizes importance of [[anarchy]], a critical concept to [[Realism]]
				- [[anarchy]]: the absence of a superordinate/central political authority
				- [[Wallerstein]]: "the absence of a single political authority makes it impossible for anyone to legislate the general will of the world-system and hence to curtail the capitalist mode of production"
				- implications of [[anarchy]] for [[Realism]] and [[Economic Structuralism]] differ
					- [[Realism]]: [[anarchy]] leads one to examine int'l political stability, [[War]], [[balance of power]] politics involving major states
					- [[Economic Structuralism]]: economic ramifications of political [[anarchy]] are paramount
						- political [[anarchy]] of the interstate system facilitates the development and expansion of world [[Capitalism]] b/c no single state can control the entire world-economy
						- result: economic *division of labor* involving *core*, *periphery*, *semiperiphery*
							- focal point of [[Economic Structuralism]] analysis
						- political [[anarchy]] becomes backdrop for extensive analysis of capitalist dynamics
			3. [[Wallerstein]] addresses issue of int'l distribution of capabilities/power
				- difference in how [[Realism]] and [[Wallerstein]] uses notion of distribution of capabilities
					- [[Wallerstein]]: the very existence of a distribution of power/capability can't be explained without reference to the underlying economic order
						- argument: [[Realism]] spends a lot of time talking about the [[balance of power]], but fail to appreciate that there's important economic processes at work that are critical for particular distribution of capabilities/[[balance of power]] in the first place
						- focusing on distribution of capabilities is insufficient if one wishes to fully comprehend the nature of the world-system
							- int'l system has always been composed of weak and strong political units
							- differential power alone isn't the defining characteristic of the system
						- the key aspects of the system are its capitalist nature, existence of global class relations, various functions states and societies perform in world-economy
- [[Capitalism]] is the defining attribute of the int'l system since the 16th c.
	- helps account for *core*, *periphery*, *semiperiphery*
	- provides critical environment in which states and classes operate by constraining, shaping, channeling behavior
		- some are states/classes are rewarded, others doomed to play subordinate roles in worldwide division of labor
	- states and politics important to [[Economic Structuralism]]
		- must be analyzed in context of [[capitalist world-system]]
		- material structure matters more than agency
- [[post-Cold War]]: [[Wallerstein]] puts events of 1989 onwards in historical perspective, taking a long multi-century view of global [[Capitalism]]
	- [[Liberalism]] has served as a "legitimating geoculture"
		- North-South relations
			- North's wealth is largely the result of a transfer of surplus value from the South
	- Vulnerability in a [[capitalist world-system]] comes from a ceaseless accumulation of capital that approaches its limit
	- grossly unequal distribution of material gains contributes to multiple strains on the system and undermines state structures
#### 7.1.2 Political, Economic, and Social Factors
- Images recap
	- [[Realism]] understands importance of economic factors, but focuses on power, [[balance of power]], political explanations of [[IR]]
	- [[Liberalism]] ([[Transnationalism]]) emphasize political, economic, social factors, depending on issue
	- [[The English School]] subordinates economic factors to their concern with elaborating the concept of [[international civil society]]
	- [[Economic Structuralism]] stresses economic factors as underlying/driving politics in a [[capitalist world-system]]
- generalizations of images, subject to *qualification*
	- [[Economic Structuralism]] emphasizes economic factors in their conceptions of the world-system, but the degree of their emphasis varies
		- debate as to whether the capitalist mode of production has been overemphasized
			- [[Wallerstein]], claimed by critics to have reduced the derivation and operation of the state system to economics
			- other [[Economic Structuralism]] scholars, stress [[interdependence]] of political and economic variables
				- accept the logic of the [[capitalist world-system]] approach
- Christopher [[Chase-Dunn]]
	- argument: "the capitalist mode of production exhibits a single logic in which both political-military power"
	- exploitative economic processes play an integrated role
	- political processes are derivative of the world-capitalist mode of production
		- they're placed in the context of economic structures and processes
		- "both the attempts and failures of imperium can be understood as responses to the pressures of *uneven development* in the world-economy.... The interstate system is dependent on the institutions and opportunities presented by the world market for its survival"
- Johan [[Galtung]]
	- perspective on [[imperialism]]
	- examines mechanisms of [[imperialism]] that cause and perpetuate the tremendous inequality within and among nations
		- parts from [[Marx]] and [[Lenin]]
			- [[imperialism]] isn't just an economic relation arising out of [[Capitalism]]
			- [[imperialism]] is a structural relation of dominance defined in political, economic, military, cultural, communications terms
				- these types of [[imperialism]] have to be seen in their totality
				- not enough for [[IR]] scholars to be preoccupied with only political, military, or economic factors
				- entire structure of dominance has to be comprehended
	- parallels Antonio [[Gramsci]]'s perspective
	- argues that we must look inside societies to understand the effects of interactions among them
		- e.g. [[imperialism]] means that elites in *core* nations dominate and collaborate with elites in *periphery* nations to the disadvantage of the majority of people in the *periphery*
			- political effect of [[imperialism]]
			- economic effects: production of only a few commodities by a *periphery* state, trade concentrated with a particular *core* state
			- equal emphasis is given to other forms of [[imperialism]]
