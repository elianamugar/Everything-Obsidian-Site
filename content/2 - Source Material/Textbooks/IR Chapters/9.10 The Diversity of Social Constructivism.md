#### 9.10.0 The Diversity of Social Constructivism
- substantial diversity in constructivist thought
	- 1970s: Ernst B. [[Haas]], [[John Ruggie]]
		- work used to be based on assumptions of [[Liberalism]] image
		- role of ideas in the heads of actors
			- ideas grounded in interests as motive forces in int'l politics
	- 1980s: [[social constructivism]] emerges
#### 9.10.1 Schools of Thought
- influences from [[Durkheim]] and [[Max Weber]]
	- [[John Ruggie]]: [[neoclassical constructivism]]
		- along with Nicholas [[Onuf]], Friedrich [[Kratochwil]], [[Emanuel Adler]], [[Martha Finnemore]], Peter [[Katzenstein]], Ernst B. [[Haas]], Jean [[Elshtain]]
	- theories incorporate values, norms, other ideational factors
		- doesn't reject canons of science, standards, methodologies for testing hypotheses/propositions
- [[Onuf]]'s [[World of Our Making (1989) by Nicholas Onuf]]
	- sets stage for important role [[constructivism]] plays in IR theory
	- "people make society and society makes people" #quote 
		- we would not be human but for our social relations
	- human agency matters
		- theories should address choices agents make
	- structure/social arrangement of society within which agents operate is given by its rules and institutions
		- rules and institutions made by human agency
			- provides basis and context for agents to act
	- institutions may refer to concepts ([[balance of power]], spheres of influence, treaties, it'l regimes)
		- not just brick-and-mortar orgs
	- [[international society]] is "a complex institution within which many other related institutions are to be found"
		- like domestic societies
- [[Kratochwil]] and [[Emanuel Adler]]
	- [[Kratochwil]]: "Norms not only establish certain games and enable the players to pursue their goals within them, they also establish inter-subjective meanings that allow the actors to direct their action towards each other, communicate with each other, appraise the quality of their actions, criticize claims and justify choices." #quote 
		- emphasis on shared understandings that provide context for political interactions
	- [[Adler]]: focus on "role of identities, norms and causal understandings in the constitution of national interests"
		- constructivism: "a social theory about the role of knowledge and knowledgeable agents in the constitution of social reality"
		- goal: advance understandings of "role of inter-subjectivity and social context, the co-constitution of agent and structure, and rule-governed nature of society"
		- "constructivism sees the world as a project under construction, as *becoming* rather than *being*."
- [[postmodernism]] constructivism
	- [[David Campbell]], James [[Der Derian]], [[R. B. J. Walker]], [[Spike Peterson]]
	- contrasts [[neoclassical constructivism]]
		- rejects conventional [[Epistemology]] of social science
		- emphasizes [[Linguistics]] construction of subjects
			- results in "discursive practices" constituting the [[Ontology]]/foundational units of reality and analysis
- [[naturalistic constructivism]]
	- [[Wendt]], Roy [[Bhashkar]], David [[Dessler]]
		- similarities with [[neoclassical constructivism]]
			- approaches IR as part of the social sciences
			- emphasizes intersubjective aspects/structures of social life
			- ideational structures exist independently of human thought and interaction
				- can be treated as non-observables (like physical non-observables, e.g. subatomic particles) that underlie what we observe in nature
	- probes deeply into human psyche to find the ideational core underlying the subjectivity and intersubjectivity that define understanding
#### 9.10.2 Levels of Analysis
- categorizing constructivism by levels of analysis
	- systemic, unit-level, holistic constructivisms
		- systemic: focus on interaction of unitary state actors with domestic politics being ignored
			- interaction and relation of actors to one another matter
			- [[Wendt]]'s work
				- focuses on interaction of states and cultures of rules and norms that have been constructed to guide them
		- unit-level: emphasizes relation between domestic legal and social norms, and identities and interests of states
			- [[Katzenstein]]'s work
				- national [[security]] policies of [[Germany]] and [[Japan]]
				- trying to solve why two states that both experienced military defeat in [[WWII]], foreign occupation, and economic and democratic development, ended up adopting different domestic and external national security policies
				- constitutive national, social, legal norms are critical to understanding this outcome
		- holistic: bridges the int'l-domestic divide
			- two faces of a single social and political order
			- primary concern: dynamics of global change
				- [[John Ruggie]]'s work
					- rise of sovereign states out of [[Europe]]'s [[feudalism]]
				- [[Kratochwil]]'s work
					- theorizing on the end of the [[Cold War]]
