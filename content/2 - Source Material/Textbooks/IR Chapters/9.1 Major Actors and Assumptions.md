#### 9.1.0 Major Actors and Assumptions
- [[constructivism]] seeks to problematize the identities and interests of states
	- contrasts [[neorealism]] and [[neoliberalism]], believing identities and interests are *givens*
	- interested in state as agent/actor, also transnational orgs and int'l orgs
	- emphasizes importance of subjective and intersubjective exchanges and actions by humans as agents of these states and non-state org entities
- [[constructivism]] views int'l *structure* in terms of a social structure infused with ideational factors (norms, rules, law)
	- this structure can influence identities and interests of agents and int'l outcomes in humanitarian interventions, taboos on use of weapons of mass destruction, etc.
	- emphasis on social dimension of structure
		- contrasts [[neorealism]] and [[neoliberalism]], heavy materialists
- [[constructivism]] views the world as a project always under construction (becoming vs. being)
	- contrasts [[Realism]], [[Liberalism]], [[Economic Structuralism]], restricted view of change
- [[constructivism]] focuses on ontological and epistemological issues
	- far from most causal theorizing
		- [[Positivism]] premises lead theorists to seek an objective explanation of reality which minimizes the subjective part of understanding
	- subjectivity of humans –> impossibility of pure objectivity
- [[constructivism]] look to models of value-rational behavior
	- they don't reject the scientific method
	- adopts value-rational behavior with causal-explanation approach (presumption of rational behavior by actors)
	- value-rational behavior
		- focuses on ontological orientation and epistemological preferences
		- [[normative]] concerns and principled beliefs of actors/agents
	- results in rich historical and empirical analysis
		- explains an event/outcome by detailing impact of such factors and how they influence the construction and changes of interests and identities over time
