#### 6.14.0 A Liberal-Institutionalist Worldview
- 1960s – 1970s: literature on [[regional integration]] rises
	- inspired by early developments in [[Post-WWII]] era
- 1970s – 1990s: ascendancy of [[Liberalism]] image in world politics
- 1991: collapse of [[USSR]] and [[Eastern Europe]] empire
	- [[Liberalism]] application of [[interdependence]], [[regime theory]], [[democratic peace theory]] were popular
		- developed as a result of [[Europe]]'s events
- [[Liberalism]] perspective evident in [[Clinton]] administration
	- foreign policy favored [[NATO]] expansion
	- assumption that [[post-Cold War]] transformed int'l politics
		- [[Realism]]'s logic no longer dominated what was taking place in [[Europe]]
		- exception: 1990s [[Balkan wars]]
- [[America]]'s [[unipolar]] moment
	- allowed [[America]] to push for a zone of peace (Atlantic to Urals)
	- assumption: self-evident [[America]] was a benign [[hegemonic power]] and there was no reason for a beaten-down [[Russia]] to view [[America]] as a threat
- [[economic interdependence]] binds [[Western Europe]] and [[Eastern Europe]]
	- 2000: [[EU]] emerges
		- economic policies expanded at the same time as [[democracy]] [[revolution]]
- logic of [[democratic peace theory]], over time war wouldn't exist
	- what's the purpose of [[NATO]] given the expanding zone of peace?
#### 6.14.1 Liberalism and the Russian Invasion of Ukraine
- over past decade, backsliding of [[democracy]] worldwide (even in [[Europe]])
- February 2022: invasion of [[Ukraine]]
	- shocked [[Western]] elites
		- assumption: reliance on [[Russia]]'s oil and gas would have economic benefits for their countries AND would make it unlikely that [[Russia]] would pursue policies that threaten this lucrative trade
	- showed [[Realism]]'s perspective on [[interdependence]] (a source of vulnerability and means to exert political pressure) was more accurate than [[Liberalism]]'s vision of trade (pacifying effect on all parties)
		- case of rising [[China]] also proved this
- over the years, [[EU]] members have lost enthusiasm for European project
	- case of [[UK]]'s [[Brexit]] (UK's withdrawal from [[EU]])
	- [[nationalism]] hasn't been pacified by positive effects of economic gains and [[interdependence]] among states
	- [[democracy]] made early [[post-Cold War]] gains, but [[authoritarian]] regimes have emerged in [[Russia]], [[Belarus]], [[Hungary]], [[Turkey]], etc.
- [[Russia-Ukraine War]]
	- [[Realism]]: focus on shifts in balance of power since 1991 that, from [[Russia]]'s perspective, threatened them
		- assertion 1: any Russian leader would've been insistent that [[NATO]]'s expansion eastward was a threat to [[Russia]]'s own security interests
			- [[Liberalism]]'s explanations of world politics fared poorly compared to [[Realism]]
			- [[Liberalism]]'s advocacy of greater [[interdependence]] in an increased [[Globalization]] would expand a zone of peace didn't work
	- critics of [[Realism]]: views [[Russia]]'s invasion due a decisive psychological factor, [[Putin]]'s [[narcissism]]
		- [[Liberalism]]: focuses on importance of individual and their agency
			- key factor: [[Putin]]
			- [[Putin]]'s mission: restoration of [[Russia]]'s greatness
				- inspired by Josef [[Stalin]], Czar [[Nicholas I]], [[Peter the Great]]
		- war termed as "Putin's War" or "Putin's Regime"
		- literature focused on individual level of analysis
			- perception, motivated bias, tendency of leaders to be overconfident in deciding to go to war
	- [[Putin]] miscalculated [[Ukraine]]'s resolve, the [[Western]] response, and ability of [[Russia]]'s military
	- phenomenon of [[groupthink]]
	- [[prospect theory]]: [[Putin]]'s apparent willingness to take risks to avoid potential loss of [[Ukraine]]
- [[agent-structure]] debate: how much of the explanation of [[Russia-Ukraine War]] is due to the structure of the int'l system and how much is due to human agency?
