#### 7.6.0 Change and Globalization
- [[IR]] scholars interested in understanding *system change*
	- changes of the system
	- lesser changes within an existing system that retains its basic characteristics
- [[Economic Structuralism]], changes within [[world-system theory]] 3 categories:
	1. changes in the actors' positions within [[capitalist world-system]]
		- [[Wallerstein]]: "There is constant and patterned movement between groups of economic actors as to who shall occupy various positions in the hierarchy of production, profit, and consumption." #quote 
			- e.g. 17th c. Dutch empire gave way to [[UK]] domination –> 20th c. [[America]] rises to prominence
		- despite different *core* powers, the hierarchical nature of the system remains the same
	2. phases/cycles of capitalist growth and contraction that affect all societies
		- period of relative social stability and economic stagnation precedes 20-30 yrs of rapid economic growth
		- then 20-30 yrs of economic decline
		- then expansion again
		- [[Hobson]]'s key factor: overproduction is central to interplay of economic, social, political forces
	3. [[structural transformation]] of the system
		- historical and geographical expansion of the [[capitalist world-system]]
			- incorporates new areas of the globe and non-integrated sectors of the world-economy
		- can still be viewed as changes within the system
			- the capitalist mode of production is still capitalist
				- even if it's changing its character
		- [[Economic Structuralism]]'s view of [[capitalist world-system]] isn't static
			- world-system is dynamic, reflecting activities and changes
- changes *of* the capitalist system
	- is [[Globalization]] a new transforming force, or is it the mere continuation of long-established capitalist trends?
	- [[world-system theory]] scholars
		- one major historical transformation occurrence: the movement from [[feudalism]] into [[Capitalism]] in 16th c. [[Europe]]
		- claim: [[Globalization]] long existed before the term existed
			- process of [[Globalization]] goes back to 16th c. and rise of [[Capitalism]]
			- [[Globalization]] isn't just a 20th-21st c. phenomenon
- what is beyond [[Capitalism]]?
	- [[Economic Structuralism]] is ambivalent on this point
		- [[Wallerstein]]: "The Rise and Future Demise of the World Capitalist System"
		- [[Wallerstein]]: "The Limited Possibilities of Transformation within the Capitalist World Order"
	- 1980s: theorists discuss [[Eastern Europe]] and [[Socialism]] states might succumb to powerful forces of [[capitalist world-system]]
		- accurate analysis
	- [[Economic Structuralism]] points to possible transformational processes that might make significant system change possible
		- non-[[Marxism]] scholars: Hayward [[Alker]], Johan [[Galtung]]
			- downplays concept of constraints that supposedly limits evolution of alternative world orders
			- [[Alker]]: change occurs through dialectical clash of world forces and different visions of world futures
				- contradictory world order contenders
					- capitalist power balancing, [[Socialism]], collective self-reliance, corporatist [[authoritarian]]ism
				- makes system transformation possible
			- [[Galtung]]: int'l system is open and subject to change
				- int'l system is on the decline (nation-states, MNCs, any world gov't that may be constructed in the distant future)
		- neither uses [[Globalization]], but what they discuss falls under the conception of contemporary [[Globalization]]
- what role does human volition play in system change?
	- [[agent-structure]] debate
		- some see large-scale historical processes as relatively immune from actions of humans
			- [[determinism]]
		- others downplay constraints
			- [[voluntarism]]
			- call for [[revolution]]s to end [[Capitalism]]'s [[exploitation]]
			- despite particularity of [[Latin American]] or other [[Third World]] regions emphasized by [[dependency]] theorists, these areas remain consistent with patterns of [[Capitalism]] discussed by [[Marx]]
			- *subordinated classes* have been neglected by some [[Economic Structuralism]] in favor of a focus on exchange relations among societies
			- class contradictions and intensification of class conflict still makes [[proletarian revolution]] possible ([[Marx]])
			- long-standing [[Marxism]] debate on potential for [[revolution]] in [[Third World]] – revolutionaries can help produce world-system change
				- do agents merely have to wait for objective conditions to be ripe, serving as catalysts when they are, or can they do something subjectively to effect these conditions?
			- collapse of [[USSR]], [[China]]'s embrace of [[Capitalism]] quieted these critics
		- some take an intermediate position
			- powerful "structures of domination" acknowledged
			- permanent struggle among classes also acknowledged
			- [[Fernando Henrique Cardoso]] ([[dependency]] theory): "Instead of accepting the existence of a determined course in history, there is a return to conceiving of it as an open-ended process. Thus, if structures delimit the range of oscillation, the actions of humans, as well as their imagination, revive and transfigure these structures and may even replace them with others that are not predetermined." #quote 
			- [[Terence Hopkins]], [[Wallerstein]]: study of [[capitalist world-system]] is the "theoretical side of the practical work of transforming the modern world-system into the [[Socialism]] world order that modern social change has made at once politically necessary and historically possible." #quote 
				- room for human political will to effect transformation
				- [[Economic Structuralism]] is not [[determinism]]
				- allows for (and even requires) political action
- [[neostructuralism]]
	- 1990s: response to [[Washington Consensus]]
		- emphasis on *austerity* and [[free-market]] oriented policies as the way for countries to solve their development problems
	- critical of [[Realism]]'s reliance on unitary, rational state-as-actor
		- in line with [[Economic Structuralism]] emphasis
	- recognizes importance of earlier [[dependency]] and [[world-system theory]]
		- influences: Fernand [[Braudel]], Karl [[Polyani]], Antonio [[Gramsci]]
	- interested in understanding how global processes interact with other processes of state and social transformation occurring at many other levels of analysis of the world-system
	- [[IR]] isn't limited to foreign policy or patterns of distributions of capabilities, nor confined to reducing [[IR]] to economic variables
		- focus of analysis on transformative processes
		- states and economic, social forces have to be taken into account
		- web of relations and forces intricately linked, transcending all levels of analysis
	- consistent with [[Economic Structuralism]] tradition: gov'ts play secondary role in socioeconomic structures and forces when it comes to explaining world politics
