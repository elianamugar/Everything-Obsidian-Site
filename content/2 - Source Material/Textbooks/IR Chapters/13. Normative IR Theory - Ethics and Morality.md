#### 13.0 Normative IR Theory - Ethics and Morality
- normative theory
	- its role in IR
	- important contributors to its literature
	- challenge of applying normative concerns to actual foreign policy choices
- intellectual traditions underlying the whole IR field is a blend of normative (what *should* be the case?) and empirical questions (what *is* the case?)
	- inconceivable of key influencers of IR theory ([[Machiavelli]], [[Grotius]], [[Kant]], [[E. H. Carr]], [[Marx]]) to discuss politics without some attention to relation between facts and values
		- facts and values are inseparable
		- writers differed on relative importance of each
- who incorporates normative considerations within subjectivities and intersubjectivities that define their interpretive understandings
	- [[constructivism]], [[critical theory]], [[postmodernism]], [[feminism]], [[green theory]]
- examination of socially constructed norms/rules that have guided actual conduct in IR rests on normative theories that contribute to their legitimacy over time
	- law of war, intervention, norms on distributive justice, human rights, humanitarian law
