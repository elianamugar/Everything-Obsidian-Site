#### 9.11.0 Wendt's "Naturalist" Constructivism
- [[Anarchy is What States Make of It by Alexander Wendt]]
	- claim: [[self-help system]] and power politics are socially constructed under [[anarchy]]
- [[Social Theory of International Politics by Alexander Wendt]]
	- challenge: finding a correct understanding of the world around us in which we're so integral a part, as subjective creatures
		- using science as far as it'll take us, fully knowing we might not get it right
		- assuming objective world is out there but constrained in search for reality by our own subjectivities as humans
			- affected by the ontologies we have and the epistemologies we're prone to adopt
	- not a pessimist
		- while we may not have unmediated access to the world, we can still make great strides in understanding how it works, yet be humble about the truth claims we assert
	- Wendt's 2 basic tenets of constructivism:
		1. the structures of human association are determined primarily by shared ideas rather than material forces
		2. identities and interests of purposive actors are constructed by these shared ideas rather than given by nature
		- ontology of IR is social
			- through ideas, states ultimately relate to one another
			- these ideas help define *who* and *what* states are
	- title of book parallels [[Theory of International Politics (1979) by Kenneth N. Waltz]] ([[Kenneth Waltz]])
	- identity of the self as a function of the other
		- agency and structure are mutually constituted, shaping one another
			- states (people acting for states) over time have constituted the int'l culture of generally accepted norms and concepts that have legitimacy as part of IR
			- ideational structure constitutes and sustains the states in the system/society of states they've constructed
			- states continue to reinforce/support existing norms, adapting them/constituting new ones that reflect changing circumstances/new points of consensus
				- e.g. norm against slavery
				- reciprocal process continues
	- opposed to [[postmodernism]] [[constructivism]], [[Wendt]] doesn't completely reject the materially based structure used in [[neorealism]] (distribution of capabilities/power among states)
		- incorporates materially based structure and redefines it in his conception of social structure
		- the ideational (culture of generally accepted norms and other values that have been constructed under [[anarchy]]) as the fabric of system structure
			- material power and interests's meaning and effects depend on the social structure of the system
				- specifically which 3 cultures of [[anarchy]] is dominant
					- [[Hobbes]], [[Locke]], [[Kant]]
			- instead of a single [[anarchy]] and singular logic, there are different understandings of anarchy
				- [[Hobbes]]: "state of war" of all against all
				- [[Locke]]: rivalry but guided by mutually agreed rules
				- [[Kant]]: community of accepted norms
			- amount of [[War]] and conflict is conditioned by type of [[anarchy]] in existence
			- these social structures have 3 elements
				- material resources
				- shared knowledge (ideas)
				- human practices
	- states (and other actors) as agents can shape the world within which they're immersed, not just prisoners of status quo
		- [[anarchy]] doesn't need to be dog-eat-dog world ([[Hobbes]], [[Realism]])
			- the strong do what they will and the weak do what they must
		- [[Locke]]: agreed rules and institutions give the systemic culture of IR today
	- [[Wendt]] rejects any claims of inevitability
		- optimistic that int'l culture can grow beyond current common understandings of rules that states and other agents follow in their relations with each other
	- important interrelations among ideas, material factors, social interaction
		- essence of mind-body relation and issue of consciousness
		- interest in how ideational and material factors interact in nature as a whole (humans and social relationships)
			- is the material before the ideational? isn't the source of ideas the human brain, a material entity?
				- [[Realism]] and "physicalists" claim
				- [[Wendt]] objects to contemporary thinking about the mind that's dominated by the [[materialism]] worldview of classical physics and the assumption that reality is purely material
					- why do we have to assume that contemporary social science and its conceptualization of the relation of mind (ideas) to body (material world) must be compatible with classical physics?
					- what if consciousness is viewed as a quantum mechanical phenomenon, the domain of subatomic physics of particles and waves?
						- humans would just be walking wave particles, not classical material objects
					- can we assume there's an ideational-material composite in the social sciences similar to wave-particle relation posited in natural sciences?
						- physicists ponder over question of whether the character of light is particle (material) or waves of energy (ideational in social sciences)
							- encapsulated by the term "quantum"
		- consciousness ([[Wendt]]: "the basis of social life") – core concept in theorizing about interpretive understanding
			- "a macroscopic quantum mechanical phenomenon" #quote 
			- it's incorrect to view matter as purely material or inanimate; it's both "material and phenomenal, outside and inside"
			- ontological position: "Matter is an active, 'minded' phenomenon, not the inert, mindless substance of materialism" #quote 
				- "the mind-body problem is a fundamental problem of *social* science, not just neuroscience"
		- material and ideational are bound together in human consciousness
			- consciousness is not external/separate from human actions; it's part of them
			- rational-choice theorists discount subjective experiences and other aspects of consciousness
				- there's "an implicit materialism telling us that consciousness is epiphenomenal" (secondary and not causal)
					- in other words: material determines the ideational, not the other way around
		- [[Wendt]]'s "quantum consciousness" and "participatory [[Epistemology]]"
			- similarities between social lives of human beings and unpredictability/uncertainty in subatomic/quantum world
			- defines social context in which humans act
- criticisms of [[Wendt]]'s mind-body formulation
	- [[reductionism]] inherent in Wendt's mind-body formulation
		- trying to get deeper within human psyche to depict the relation between the ideational and the material
	- [[Positivism]] criticism
		- objects Wendt's [[metaphysics]] (characterization of ideational metaphorically as a "wave" lacking the materiality of particles, thus coming close to identifying a life force/spirit)
	- fault with Wendt's metaphorical use of [[quantum]]
- Wendt's other dualisms and dialectics
	- is there synthesis to be found (should we learn to live with the tensions) between
		1. [[Positivism]] and [[normative]] (subjectivity/interpretivism) – objective science and human self-understanding
		2. [[rationalism]] and [[constructivism]]
		3. [[Idealism]] and [[Realism]] – ideas and the material world
- Wendt doesn't see a contradiction between science or positivism and interpretive understanding
	- draws criticism from causal and interpretive theorists
	- believes one can be informed by (and compatible with) the other unless we build them as straw men – constructing them as if they were mutually exclusive
	- Wendt's [[Positivism]] is broad commitment to *science*
		- rejects a certain kind of [[Positivism]] when it's treated as a *philosophy* of science
			- [[David Hume]]'s causation, law-like generalizations, deductive theory, etc.
	- tries to combine positivist [[Epistemology]] with interpretivist ontology
		- complementary between positivism and interpretivism
		- don't need to be forced to choose "between a positivism in which consciousness makes no difference and an interpretivism in which it has no naturalistic basis" #quote
