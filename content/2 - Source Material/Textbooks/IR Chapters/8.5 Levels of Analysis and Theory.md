#### 8.5.0 Levels of Analysis and Theory
- primary level of analysis: global/systems level
	- 3 key concepts: int'l system, [[international society]], [[world society]]
	- states are major actors in [[international society]]
		- historical-sociological approach emphasizes rise of [[Europe]]'s state and societal systems –> spread of [[Western]] conceptions (i.e. [[sovereignty]]) globally
- [[Ontology]] primacy in [[The English School]]'s concept of [[world society]]
	- opened to other units and levels of analysis to include individuals, groups, transnational orgs 
	- impact of [[constructivism]]
		- emphasis on social relations and [[agent-structure]] debate
	- methodology: traditional approaches ([[Hedley Bull]])
		- develop set of interrelated concepts and categories to guide research and help structure questions that guide scholarly inquiry
		- emphasis on [[historical sociology]]
			- rebuttal to historicism of some [[neorealism]] conceptions of [[IR]]
- causal theorizing based on [[Positivism]] in early [[The English School]]
	- associated with [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]]
	- newer scholars avoid [[Positivism]] explanatory vs. interpretive understanding dichotomy
		- offers synthesis of different perspectives and concepts
	- mostly spawned positivist theories and interpretive understandings that guide much of contemporary IR theorizing
