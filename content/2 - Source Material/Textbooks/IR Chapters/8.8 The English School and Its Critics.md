#### 8.8.3 Political Economy, the Environment, and Gender
- what's absent from early [[The English School]] authors
	- economy and global environment integral to [[international society]] and [[world society]]
	- maybe concerned with high politics of [[diplomacy]], [[collective security]], alliance politics
		- discounts the salience of political-economic and environmental issues
		- [[Barry Buzan]]: "The English School's founding fathers... were too much in thrall to [[universalism]] principles of order and justice derived from debates in political theory and were too disinterested in international political economy." #quote 
	- founders gave less attention to int'l economy
		- environment, human rights successor generations examine possibilities and limitations of integrating concerns into the triad and its understanding of [[international society]]
- [[The English School]]'s commonalities with other images
	- almost a complete lack of knowledge or interest in [[feminism]] gender perspective on IR
		- [[Jacqui True]]: "This neglect of gender reveals the concept of [[international society]] to be neither open nor sufficiently dynamic enough to capture or explain the social sources and dimensions of interstate behavior, and world politics more broadly." #quote 
			- where are women in international society?
#### 8.8.4 Conceptual and Philosophical Eclecticism
- recognition of diverse traditions of thought 
	- [[The English School]] influenced by [[Hobbes]], [[Machiavelli]], [[Grotius]], [[Kant]]
	- [[Realism]] appreciates [[The English School]]'s recognition of [[anarchy]]
	- [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]] agree on importance of [[Grotius]] to our understanding of IR
	- [[social constructivism]] and [[The English School]]'s compatible view on evolution of [[international society]] and development of norms
		- [[Alexander Wendt]]'s description of [[Locke]]'s culture comes close to [[Grotius]]'s [[rationalism]] perspective in [[The English School]]
- eclectic intellectual heritage can lead to criticism
	- from [[Positivism]] who aspires to develop deductively-based theories
	- [[structural realism]] or [[neorealism]] find [[The English School]]'s smorgasbord unappealing
		- agree with [[Hedley Bull]]'s critique of [[behavioralism]]
		- question to what extent a truly "classical" and [[historical sociology]] approach to understanding IR can help in developing new explanatory theories
	- [[neoliberal institutionalism]] finds [[The English School]]'s assumptions too diverse
		- relies on [[rational-actor assumption]] to account for the conditions under which institutions can aid int'l cooperation
- [[The English School]] attempts integration of the field
	- from 1960s onward
	- positivist and interpretive understandings compete ([[Positivism]] vs. [[normative]])
	- images clash based on diverse underlying assumptions
	- may not be a burning issue
		- multiplicity of interests, methods, paradigms, images, concepts, value preferences may be beneficial
		- greater unity could result in uniformity and intellectual tyranny of a dominant paradigm/school of thought
		- eclecticism might be encouraged
	- main concern: whether diversity amounts to no more than multiple limited perspectives
		- closes off and encapsulates work in separate domains within the field, mutually exclusive
		- [[The English School]] can be an antidote to this narrowness
