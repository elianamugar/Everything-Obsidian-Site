#### 2.2.0 Sun Tzu
- observed the conduct of political entities in ancient China (ways and means of warfare)
- [[The Art of War]] - leadership and strategy in warfare, sports, business, politics, marriage, etc.
- reflects realist perspective ([[Realism]])
- reflects [[Taoism]] philosophy
- addresses conduct of war and competition between states
##### 2.2.0.1 Historical Context
- Zhou dynasty lasted longer than any other dynasty (1050-221 BCE) –> Qin dynasty
	- rise of hegemonic kingdoms (7 major competitors) that vied for expanded power and territory (similar to Europe in early 15th c.) ([[Warring States period]])
- larger armies –> arms race (soldiers, supplies, weapons, bureaucracies to administer them)
- perpetual struggle for [[predominance]]
	- required mobilization of resources to engage in total war
	- similar to Europe with the rise of centralized, expanded state power
- **Charles Tilly:** war made the state and the state made war
##### 2.2.0.2 The Art of War
- Han dynasty - famous as a strategist and his treatise
- role of the commander/strategist to assess the relative importance of each aphorism and, in combo, develop a strategy that can deceive an opponent
- [[Realism]] emphasis on the role of rationality in planning for the defense of the state
	- ultimate responsibility of the ruler
	- Machiavelli in 16th c., Clausewitz in 19th c.
- conventional wisdom of the time: martial virtues, valor, glory of war (in elite, aristocratic ethos)
- refutes Confucianism, which viewed war as evil and condemned military obsssions of rulers
- *if war is to be fought, it should be done in an ethical manner*
	- the cause must be worthy one and have the support of the people
	- contrasts current sentiment of US-Israel-Iran war (2026)
- Ch. 1: making of plans
	- "War is a grave affair of state; it is a place of life and death, a road to survival and extinction, a matter to be pondered carefully."
	- Five Fundamentals - allows comparison and contrast the approach to war by different leaders and commanders
		1. The Way - military men must be of one mind with their rulers and willing to die for them
		2. Heaven - yin and yang, cold and hot, cycle of the seasons
		3. Earth - distance and proximity, open and confined ground, ease and danger, life and death
		4. Command - wisdom, integrity, compassion, courage, severity
		5. Discipline - organization, chain of command, control of expenditures
	- "every commander is aware of these Five Fundamentals. He who grasps them wins; he who fails to grasp them loses."
	- emphasizes solidarity of the relationship between commander and ruler, of military strategy and tactics with political objectives
	- "The Way of War Is a Way of Deception" - use of deception at the strategic and tactical level
		- "When able, feign inability"; "When near, appear far"; "Lure with bait; strike with chaos"; "Attack where he is unprepared, appear where you are unexpected."
- Ch. 2: waging war
	- serious deliberation on war must start with this question: "Which ruler has the Way?" followed by "Which general has the ability?"
	- key to successful implementation is to "exploit the dynamic within" the plan to include opportunities that present themselves to the commander
- Ch. 3-12: required talents of a military commander
	- "Ultimate excellence lies not in winning every battle, but in defeating the enemy without ever fighting"
	- list of priorities that a commander should attack:
		1. enemy's strategy
		2. enemy's alliances
		3. enemy's army
		4. the use of siege warfare against his cities
			- heavy costs ( time, men, material)
	- skillful strategist defeats the enemy without actually engaging in battle
	- civil-military nexus: relationship between king and commander
		- Sun Tzu on side of commander
- Ch. 13: espionage
	- information superiority
	- collect and utilize intelligence to commander's advantage
	- use of spies
	- useful intelligence helps strategy to calculate where to strike the decisive blow, but also to keep the enemy off-balance, leaving the enemy's planning in uncertainty
- main focus is military contest
	- each military decision has to be recalibrated once the enemy has responded
- "Know the enemy, know yourself, and victory is never in doubt, not in a hundred battles."
	- contemporary strategic thinking: "net assessment" - consider how the strategy and capabilities of both sides compare to one another
#### 2.2.1 Kautilya
- Kautilya is author of [[The Arthashastra]]
	- rooted in realism
- key advisor and mentor in assisting rise to power of [[Chandragupta]], first Mauryan emperor
- focus on art of government, state has three purposes:
	1. protect the state from external aggression
	2. maintain law and order within the state
	3. safeguard the welfare of the people
- foreign policy, less than 1/5 of the work
	- generalizes about international relations
- similar to Machiavelli, concerned with security and foreign policy of small states existing in a highly competitive international environment
- takes into consideration:
	- relative power among states
	- need to balance short-term advantages with potential future long-term advantages
	- intangible and unpredictable factors that can affect foreign policy choices
- "war" in 4 categories:
	1. diplomacy - a king finds himself in a weaker military position and decides it's unwise to engage in battle
	2. open warfare - specifying time and place for battle and devising an appropriate strategy
	3. concealed warfare - psychological warfare
	4. clandestine warfare - achieve the objective without actual battle (primarily using assassination as a tool)
- defense measures - establish forts for different terrains
	- includes weapons, armor, siege engines
- force structure - elephants, chariots, horses, infantry
- covert ops + spying = important to foreign policy
	- creation of secret service
	- intelligence is critical
#### 2.2.2 Overview of Early Greek, Chinese, and Indian Thinkers
- Homer - determinism vs. voluntarism (free will of human agency)
	- individuals matter in war
		- outcome linked to physical, moral, intellectual strength
	- gods and oracles can still dominate events if they choose to do so, making it difficult for humans to foresee the outcomes of their actions
- Herodotus - concern for security and autonomy of tribes, cultures, city-states
	- individuals' thirst for power and glory initiate conflicts
	- marshaling internal resources and joining alliances are two basic ways to defend a state's autonomy
- Greek writing on war and peace –> realist tradition and security
	- martial values: heroism and glory
- *Lysistrata* by Aristophanes
	- antiwar themes
		- women agree to deny their favors to their husbands until men finally renounce warfare and make peace
- Sun Tzu - importance of commander
- Kautilya - emphasis of rational planning
	- most important responsibility of any ruler, defense of the state
