#### 8.7.0 The English School, Liberals, and Social Constructivists
- [[The English School]]'s similarities with [[Liberalism]] and [[social constructivism]]
	- emphasis on [[anarchy]], [[international society]]
	- norms that become established in form of accepted understandings or agreed-upon rules are foundation for int'l regimes
		- [[Liberalism]]: regimes consist of voluntarily agreed sets of principles, norms, rules, procedures on diverse issues
			- human rights, war and peace, commercial transactions, etc.
		- and servicing institutions, gov't and non-gov't orgs
		- regimes and institutions are outcomes of human design centuries
			- provides an authoritative basis for regulating/influencing the behavior of state and non-state actors
		- development of [[international society]] can be viewed as [[constructivism]]
- [[social constructivism]]
	- portrays [[self-help system]], power politics, [[sovereignty]], and similar concepts have been socially constructed or evolved under [[anarchy]] of IR and world politics
		- IR and world politics don't have an independent existence
		- ideas, culture, and norms matter and can influence behavior, including creation of multilateral institutions
- [[The English School]] scholars
	- those sympathetic to [[solidarism]] are more prone to examine human rights and humanitarian intervention
		- similar interest expressed about global environment
	- reflects [[normative]] aspirations in the School
