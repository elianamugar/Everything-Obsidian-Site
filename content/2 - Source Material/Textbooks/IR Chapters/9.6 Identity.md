#### 9.6.0 Identity
- identities: relatively stable, role-specific understandings and expectations about one's self
	- acquired by interacting with/defining the self in relation to a structure composed of social relationships, shared meanings, rules, norms, practices
		- identity breaks down [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]] dichotomy between systemic and state levels of analysis
		- provides broader perspective on mutual constitution of state and system or agent and structure
- individual level
	- human consciousness is important
		- the "self" that interprets and thus constitutes the "other" outside of oneself, much as the other outside of the self, gives meaning or identity to the self
			- e.g. respective identities of professor and student makes sense within context and interaction of a classroom setting
				- identities mutually constituted
		- over time, identities can change
			- e.g. student goes to graduate school and eventually becomes a professor and rejoins alma mater as faculty member
				- student-professor identities evolve into colleagues
- IR
	- dominant intersubjective understanding and social relationship between [[USSR]] and [[America]] during [[Cold War]] was *enemies*
		- anti-[[USSR]] and anti-[[Communism]] –> [[America]] identity
			- framework for [[America]] view on [[USSR]] with suspicion and interpreted all Soviet actions
	- 1980s – 1990s: social relationship changes when [[Communism]] regimes collapse
		- [[America]] identity associated with anti-[[Communism]] changes
		- [[America]] definition of int'l threats and nat'l interests shifts
	- 2022: [[Russia-Ukraine War]] 
		- speculation of new [[Cold War]]
		- return to antagonistic relationship between [[America]] and [[Russia]]
- how can one recognize the existence of a particular identity?
	- look for habitual actions consistent with this identity and interpretations
		- e.g. [[America]] attempts during [[Cold War]] to block expansion of [[USSR]] influence in [[Third World]] and [[Western Europe]]
		- actions referred to as *practices*
	- monitor the discourse or combination of language and techniques employed to maintain these practices
		- diplomatic language emphasizing constructive engagement, [[balance of power]], use of force, [[deterrence theory]]
- identities can change over time and across contexts
	- identities aren't immutable characteristics of individuals, groups, states, whatever agent
	- identities are produced and aren't givens (like state's interests)
	- empirical research task for [[constructivism]]
		- explore how interaction and context influence the development of the meaning of self
			- self at level of individual decision maker, group of decision makers, an NGO, or conceptualized as an aggregate applying to state, nation, or society as a whole
		- interactions may bolster, undermine, or change these identities resulting in either positive or negative outcomes
- influences on identities can stem from any number of sources
	- domestic/endogenous sources
		- broad cultural aspects of a society or military doctrine
			- can result from internal distribution of political power
	- race, gender, nationality, religion, ideology
	- external/exogenous sources
		- int'l norms ([[multilateralism]])
			- can contribute to defining a country's identity and role it assumes in relations with other countries
	- principled beliefs
		- e.g. moral illegitimacy of slavery and commitment to human rights
	- case study: [[Cold War]]
		- empirical studies examining the development of collective identity among members of [[NATO]]
			- created in 1949 to counter perceived [[USSR]] threat
	- case studies: [[Middle East]], [[Japan]], [[Germany]], [[USSR]]
		- examines identity creation, transformation of alliances, shifts in identity
- construction of identity isn't limited to perceptions of another actor being friend or foe
	- idea of mutual constitution of states and structures goes beyond recognizing that there exists interaction between them
	- all images and approaches to IR discuss impact of interactions
		- key point: while the interactions of states contribute to the construction of norms, shared meanings, institutions of IR, they in turn influence the identity and behavior of states (and human agents who represent them)
		- a reciprocal process
		- identity and behavior can be redefined
