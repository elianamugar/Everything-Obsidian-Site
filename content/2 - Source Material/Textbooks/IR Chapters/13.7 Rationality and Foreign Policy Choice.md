#### 13.7.0 Rationality and Foreign Policy 
- foreign policy choice
	- domain in which moral and ethical values apply directly
	- decision makers choose authoritatively among competing alternatives
	- can the rational choice model allow us to avoid or side-step normative issues?
- rational model
	- critical element in [[Realism]]
	- amounts to policy makers' ordering of alternatives, making decisions, taking actions to achieve most efficient outcome in terms of the ends sought
		- not a value-free process
			1. determining the objective/ends to be sought involves value choices
			2. the idea that the means chosen to achieve those goals should be the most efficient ,the best, or "good enough" is itself a value underlying the decision-making calculus
			3. even if statesmen can reach a consensus on what general values should be pursued internationally, there may be honest disagreement as to how these values are to be defined and implemented
				- e.g. human rights
					- difficult to forge a consensus among gov'ts on which criteria should apply
					- 1948: [[Universal Declaration of Human Rights]]
					- which rights are to be protected?
						- the relative importance or weight of different values when they conflict
						- whose rights (individuals, groups, classes, states) take precedence?
	- [[Liberalism]] challenge
		- whether foreign policy decision making can ever conform to a rational model when actors involved are various orgs and small groups of individuals
			- when decisions are the outcome of [[bargaining]], compromising, "end-running", etc.
			- each separate actor may act rationally to achieve its own goals and values
				- not same as assuming that those in authority act rationally to achieve the goals/interests of the entire state and society
	- [[Economic Structuralism]]
		- some question the rationality of a decision-making/foreign policy process that's dominated by narrow class interests
- whatever may be the rationality of individuals, institutions, classes in maximizing/serving their own values/interests, the outcome for the whole may be suboptimal
