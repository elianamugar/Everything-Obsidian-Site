#### 9.2.0 Intellectual Precursors and Influences
- [[Kant]] (1724 – 1804)
	- impact on development of [[Phenomenology]]
		- how human consciousness affects our understandings of what we observe or what appears to us
			- *phenomena*: objects we observe
			- *noumena*: the unknowable essence of objects as things in themselves, apart from how we may see them or how they may appear
	- late-19th c. – early-20th c. neo-Kantians
		- phenomenology perspective from nature –> culture and social relations
			- net effect: set the social science apart from natural sciences
			- our knowledge (what we think we "know") flows from our subjectivity, imposing mental frameworks on nature and our social world
			- observing phenomena in nature vs. humans
				- being a part of the social world we're observing (not separate)
	- [[constructivism]] scholars who follow this thinking
		- skeptical of natural science's [[Epistemology]] and [[Methodologies]] can be applied to IR or another social science inquiry
			- we take in what appears to us, then probe to discover what we can about the underlying essence of what we observe
			- knowing is a subjective endeavor
			- establish shared knowledge of nature with others when engaged intersubjectively
		- views individuals (and states) as social beings enmeshed in interactive [[normative]] context, which influences who they are and how they see others
- [[Locke]] (1632 – 1704)
	- influenced views on nature of [[anarchy]]
	- argument: before society is formed, one may be at peace in a state of nature or move to a state of war and back again
		- state of nature isn't necessarily a state of war
		- state of nature's [[anarchy]] isn't necessarily warlike
		- opposes [[Hobbes]]'s view of state of nature as a perpetual state of war
	- social contract among individuals establishes society ([[social contract theory]])
		- coming together by contract/agreement + state of nature isn't necessarily warlike –> influences [[social constructivism]]
	- Locke applies domestic level of analysis
		- [[Wendt]] applied –> IR
	- states and their established relations are constituted by *Lockean*, social-contract, rule-oriented culture
		- opposed to [[Hobbes]]'s dog-eat-dog world ([[Realism]] assumption)
	- [[constructivism]] views [[Realism]]'s view of [[anarchy]] as one of many types of anarchy
		- it's not a state of nature without rules
	- a rival view to [[Hobbes]]
		- [[sovereignty]] - *shared* expectation concerning *rules* of the game in relations among states
			- result of interactions, socialization, internalization of norms/agreed rules that mitigate danger of IR becoming a "war of all against all"
		- states may reach agreements with each other to maintain peace
			- remaining in Lockean state of nature or leaving it by forming a more peaceful int'l community envisioned by [[Kant]]
- Emile [[Durkheim]] (1858 – 1917)
	- inspired [[neorealism]] scholars
		- idea that structure (i.e. int'l system) shapes and constrains relations among units
	- engages in series of empirical studies to explain how various social outcomes are influenced by different bonds within social orders
		- e.g. individual feelings of social estrangement and suicide rates
			- claims that lower incidence of suicide rates in rural areas of [[France]] during [[Industrial Revolution]] was due to strong moral and social bonds of more traditional communities in countryside
	- important to [[constructivism]]: forms of sociality have causal impact
		- the nature of relations and interactions among actors
	- believed these relations can't be reduced to just material factors
		- like material reality, ideational factors can be studied scientifically
	- [[Positivism]] isn't inherently antithetical to constructivists's [[normative]] approach to understanding IR
- [[Max Weber]] (1864 – 1920)
	- influenced causal and interpretive understanding approaches to IR
	- questions objectivity in the social sciences
		- what we know is based on interpretations of what we think we see
		- knowledge in social sciences depends on interpretive understanding of the phenomena we observe
			- central to the discussion of general theory
				- [[constructivism]], [[feminism]], [[postmodernism]], [[critical theory]]
					- these lenses emphasize interpretive understanding that's found in [[Max Weber]]'s writing
				- [[Realism]], [[Liberalism]], [[Economic Structuralism]], [[The English School]]
					- framed by the images, standpoints, perspective that inform the way they approach IR theory
	- references the state as collective actor and contexts within which human behavior takes place
		- anticipates levels of analysis prominent in present-day IR theory
	- causal models
		- assumes behavior is rational and designed to achieve specific goals that may conflict with other actors' goals
			- starting point for [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]] to develop explanatory theory
		- [[Max Weber]]: human actions can be value driven
			- a value rationality in which choices follow from beliefs or commitments often rooted in moral, ethical, religious understandings
			- not less "rational" than behavior that follows an approach of maximizing gains/minimizing losses
			- "We are *cultural* beings endowed with the capacity and the will to take a deliberate attitude toward the world and to lend it *significance*." #quote 
			- actors' ability to interpret the meaning and significance of their social actions
			- ideas can play a [[normative]] role that goes beyond the narrow end of maximizing utility
- [[Durkheim]] and [[Max Weber]] argue that the critical ties that bind individuals to groups are ideational
	- ideas are subject to investigation by social-scientific methods
