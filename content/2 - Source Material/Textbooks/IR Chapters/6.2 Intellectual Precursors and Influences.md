#### 6.2.0 Intellectual Precursors and Influences
- impact of more recent theorists tends to be overlooked/ignored
	- not necessarily observers of [[IR]]
	- mostly economists, social scientists, theologians, political scientists interested in domestic politics
- common denominator: interest in state, individual, group
	- important theme: agency, focus on actors
#### 6.2.1 Interest Group Liberalism
- human agents at individual and small-group levels of analysis matter
- multi-actor [[pluralism]] of individuals in groups interacting and forming coalitions/counter-coalitions in domestic politics
	- [[Liberalism]]: this captures essence of politics across entire globe
	- [[interest group liberalism]]: int'l political processes aren't that different from domestic political processes within state
		- liberals reject [[Realism]]'s distinction between int'l and domestic politics
			- one is an extension of the other
			- literature on **decision-making** and [[Transnationalism]]
				- state-as-actor into component parts
				- emphasis on agency
- Theodore J. [[Lowi]]
	- [[America]]n political scientist
	- [[interest group liberalism]] 
		- assumes "role of gov't is one of assuring access to the most effectively organized, and of ratifying the agreements and adjustments worked out among competing leaders and their claims"
		- "belief in natural harmony of group competition"
		- defines "policy agenda and the public interest in terms of the organized interests in society"
	- observations of [[interest group liberalism]] consistent with liberal notions
		1. state as neutral arbiter
		2. potential for a natural harmon of interest (among groups of individuals)
		3. public concern for and participation in a policy process not restricted to elites
	- conflicts and competition, cooperation and collaboration among interest groups play an important role in image of politics
		- individuals form interest groups in attempts to outmaneuver, end-run, or overwhelm opposing groups/coalitions
		- politics is a game with very real stakes to be won or lost
			- authoritative choices made by gov't decision makers as outcome of this process
- [[David Truman]]
	- writings on [[interest group liberalism]]
	- influenced by Arthur F. [[Bentley]]'s [[The Process of Government (1908) by Arthur F. Bentley]]
	- outstanding characteristic of [[America]]'s politics
		- "multiplicity of coordinate points of access to gov'tal decisions"
	- conflictual nature of [[America]]'s politics
		- "overlapping membership among organized interest groups" provides "the principal balancing force in the politics of a multi-group society such as [[America]]" #quote 
- Harold [[Lasswell]] and [[Robert Dahl]]
	- [[Robert Dahl]]'s writings
		- [[America]]'s politics as a "system in which all the active and legitimate groups in the population can make themselves heard at some crucial stage in the decision process"
			- a decentralized system
				- "decisions are made by endless bargaining"
				- groups are central to the process
		- rather than majority rule or minority rule, argues "minorities rule" is more accurate
			- politically active groups (minorities) are most influential
- [[interest group liberalism]]'s image of politics
	- fragmented political system
		- multiple actors compete
	- human agents matter as much as societal factors, systems, structures
- what the group is to [[interest group liberalism]], the individual is to [[Liberalism]]
	- commonalities
		- agreement on fragmented nature of the state and society
		- agreement on potential for harmony to develop within representative democracies out of competition and conflict
	- state is not an independent actor separated from society
		- primary function as arbiter of conflicting demands and claims/arena for expression of such interests
	- focus of analysis less on state and more on competition and cooperation among individuals and groups as agents of both state and non-state actors in world politics
- theoretical efforts that reflect influence of [[Liberalism]] to understand how int'l order and collaboration can be achieved to enhance prospects for material welfare and peace
	- efforts reflect int'l problems confronted at the time of research and writing
		- [[Post-WWII]]
		- [[Cold War]]
