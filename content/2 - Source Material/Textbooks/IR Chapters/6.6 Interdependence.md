#### 6.6.0 Interdependence
- 1977: [[Keohane]] and [[Joseph Nye Jr.]] publish [[Power and Interdependence – World Politics in Transition (1977) by Robert O. Keohane and Joseph Nye Jr.]]
	- to develop concept of [[interdependence]] and make it analytically useful, *power* must be taken into consideration
	- [[interdependence]]'s definition
		- mutual dependence resulting from types of int'l transactions catalogued by [[Transnationalism]] scholars
			- flows of money, goods, services, people, communications
		- exists when there are "reciprocal (not necessarily symmetrical) effects among countries/actors in different countries"
			- sensitivity in Country *B* to what's going on/emanating from Country *A*
				- [[interdependence]] restricts autonomy
				- benefits to either party or both may outweigh the costs of [[interdependence]]
			- [[interdependence]] isn't just a matter of Country *B*'s vulnerability to Country *A* ([[Realism]] perspective)
	- recognition of [[vulnerability interdependence]]
		- e.g. when one country can manipulate the flow of oil to other countries
	- interests lay elsewhere
	- enthusiasts believe this as a challenge to [[Realism]] conceptions of [[IR]]
	- [[Keohane]] and [[Joseph Nye Jr.]] purpose of book: to provide analytical insights and a research program in areas that traditional [[Realism]]'s focus on matters of military-security and force tended to discount
- [[complex interdependence]]
	- ideal type constructed to analyze situations involving transnational issues
	- multiple channels connect society
	- absence of hierarchy among issues
	- military force isn't used by gov'ts against other gov'ts involved in the interdependent relation
