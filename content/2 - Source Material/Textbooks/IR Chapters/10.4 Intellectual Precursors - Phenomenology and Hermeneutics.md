#### 10.4.0 Intellectual Precursors – Phenomenology and Hermeneutics
- influence of [[Phenomenology]] in IR
	- a philosophical understanding that leads one to reject claims to knowing any "objective" reality independent of the human consciousness that gives meaning to the world around us
		- interpretation central to what we see
	- found in the interpretive understandings of [[constructivism]], [[feminism]], [[green theory]] (plus critical theory + postmodernism)
	- responds to an old philosophical question
		- [[Plato]] (427 – 347 BCE)
			- raises problem of distinguishing between appearances and underlying realities
				- ideal forms, essence, or spirit that lies beyond our senses or world of appearances
				- "knowledge has for its natural object the real – to know the truth about reality"
					- unity to be found in knowledge between an object we observe and its underlying essence/form
					- e.g. something may be beautiful, but it's the underlying idea/"essential form of beauty" that makes it appear to us as such
			- underlying form of justice in Plato's ideal [[Republic]] is an aim/challenge within state, domestic society, and IR
	- [[Kant]] (1724 – 1804)
		- [[Critique of Pure Reason (1781) by Immanuel Kant]]
			- distinction between appearance and reality
			- objects we observe called "phenomena"
				- distinguished from abstract "noumena" – the unknowable essence of objects as things in themselves
	- [[Hegel]] (1770 – 1831)
		- [[Phenomenology of Spirit (1807) by George W. F. Hegel]]
			- accepts Kant's claim that we can't know the "in-itself" (inner essence of what we observe)
			- explores ways and means by which we can unify the objective + subjective
				- "what something actually is" (in-itself) + "what we understand it to be" (for-itself)
				- in our consciousness of ourselves and the world around us that we experience
			- how are we to unify what actually *is* (objective) with what we observe/experience in our consciousness (subjective)?
			- how can we know "what truth is... if consciousness is still filled and weighed down with... so-called natural representations, thoughts, and opinions?"
				- seeking beyond prior perspectives/illusions and focus on phenomena themselves
					- Hegel's "phenomenological path"
						- we try to understand the essence/"spirit" underlying appearances
- phenomenologists
	- Edmund [[Husserl]] (1859 – 1938)
		- following Husserl
			- engaged in reflective study to phenomena to approach knowledge of underlying realities in developing interpretive understandings
		- influence reflected in existentialism (linked to [[France]])
			- influenced [[Sartre]]
	- Martin [[Heidegger]] (1889 – 1976)
		- student of [[Husserl]]
		- disputes Hegel's claim that such reality can be uncovered so decisively
		- focuses on the objects we experience, the sense of our own being, and what we understand to be the essence of things in general
			- our ontology in relation to the world we experience
		- denies belief in [[Existentialism]]
- phenomenology leads one to not take things as they may first appear (or have been made to appear)
	- probe for what underlies/stands behind appearances
- [[postmodernism]]'s major research concern of language
	- [[hermeneutics]]
		- challenges [[Positivism]]
		- argues that social facts are constituted and given meaning by the structures of language
			- consciousness can be studied only as mediated by language
			- language is what gives material conditions meaning for humans
		- these approaches seek to understand/recover the meanings common to actors by interpreting the self-understanding of actors
			- contrasts positivist explanation of independent causal processes
			- [[Heidegger]], [[Wittgenstein]]
				- [[Philosophical Investigations (1953) by Ludwig Wittgenstein]]
					- coping with ambiguities that come from the context of language use
			- later [[Europe]] perspectives that impacted IR
				- Jürgen [[Habermas]] (1929 – 2026)
					- [[Frankfurt School]] of [[critical theory]]
				- [[Nietzsche]] (1844 – 1900)
					- influenced [[postmodernism]]
				- [[Foucault]] (1926 – 1984), [[Derrida]] (1930 – 2004), [[Lyotard]] (1924 – 1998)
					- updated [[Nietzsche]]'s ideas
- positivism under assault for its attempts to:
	- separate facts from values
	- define and operationalize value-neutral concepts into precisely and accurately measurable variables
	- test truth claims in form of hypotheses drawn from/integral to theories
	- those who have tried to be scientific have been criticized for ignoring/taking insufficient account of the personal/human dimension of scholarship
- human consciousness and human subjectivity matter
	- we're not able to separate ourselves from the world we're observing
	- even if human agency doesn't impact the "laws" that govern the natural world, what we say and do has effects in the social world
		- we're hampered by the inability to be completely independent of the phenomena we're observing
	- complete value-free science is not possible
	- postmodernists are more skeptical of "scientific" truth claims that are so dependent on the meanings we assign to the concepts we employ
		- some postmodernists see knowledge in entirely relativist terms (extreme)
	- critical theorists don't abandon science, but try to expose ideological claims masquerading as theories with scientific bases of support
		- false pretenses used to legitimate self-servicing practices
		- search for ways and means by which the powerful attempt to legitimate their exploitative positions of dominance
			- their self-serving manipulation of ideas/meanings in theories others are led to believe have scientific underpinnings
