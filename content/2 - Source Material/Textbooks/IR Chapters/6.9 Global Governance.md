#### 6.9.0 Global Governance
- [[global governance]]
	- increasing complexity of issues on global agenda
	- [[neoliberal institutionalism]]
	- not a new concept
	- [[Kant]]'s proposal of decentralized, self-enforcing peace w/o world gov't
	- involves processes and institutions (formal and informal) that guide and restrain collective activities of groups
	- [[Keohane]]: "partially globalized world" as one with "thick networks of [[interdependence]] in which boundaries and states nevertheless matter a great deal"
		- increasing [[interdependence]] in partially globalized world
		- [[Keohane]] sees greater institutionalization as world becomes more like a polity with governance
			- essential to trade, finance, environment, security, other matters of global import
- [[Keohane]]: [[global governance]] isn't the same as world gov't (a new, unitary superstate)
	- [[global governance]] is a design that integrates "networks among agents and norms – standards of expected behavior – that are widely accepted among agents"
		- devising better, more effective global institutions to serve the needs of humankind is imperative
	- "the challenge for [[America]]n political science resembles that of founders of [[America]]: how to design institutions for a polity of unprecedented size and diversity"
	- "increased [[interdependence]] among human beings produces discord, since self-regarding actions affect the welfare of others"
	- concerned that institutional approaches to problems may not always be benign
		- institutional approaches "can foster exploitation or even oppression"
	- "stakes in the mission" of establishing the ways and means of [[Globalization]] are high
		- "if global institutions are designed well, they'll promote human welfare, but if we bungle the job, the results could be disastrous"
	- to avoid adverse outcomes, must draw insights "from a variety of perspectives, including [[Game Theory]], study of political culture, and work on role that ideas play in politics"
		- to learn "how important beliefs are in reaching equilibrium solutions and how institutionalized beliefs structure situations of political choice"
	- "from traditional political theory we are reminded of the importance of [[normative]] beliefs for the practice of politics... from historical institutionalism and political sociology we understand how values and norms operate in society... and from [[democratic theory]] we discover the crucial roles of accountability, participation, and especially persuasion in creating legitimate political institutions"
- most issues on [[global governance]] agenda can't be managed unilaterally even if states wanted to
	- global economy, health, environment, human rights, human security
	- developing consensus on what needs to be done is hard
		- finding resources that can be allocated to these matters is also hard
	- understanding cause-effect relations associated with different options under consideration
- concern over the past 2 decades regarding [[global governance]]
	- noticeable slowdown
	- growing "governance deficit" in progress towards [[global governance]] in many issue areas
	- shift from examining how interest alignment among states in multilateral settings can be aligned to re-imaging [[global governance]] as a more explicit realm of disputes and confrontations
