#### 13.6.0 Alternative Images and Foreign Policy Choice
- relative emphasis placed on order, justice, freedom, change varies in different images
	- values that are part of foreign policy-making and have direct bearing on int'l politics
	- [[Realism]]
		- concern with [[power]] and [[balance of power]]
		- related to value commitments of those in authority who see order as essential to nat'l [[security]]
		- managing conflict and maximizing accomplishment of state objectives to further avoid war, however constrained by states comprising the balance
		- nat'l security/interest justifies state policy
			- influenced by [[Machiavelli]]
	- [[The English School]]
		- importance of int'l order
		- where order comes from in [[anarchy]] society
- value bias
	- [[Liberalism]] and [[The English School]]
		- change should be evolutionary and incremental
		- if change is willed, then reformist measures are the appropriate ones (not revolutionary)
		- fragmentation of states and proliferation of actors
			- focus on formation of coalitions/counter-coalitions
			- not an environment for radical changes that require greater societal unity/strong unitary leadership
			- too many obstacles to make change easy to come by
		- emphasis on democratic notions of human rights and justice for individuals, groups, societies than order within states
			- Richard [[Falk]] (1930–), 4 values to be maximized in [[World Order Models Project]]
				1. minimization of collective violence
					- order is important
					- focus on social and welfare issues that need to be addressed as part of the world order
					- "a transnational social movement dedicated to global reform"
				2. maximization of economic well-being
				3. maximization of social and political justice
				4. maximization of ecological quality
- justice (distributive justice)
	- [[Liberalism]], [[Economic Structuralism]]
		- John [[Rawls]]
			- non-Marxist formulation: supports [[normative]] preference focusing on patterns of human [[exploitation]] and inequalities in the distribution of wealth between [[First World]] and [[Third World]]
		- Marxist economic structuralists, [[postcolonialism]] scholars
			- long-standing moral concern with exploitative class relations
			- overturning what they see as existing, unjust world order
		- reformism and incremental change
			- maintaining status quo dominance by owners and managers of capital (OMC)
				- if justice is to be served, there may need to be [[revolution]]
					- sweeps out unjust world order and replaces it with equitable distribution of wealth and resources
				- justice requires change
					- order, peace, individual freedom will be established after fundamental/revolutionary change of existing order
