#### 6.3.0 International Organization
- [[post-WWI]]
	- [[League of Nations]] created
		- effort to maintain peace drew upon [[Europe]]'s diplomatic experience
- post-[[Napoleonic period]]
	- [[Concert of Europe]]
		- reflected [[Liberalism]] ideas found in [[Kant]] and others
- [[Wilson]]'s influence of [[Liberalism]]
	- negotiators moved away from alliances, power, and [[balance of power]]
		- and secret agreements among them that might've triggered [[WWI]]
	- these were causes of [[War]] than mechanisms for maintaining peace
	- [[collective security]] based on rule of law
		- aggression prohibited
		- war declared illegal
		- law-abiding states come together as collective law enforcers against any state committing aggression
- [[Realism]]'s critique of [[collective security]]
	- such [[Idealism]] posed no effective obstacles in interwar years ([[post-WWI]]) to stop aggression by [[Germany]], [[Italy]], [[Japan]], and other Axis powers
	- root of [[Realism]]-[[Idealism]] debate
		- failed experience with [[collective security]] through rule of law
			- continued [[Post-WWII]]
- new [[United Nations]] retained [[collective security]]
	- augmented it with [[collective defense]]
		- euphemism for bringing alliances back to supplement a sovereign state's right to self-defense under int'l law
	- in practice: [[Liberalism]] and [[Realism]] conceptions come together in multilateral security mechanism constituted under [[United Nations]]
	- [[Liberalism]] became embedded in wide variety of [[United Nations]]-affiliated and other int'l orgs 
		- World Bank, International Monetary Fund ([[IMF]]), General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade, World Trade Organization, Int'l Telecommunications Union, Int'l Civil Aviation and Maritime Organizations, World Health Organization([[WHO]]), Food and Agricultural Organization, International Labor Organization, UN High Commission on Human Rights, etc.
- present-day story of [[Liberalism]] and [[IR]]
	- begins in [[Europe]] [[Post-WWII]], incoming [[Cold War]]
		- [[embedded liberalism]] in int'l orgs: political-economic compromise that balanced open int'l markets with domestic state intervention to secure welfare and full employment
		- [[Realism]] secures place in [[IR]]
	- focus on crises in [[Germany]] (Berlin, East-West tensions)
		- [[Europe]] test bed for [[Liberalism]] theories
	- economic rebuilding of [[Western Europe]]
		- humanitarian and political priority
		- fear: failure to rebuild [[Europe]] would make [[Communism]] subversion and political unrest more likely (particularly in [[Germany]])
		- hope: a rebuilt [[Europe]] would eventually tie together states ([[France]], [[Germany]]) into web of [[interdependence]] to reduce likelihood of another devastating war
	- [[Liberalism]] theory: increased economic ties would play major role in [[regional integration]]
