#### 1.1.0 Epistemology, Methodology, and Ontology
- **[[Epistemology]]:** the ways and means by which we come to know something about the world.
	- e.g. **[[Empiricism]]:** the view that the only grounds for making truth claims is through direct observation of the world using our senses.
- **[[Positivism]]:** consists of four underlying implicit assumptions/beliefs
	1. The unity of the natural and social sciences – we can study society as we study the natural world.
	2. We can draw a distinction between facts and values.
	3. Regularities exist in the social as well as the natural world and they can be identified.
	4. Empirical validation or falsification is the hallmark of "real" inquiry.
- Positivism endorses the use of formal hypothesis testing or causal modeling as methodologies
	- Focus on what's observable, empirical and measurable
- **[[Methodologies]]:** modes of research and analysis or a set of rules for the actual practice of investigating IR.
	- **[[Quantitative]]:** statistics and mathematical equations
	- **[[Qualitative]]:** in-depth case and comparative case studies
- **[[Ontology]]:** how each of us views the world, how we see or understand the essence of things around us (*worldview*).
	- Are there actual structures out there that influence the behavior of actors?
		- e.g. concept of an "international system"
			- Is it an actual material, physical, tangible structure consisting of capabilities (weapons, troops, economic resources)?
			- Is it internationally shared ideas, beliefs, and norms?
			- Is what we observe caused, facilitated, or impeded by these material or ideational structures (distribution of power or cultures) external to the actors or within which they're immersed?
	- **Ernst B. Haas:** his work was influenced by an ontological orientation that "avoided fixed dogmas and unchanging universal values" and "highlighted human agency over other causal forces"
	- **James N. Rosenau:** some of us are ontologically more prone to engage in theorizing than others.
		- Ontological predisposition: one's being "able to assume that human affairs are founded on an underlying order"
		- Eight "preconditions for creative theorizing" one:
			1. has to avoid treating the task as that of formulating an appropriate definition of theory
			2. has to be clear as to whether one aspires to empirical theory or value theory
			3. must be predisposed to ask about every event, every situation, or every observed phenomenon, "of what instance?"
			4. must be ready to appreciate and accept the need to sacrificed detailed description for broad observations
			5. must be tolerant of ambiguity, concerned about probabilities, and distrustful of absolutes
			6. must be playful about international phenomena having a creative imagination, the habit of specifying unlikely conditions and analyzing them as *if* they prevailed
			7. must be genuinely puzzled by international phenomena
			8. must be constantly ready to be proven wrong
	- **Kenneth N. Waltz:** world of competition among self-oriented states as principal actors with different interests and capabilities or power they bring to bear in the pursuit of these interests.
		- realist/darker view of reality; a dimmer view of humans and their potential than liberals hold
		- Traditional thought in Thucydides, Machiavelli, Hobbes, James Madison, Hamilton
		- **Depravity:** describes the human condition/natural state that humans find themselves in
		- Liberals viewed as misinformed by taking the ontological view that harmony is the natural condition for humans and dismissing dissension and strife as arising from "mistaken belief, inadequate knowledge, and defective governance"
	- **[[Economic structuralists]]:** exploitation and victimization describe the human condition.
		- similar to realists, dim view of reality
		- **Dialectical materialism:** rooted in [[Marxism]], materialist ontology; the material world is objective real, exists independently of consciousness, and is in a constant state of change driven by internal, contradictory forces (e.g. class struggle)
- **[[1.4 Images]]:** general perspectives on international relations and world politics that consist of certain assumptions about key actors and processes that influence our theorizing.
	- Fine line between
		- How we understand the essence of things (condition/nature of humans, the degree to which humans as agents matter)
		- Images we have of international/world politics
	- Ontologies and images are not the same, but are related
