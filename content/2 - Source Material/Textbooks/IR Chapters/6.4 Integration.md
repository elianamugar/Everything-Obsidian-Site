#### 6.4.0 Integration
- David [[Mitrany]]
	- [[integration]]
		- broke away from traditional [[Liberalism]] of merely describing int'l orgs and encouraging expansion and application of int'l law
		- developed theory how collaborative behavior among states could be achieved
	- [[functionalism]] theory
		- [[Mitrany]]: modern society faces a myriad of technical problems that can be resolved by experts rather than politicians
		- nonpolitical problems (economic, social, scientific) should be assigned to non-political experts from affected countries for resolution
		- successful collaboration in one technical field will lead to further collaboration in other fields
		- gov'ts would recognize common benefits to gain from cooperation
		- a way to sneak up on the authority of sovereign state
			- as states become increasingly integrated due to expansion of collaboration in technical areas in which all parties made *absolute gains*, the cost of breaking these functional ties would be great and hence give leaders reason to pause before doing so
- what led [[Mitrany]] to develop [[integration]] and [[functionalism]] theories
	- 1952: successful creation of European Coal and Steel Community ([[ECSC]])
	- 1956: formation of European Economic Community ([[EEC]]) in [[Treaty of Rome]]
		- eventual political integration of [[Western Europe]]
		- initial successes in economic integration
			- under which conditions is integration among states possible?
				- what was occurring in [[Western Europe]] did not match [[Hobbes]]'s image of states constantly prepared to go to war
					- [[Hobbes]] image: little faith in the possibility of collaborative behavior among sovereign states
- Karl [[Deutsch]]
	- development of concept of [[security community]] in [[Europe]]
	- thesis: peace can be achieved through a shared sense of community resulting from the increase in communication and collaboration in economic and social activities
		- pacifying effect of [[interdependence]] across increasingly integrated [[Europe]]
			- reflected [[Kant]] and influenced logic of modern [[democratic peace theory]]
- Ernst [[Haas]]
	- most prominent theorist of [[regional integration]]
	- [[neofunctionalism]]
		- [[Mitrany]]'s [[functionalism]] logic of technical tasks driving creation of int'l orgs discounted the importance of politics
		- [[neofunctionalism]] puts politics in center stage
			- acknowledges how integral politics is to [[integration]] processes
		- neofunctionalists part from Mitrany, rejecting notion that one can separate technical tasks from politics
		- [[Haas]]'s argument: for [[integration]] to occur, it must be perceived by politically connected elites to be in their interest to pursue such aims
	- [[Haas]] as an early [[constructivism]] scholar
		- ideas grounded in interests of actors as driving forces in politics
		- assigning of tasks to an int'l org will be attained and sustained only if actors believe their interests are best served by making a political commitment ton constructing and maintaining such institutions
	- application of [[Game Theory]] to [[neofunctionalism]]
		- politics can produce a [[variable-sum]] or [[positive-sum]] outcome for all actors
		- perspective of [[Rousseau]]'s stag hunt fable ([[5.4 System]])
			- collaborative behavior *is* possible and in the enlightened self-interest of states
			- rather than compete in ongoing [[zero-sum]] contests, optimizing short-term self-interest at the expense of others, they can upgrade service of their common and long-term interests through cooperative and collaborative efforts reflect in int'l institutions
	- application of [[neofunctionalism]]'s internal logic
		- [[Europe]]an case: how increased [[integration]] in particular economic sectors would "spill over" into other related sectors
			- process carried by politically connected elites seeing positive gains to be found in increased collaboration as states became increasingly integrated economically
		- 1958: 6 [[Europe]]an states ([[Germany]], [[France]], [[Italy]], [[Belgium]], [[Netherlands]], [[Luxembourg]]) agree to move beyond [[ECSC]] and establish atomic energy agency ([[EURATOM]]) and [[EEC]] (full customs union finally achieved by 1967)
		- 1992: [[EEC]] countries rename themselves [[EU]] and set sights on a full economic and monetary union
			- critics's observation: [[Europe]]an [[integration]] allowed members to pick and choose the level of [[integration]] that suited their fancy
				- problem has grown with expansion of [[EU]] as it incorporated [[Europe]]an states of great diversity in levels of economic development
			- critics of "widening" membership noted that doing so would make the "deepening" of [[integration]] even more difficult
- [[integration]] as a robust research program has faded
	- focus now: resurgence of populist [[nationalism]] in [[Europe]] due to refugee crises and economic difficulties and whether the [[EU]] experiment can survive
	- [[Haas]]'s conclusion: [[regional integration]] theories should be subordinate to a broader theory of [[interdependence]]
		- [[interdependence]] theory didn't anticipate a transfer of state sovereignty to regional orgs
- [[neofunctionalism]] and [[integration]] research findings
	- unresolved [[agent-structure]] debates that are still prominent in [[IR]] ([[constructivism]])
	- how ideas relate to material considerations in the minds of agents
	- how material structures enable/constrain these agents
	- [[Haas]] holds position that agency matters
		- individuals aren't captives of system structure and can influence course of events
	- changing knowledge can lead to redefining interests
	- orgs composed of thinking people can adapt, learn, innovate in changing circumstances
- James [[Rosenau]] (1924 – 2011)
	- work focused on important roles played by state and non-state actors in world politics
	- identified turbulence as a substantial challenge (like [[Haas]])
	- his [[Ontology]]
		- seeing two competing "worlds"
			- first world: state-centric
			- second world: multi-centric, involving diverse state and non-state actors
	- [[agent-structure]] issue
		- reflected in [[Rosenau]]'s distinction he drew between parameters at micro-level (individual) and macro-level (system structure) and the "relational one" that tries to put micro and macro together
- change propelled by 
	- dynamics of technology
	- emergence of complex issues
	- reduced capacity of states to deal effectively with many contemporary problems
	- emergence of "[[subgroupism]]" and individuals who are analytically ever more capable and diverse in orientation
- agents matter to [[Rosenau]] and [[Haas]]
