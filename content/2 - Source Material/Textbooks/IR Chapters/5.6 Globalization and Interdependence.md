#### 5.6.0 Globalization and Interdependence
- realists doesn't have enthusiasm for *[[Globalization]]*
	- problem of definition
		- [[Liberalism]] emphasizes the continual increase in transnational and worldwide economic, social, cultural interactions among societies that transcend boundaries of states
			- aided by advances in technology
	- term is descriptive and lacking in theoretical content
		- hardly qualifies as a "concept" suitable for theory building
	- term is trendy, which makes realists suspicious (personal note: LOL)
		- rare for academic theoretical concepts to gain widespread publicity
	- [[Globalization]] assumes increase in transactions among societies leads to erosion of sovereignty and blurs boundaries between state and int'l system
		- [[Realism]]: [[anarchy]] is distinguishing feature in [[IR]]
			- anything that questions separation of domestic and int'l politics threatens the centrality of this key [[Realism]] concept
- [[Globalization]] has an affinity with [[interdependence]]
	- popular concept rising in the 1970s
		- part of why realists were skeptical about its conceptual utility
	- some realists have used the concept
		- [[interdependence]]: viewed as being between or among states
			- [[balance of power]] can be a kind of [[interdependence]]
			- for any one state, [[interdependence]] among states is not necessarily good
				- a dominance-dependence relation, not a symmetric relation between coequal parties
					- dependent party is vulnerable to choices of dominant party
					- vulnerability interdependence is source of power for one state over another
					- to reduce vulnerability, state should be independent/minimize dependency on other states
			- [[interdependence]] doesn't affect all states equally
				- economic and military realms
					- e.g. although economies of most oil-importing countries are affected by oil prices rising, they're not equally vulnerable
				- vulnerability is part of what alternatives are available
					- e.g. [[America]] increased domestic production, created strategic oil reserve to be drawn from only in emergencies, found other foreign sources of oil, substitute alternative forms of energy (natural gas)
						- reduced vulnerability to any new oil embargo/disruption of supply due to war/other regional instabilities in [[Middle East]]/elsewhere
					- [[Europe]] working to reduce reliance on [[Russia]]'s energy following invasion of [[Ukraine]] in 2022
				- if a state wants to be more powerful, it avoids/minimizes economic/political/military dependency on other states
					- if this were to amount to a reduction in its relative power position
				- dependency on others is to be minimized
				- dependency *of* others may be desirable if it increases leverage over other states
				- not all states are equally vulnerable
					- [[Realism]] suspicion of "given increasing globalization, the entire world is increasing interdependent/interconnected"
			- [[interdependence]] may or may not enhance prospects for peace
				- conflict, not cooperation, could easily result
				- one way to establish peace: eliminate/minimize contact among opponents/potential adversaries
					- separation from other units (if possible) would mean less contact –> less conflict
				- realists do not agree that increase in globalization has a pacifying effect anymore than the assumption [[interdependence]] leads to peace
		- [[interdependence]]: involving non-state actors (e.g. multinational corporations)
		- core of realist thought: image of interactions among states
