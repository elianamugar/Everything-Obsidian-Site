#### 7.2.0 Intellectual Precursors and Influences
- writers that impacted [[Economic Structuralism]]
	- Karl [[Marx]] (1818 – 1883)
		- disclaimer: not all [[Economic structuralists]] are Marxists
		- influenced methods of analysis and certain critical insights into functioning, development, expansion of [[Capitalism]]'s *mode of production*
		- focused on unequal and exploitative relations
			- sets important context for scholarship by [[Economic Structuralism]]
		- 3 influences on [[Economic Structuralism]]
			1. concerned with exploitation of the many by the few
				- patterns and mechanisms of exploitation in different mods of economic production
				- recognized historically progressive role played by capitalists
					- [[proletarian revolution]] wouldn't have been possible until after establishment of [[Capitalism]]'s system
				- personal sympathies were with downtrodden alienated form means of production
			2. [[Capitalism]] exhibits certain law-like qualities in terms of its development and expansion
				- views [[Capitalism]] as part of a world-historical process unfolding through [[dialectical reasoning]], an economic system riddled with clashing contradictions/internal tensions that can only be resolved by a [[revolution]]ary transformation into a [[Socialism]] mode of production
				- recognizes important role of human agency in moving history forward
					- historical economic and social realities are paramount in explaining outcomes
				- context of [[agent-structure]]
					- [[Marx]]'s argument: "Men make their own history, but they do not make it just as they please; they do not make it under circumstances chosen by themselves, but under circumstances directly found, given, and transmitted from the past. The tradition of all dead generations weights heavily like a nightmare on the brain of the living." #quote 
				- interested in interplay/dialectic between agents and structures
					- interest in the process by which structures were historically constructed
			3. society must be studied in its totality, not piecemeal
				- analyst must be aware of how various parts of society were interrelated (even unobvious aspects to casual observers)
				- Robert L. [[Heilbroner]]: "The entire contribution of [[Marxism]] to social thought rests ultimately on its effort to penetrate the veil of appearances to discover the hidden essence of things, the web of relations that is the real ground of reality and not the surface manifestations that are its facade." #quote 
				- [[Marx]] is an intellectual precursor of present-day critical theorists
				- some [[Economic structuralists]] early in their careers had little use for history and preoccupied with state or individual units of analysis
					- "If there is one thing which distinguishes a world-system perspective from any other, it is its insistence that the unit of analysis is a world-system defined in terms of economic processes and links, and not any units defined in terms of juridical, political, cultural, geographical, or other criteria." #quote 
				- how [[Economic Structuralism]] views state level of analysis
					- the state derives its significance from the role it plays in actively aiding/hindering [[Capitalism]]'s accumulation process
						- not viewed in terms of its sovereign entity preoccupied with security concerns
					- any one state is viewed in terms of how it fits into the overall [[capitalist world-system]]
						- not viewed in isolation
		- emphasis on 
			- [[exploitation]]
			- discernible historical patterns of [[Capitalism]]'s development and expansion
			- methodology of [[historical materialism]]
			- how change occurs
			- importance of understanding the "big picture", then asking how individual parts fit into the whole
		- [[Capitalism]] as a worldwide mode of production
		- observations on [[Capitalism]] in 19th c. applied to various theories of [[imperialism]]
			- [[imperialism]] assumes an int'l, hierarchical division of labor between rich and poor regions of the world
				- relation isn't mutually beneficial *comparative advantage*
				- relation is [[exploitation]]
	- John A. [[Hobson]] (1858 – 1940)
		- significant theorist of [[imperialism]]
		- [[Capitalism]] societies are faced with 3 basic interrelated problems:
			- overproduction of goods
			- underconsumption of these goods by workers and other classes
			- over-savings on the part of capitalists
		- as capitalist owners of industry continued to exploit workers and pay the lowest possible wages, profits mounted and goods began to pile up
			- what could capitalists have done with excess goods and profits?
			- how could capitalists resolve the underconsumption problem?
				- redistribute wealth?
					- highly unlikely
			- because [[Capitalism]] in [[Europe]] and [[North America]] powers were experiencing overproduction and domestic underconsumption, investment opportunities in developed countries remained limited
		- solution by capitalists: invest in [[Third World]] countries (during [[Cold War]])
			- result: [[imperialism]]
				- "the endeavor of the great controllers of industry to broaden the channel for the flow of their surplus wealth by seeking foreign markets and foreign investments to take off the goods and capital they cannot sell or use at home." #quote 
			- [[Hobson]]'s argument against "supposed inevitability of imperial expansion"
				- it's "not inherent in the nature of things that we should spend our natural resources on militarism, [[War]], and risky, unscrupulous diplomacy, in order to find markets for our goods and surplus capital"
			- [[Hobson]] rejects [[determinism]] found in [[Marxism]] scholar works of [[imperialism]]
	- Vladimir Ilyich [[Lenin]] (1870 – 1924)
		- [[Imperialism – The Highest Stage of Capitalism (1916) by Vladimir Ilyich Lenin]]
			- most important theoretical work of [[Economic Structuralism]] interest
			- written during [[WWI]]
			- develops theory that explains the necessity for [[Capitalism]]'s [[exploitation]] of lesser-developed countries and causes of [[War]] among advanced capitalist states
			- draws on [[Hobson]] and Rudolf [[Hilferding]] (1877 – 1941, [[Germany]] [[social democracy]])
				- from [[Hobson]]
					- [[Lenin]] accepts key argument that underconsumption and overproduction cause capitalists to scramble for foreign markets beyond [[Europe]] and to engage in [[Colonialism]]
				- from [[Hilferding]]
					- [[Lenin]] takes notion that [[imperialism]] policies reflect the existence of monopoly and finance capital (highest stage of [[Capitalism]])
						- [[Capitalism]] had developed such that [[oligopoly]] and [[monopoly]] controlled the key sectors of the economy, taking over smaller firms and milking domestic markets dry
						- result: need to look elsewhere for investment opportunities
							- logically entails creating overseas markets
						- as markets expand, they require more economic inputs (e.g. raw materials)
							- encourages further spread of [[imperialism]] to secure such resources
			- by buying off [[Europe]]'s working class in the short term through higher wages, [[imperialism]] delayed inevitable domestic [[revolution]]
				- important trade-off: domestic stability was achieved at the cost of wars among capitalist powers
					- results from continual struggle for overseas markets
				- once globe was divided up, further [[expansionism]] would come only at the expense of a capitalist rival
		- [[Lenin]]'s influence on [[Economic Structuralism]]
			- emphasis on global nature of [[Capitalism]] and its inherent [[exploitation]] that primarily benefits the [[bourgeoisie]] in [[First World]] countries at the expense of [[Third World]] countries
			- [[determinism]] in his theory of [[imperialism]]
				- but reflects considerable [[voluntarism]] in practice
			- agency matters
				- his call for a "vanguard of the proletariat" led by a [[Communism]] party to push history down its revolutionary past
					- developed in theory AND in practice
				- actions of [[revolution]]aries are to serve as catalysts to the worldwide [[proletarian revolution]] whenever the objective conditions of working-class [[exploitation]] are ripe/have reached their revolutionary stage
					- later writers argue that *revolutionaries* play more of a role than merely *catalysts*
						- [[Che Guevara]] (1928 – 1967)
						- [[Mao Zedong]] (1893 – 1976)
					- *revolutionaries* can create conditions for [[revolution]]
						- the "subjective" can create the "objective"
						- agency matters
