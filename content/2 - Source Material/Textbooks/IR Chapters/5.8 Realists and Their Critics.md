#### 5.8.1 Realism: The Term Itself
- an image of int'l politics represented as close to the reality of how things are
	- not how things ought to be
- competing images
	- in debate and discourse, labels are important
	- interwar period of [[WWI]] and [[WWII]]
		- realists challenged by advocates of [[League of Nations]], world [[federalism]], peace through int'l law
			- [[Idealism]]/[[utopian]]
				- conjures images of impracticality, unsophisticated peace, and utopian schemes
- [[Realism]] should be taken at face value
	- basic tenets: closely approximate the world as it is
- [[Realism]]'s strong policy-prescriptive components
	- [[Machiavelli]]'s [[The Prince by Niccolo Machiavelli]]
		- expressly presented as a guide for the ruler
	- [[America]] political scientists in national security advisor positions in [[White House]] are realists
		- [[Nixon]], [[Gerald Ford]]: [[Kissinger]]
		- [[Gerald Ford]], [[H.W. Bush]]: Brent [[Scowcroft]] 
		- [[Jimmy Carter]]: Zbigniew [[Brzezinski]]
		- [[Bush]]: [[Condoleeza Rice]] 
		- [[Obama]]: Thomas [[Donilon]]
		- [[Trump]]: [[John Bolton]] ([[neoconservatism]])
		- [[Biden]]: [[Jake Sullivan]]
- critique on [[Realism]]
	- argument: realists help perpetuate the very world they analyze
		- describes the world in terms of violence, duplicity, [[War]]
		- provides advice to policy makers on how they *should* act
		- realists justifying one particular conception of [[IR]]
		- [[Realism]] becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy
			- stronger theoretical foundations ([[structural realism]], [[neorealism]]) that favor explanation over policy prescription still have same effect
	- argument: realists suffer from a lack of imagination
	- argument: realists suffer an inability to consider alternative conceptions of world politics and how these might be achieved
		- realist rebuttal: nothing inherently wrong with being policy-relevant and helping leaders navigate through dangerous waters
		- realist rebuttal: advice based on wishful thinking could lead to disastrous policies, especially [[Idealism]] lone leader in world of realists
		- realist rebuttal: criticisms are based on selective reading of realists, ignoring genuine concern with causes of [[War]], but also how peace and stability can be achieved/maintained
- realists who have cautioned against ill-advised foreign policies
	- [[Kenneth Waltz]]: against [[America]] involvement in [[Vietnam War]]
	- George [[Kennan]]: expansion of [[NATO]] eastward [[post-Cold War]] would needlessly provoke [[Russia]]
- not all realists claim to be interested in providing advice to statesmen
	- would rather use [[Realism]] assumptions/insights to better develop theories of int'l politics
#### 5.8.2 The System and Determinism
- key concept to [[Realism]]: concept of **system**
	- its impact on int'l actors
	- system has a life of its own
		- independent of wishes and actions of states, even though it's a result of the preference and powers of the constituent states
		- statesmen granted too little autonomy
			- constraints and few opportunities
		- decision-making devoid of human volition
		- [[determinism]] and [[Pessimism]]
- disagreement in [[Realism]] as to what extent the system functions as an independent variable influencing state behavior
	- [[structural realism]]/[[neorealism]]: system represents material structure that *does* influence behavior of states that are part of the system
		- reject that they're structural determinists who ignore actors operating at the unit/state level of analysis
	- [[defensive realism]]: unit/non-system variables to explain instances where states don't act with a system-structure perspective
	- [[classical realism]]: distinguishes imperialist, revolutionary, revisionist states vs. status quo powers interested in maintaining their own positions in relatively constant regional/global order
		- [[Wolfers]], [[Morgenthau]], [[Kissinger]]
	- [[neoclassical realism]]: incorporates int'l institutions and explanatory factors at state-society level of analysis
		- appreciates insights of [[neorealism]]
		- Randall [[Schweller]]
	- others
		- examine relations among states that analytically fall between level of system structure and level of state and society
			- arms racing and arms control
			- alliance behavior ([[balancing]], [[bandwagoning]])
		- factors will affect stability of [[bipolar distribution of power]] or [[multipolar]] systems and the possibility of moving towards more peaceful world
- realists differ on extent to which policy makers impose themselves on events or vice versa
	- no realist is completely [[determinism]] or [[voluntarism]]
	- no realist exclusively emphasizes structures or agents/human actors
#### 5.8.3 Realists and the State
- key concept to [[Realism]]: the **state**
- criticism of [[Realism]]: too obsessed with state, ignoring other actors and issues not directly related to maintenance of state security
	- non-state actors: multinational corporations, banks, terrorists, int'l orgs
		- excluded, downplayed, trivialized in [[Realism]]
	- concerns of global environment, socioeconomic gap, implications of [[Globalization]] don't matter in [[Realism]]
		- b/c of [[Realism]]'s national security prism
- [[Realism]]'s rebuttal: non-state actors aren't dealt with in-depth, but they're not completely irrelevant
	- a theory concerned with explaining state behavior naturally focuses on states, not terrorist groups/multinational corporations
	- theories purposely limit type of actors analyzed
		- theories constructed to answer certain questions and explain certain types of int'l behavior/outcomes
- [[Realism]]'s rebuttal: deteriorating environment is critical global concern with implications for national security goals of all states
	- solving these problems have to be done by states taking lead
- [[Realism]]'s argument: focusing on state is justified on [[normative]] grounds
	- concern with how unbridled arms races and military spending contributes to int'l tension
		- exclusively states spend money to buy/produce military hardware
			- focus on states as unit of analysis
	- realists critical of political entities that are deemed too important to be ignored
#### 5.8.4 Realists and the Balance of Power
- key concept to [[Realism]]: [[balance of power]]
- criticisms of [[balance of power]]
	- definitional confusion
		- [[Morgenthau]]'s 4 definitions:
			1. a policy aimed at a certain state of affairs
			2. an objective or actual state of affairs
			3. an approximately equal distribution of power
				- as when [[balance of power]] existed between [[America]] and [[USSR]]
			4. any distribution of power including a predominance of power
				- as when [[balance of power]] shifts in favor of either superpower
		- Ernst B. [[Haas]]'s 7 definitions:
			1. distribution of power
			2. equilibrium
			3. [[hegemony]]
			4. stability and peace
			5. instability and [[War]]
			6. power politics generally
			7. a universal law of history
	- leads to war rather than preventing it
		- serves as poor guide for policy makers
		- functions as propaganda tool to justify defense spending and foreign adventures
- attempts to clear up misconceptions and misinterpretations of [[balance of power]]
	- [[Kenneth Waltz]]
#### 5.8.5 Realism and Change
- critics of [[Realism]] suggest there's little possibility left for fundamental and peaceful transformation of int'l politics
- realists claim critics best offer analysis aimed at understanding how int'l stability is achieved, but nothing approaching true peace
- realist stability reflects world with weapons, forever on verge of [[War]]
- alternative world futures representing real alternative to dismal [[Hobbes]]'s world are rarely discussed/taken seriously
	- timelessness to repetitious nature and cycles of [[war]]
		- strong do as they will and the weak do as they must
- critics of [[Realism]]: realists assume state interests, but tell little of how states come to define their interests
	- [[constructivism]]: int'l [[anarchy]] is what states make of it
		- interests constructed by state
		- Alexander [[Wendt]]
- issue of change connected with [[determinism]]
	- [[agent-structure]] problem
- power politics and state are central to all realist analyses
	- fundamental change is still possible
	- change isn't limited to [[War]] and the cyclical rise and fall of states
		- strength of these views depends on the author under consideration
#### 5.8.6 Realism: The Entire Enterprise
- critics of [[Realism]] have a hard time criticizing [[Realism]] because it has been unscathed by years of criticism
	- [[Realism]]'s affinity to real world policy-making makes [[Realism]] attractive and legitimate
		- represents the world out there, not ivory tower perspective on human events
		- [[Realism]] is the accepted wisdom of [[Western]] AND outside northern hemisphere establishments
			- concern over the survival of their regimes and states
		- [[Realism]] is attractive to academia
			- "peace studies" programs –> "security and conflict studies"
	- [[Realism]] has an increasingly scientific face
		- [[classical realism]] work claimed as insight without evidence
		- [[defensive realism]] and [[offensive realism]] cast insights through hypotheses, testable quantitatively/non-quantitative indicators
		- work is better grounded scientifically
			- placed within context of [[Positivism]] view of how we comprehend reality
				- positivist approach to knowledge remains prominent in social and natural sciences
- realists see positivist orientation as a virtue
	- [[postmodernism]] and others see [[Positivism]] as erroneous
		- what is this "knowable reality" of [[IR]] that [[Realism]] claims to be true?
			- underlying issues of [[Ontology]] 
				- how theorists view the essence of politics
				- nature of actors (states) and how they're prone to act
			- underlying issues of [[Epistemology]]
				- verification of knowledge claims
					- how we know what we think we know
			- underlying issues of [[Methodologies]] 
				- modes of research and analysis
		- is reality simply "out there" waiting to be discovered?
		- is reality constructed by discourse?
		- is realism best viewed as simply another perspective/construction of how the world works?
- reminders on criticism of images
	- it's not difficult to find fault with work of individual theorists, then blanket an entire approach for the supposed "sins" of an individual author
	- this chapter illustrates: although realists may find common ground in terms of basic assumptions and key int'l actors, there are differences between [[classical realism]]/[[neoclassical realism]] and [[structural realism]], and [[offensive realism]] and [[defensive realism]]
		- realists of any subcategory may differ in several important respects (like methods used, levels of analysis chosen, and what's assumed about ability of decision makers to influence int'l outcomes)
	- important to refer to original sources
