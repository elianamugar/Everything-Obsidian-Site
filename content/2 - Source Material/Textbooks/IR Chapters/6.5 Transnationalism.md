#### 6.5.0 Transnationalism
- 1970s: [[Transnationalism]]
	- attention to increasing role of MNCs abroad and challenge they pose to sovereign prerogatives of states
	- interest in [[political economy]] increased
- 1971: [[America]] unilaterally goes off the gold-exchange standard of $35/oz and and allows dollar's value to float
	- US Treasury maintained that gold-exchange standard since 1934
	- turmoil in int'l currency markets
- Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries ([[OPEC]]) raise case of oil by regulating its supply to global markets
	- economics becomes "high politics" rather than "low politics"
- security matters changing
	- [[zero-sum]] calculations and *relative gains* prominent in [[Realism]] were increased by [[positive-sum]] understandings common in [[Liberalism]]
	- 1960s: [[America]]-[[USSR]] relations in [[Cold War]] produced [[détente]], a relaxation of tensions between two superpowers
		- climate conducive to arms control
			- intended as [[balance of power]] politics regarding [[USSR]]
		- [[America]] interacts with [[China]], a process leading to normalization of relations between [[Cold War]] adversaries
	- [[power-based realism]]
		- the strong do what they will and the weak do what they must
		- difficulty accounting for why a superpower like [[America]] would get bogged down and lose in the [[Vietnam War]]
	- 1975: [[America]] forced to evacuate its remaining forces
- new developments in [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]] in [[IR]]
	- 1971, 1972: question of conceptual adequacy of [[Realism]]'s approach to [[IR]]
		- [[John W. Burton]]
			- "cobweb" model of multiple state and non-state actors better captured nature of current reality than [[Realism]]'s "billiard ball" model of states merely interacting with one another
			- idea of a "world society" is descriptively more accurate than the concept of [[IR]]
		- Robert O. [[Keohane]] and [[Joseph Nye Jr.]]
			- [[Transnational Relations and World Politics by Robert O. Keohane and Joseph Nye Jr.]]
				- attention to MNCs and other nongov'tal, transnational orgs, bureaucratic agencies, depts, other components of gov'ts that operate across state boundaries
					- these entities sometimes form transgovernmental coalitions/counter-coalitions with counterparts in other countries
- [[Liberalism]] theories in 1970s move to center stage in [[IR]]
	- [[Transnationalism]]: describes an actor (i.e. MNC) or pattern of behavior (i.e. MNCs act transnationally)
		- new focus on studying these actors, interactions, coalitions they form across state boundaries
		- involves nongov'tal actors
			- MNCs, banks, churches
			- human rights, environmental, terror/criminal networks
	- transgovernmental links at level of bureaucracies were new item on [[Liberalism]]'s agenda
		- challenges [[Realism]] claims of state as unitary actor
	- links/coalitions between nongov'tal orgs and transgovernmental actors became subject of some interest
- chipping away or leakage of state sovereignty?
	- states, assuming they survived as actors over long term, would no longer be fully independent
	- some scholars suggest that even if countries continue to exist, they may become heavily constrained by global connections and institutions, so much so that they'd no longer be fully independent
- problem with new transnational literature
	- most cases, work was highly descriptive, lacking theoretical content
	- [[Realism]] response: reasserting enduring importance of state and capabilities/power among states
		- [[Kenneth Waltz]]
			- [[Theory of International Politics (1979) by Kenneth N. Waltz]]
