#### 11.3.0 Strands of Feminism in International Relations
- strands of feminism
	- [[liberal feminism]]
		- emphasizes exclusion of women from important public spheres of social, political, economic life
		- strands of research
			1. exposing many areas of IR where women are underrepresented and to identify ways to overcome barriers to expanded participation
				- underrepresentation of women in [[security]] and arms control policymaking circles/int'l orgs
				- accepts the position of mainstream IR that the important subject of research is the *high politics* of military security
					- in line with [[Realism]]
				- success for women in IR becomes a practical matter of upping their numbers in diplomatic and security policymaking decisions
			2. uncovering ways in which women have actually been there
				- participants in and witnesses of major events, but their presence not reported
				- [[Bananas, Beaches, and Bases by Cynthia Enloe]]
					- [[Cynthia Enloe]]
						- some women are "protected" while other categories are exploited
							- protected: spouses, mothers, daughters, girlfriends, etc.
							- unprotected: non-Americans, racial minorities, prostitutes
								- provides necessary support services: security supplies, doing laundry, nursing
						- protection and exploitation are two manifestations of male dominance associated historically with militaries at home and abroad
				- masculinist behaviors associated with militarization of social life has adverse effects on women
					- not unique to [[America]], global
				- studies on role of women at home and their role in filling positions in factories vacated by conscripted men
				- examined important but unreported role of women in economic development of the [[Global South]]
		- feminism's issues with [[liberal feminism]]
			- underlying assumption that including more women in positions previously denied to them will eliminate gender inequalities
				- feminists from class/patriarchy class: inequalities define the very structures in which women might participate
					- participation alone won't alter this fundamental fact
	- subordination and domination of women by men is the most basic form of oppression
		- society's structure reinforces and maintains patriarchy
		- masculine perspectives emphasize conflict, dominates social sciences and IR theorizing
			- defining security in terms of aggregating power
		- feminist concept of security
			- includes empowerment of women, economic development, human rights, concern for global ecological commons
		- perspective of women as nurturers and more peace-oriented than men
			- some think genetic code in men who are aggressive and territorial
			- others reject biological [[determinism]]
				- young men raised in societies that educate them in martial values and devalue by comparison the work of women to include their running households and raising children
		- reject idea that IR is limited to *high politics*
			- rejection of distinction between public and private realms
			- danger is that peace-loving and nurturing female plays to stereotypes of those who would prefer to confine them to subordinate status and caution against the role of women in nat'l security policy-making positions
	- [[postmodernism]] feminism
		- displaces [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]] [[Positivism]] discourse and [[Epistemology]] with commitment to skepticism concerning truth claims about IR
		- emergence of [[postcolonialism]] theory
			- assessing historical legacy of colonial and imperial experiences that [[Global South]] societies and cultures still have to confront
				- important feminist component relating to these development and human rights challenges
			- focused on developing countries and roles women have played and continue to play in tribal and other settings
		- allied with postcolonial critics of [[liberal feminism]] and [[radical feminism]]
			- rejects implicit assumption that women are a homogenous group unaffected by race, class, culture, sexuality, history
			- concern for nature of power relationships up and down the levels of analysis connects them all
		- rejects idea that there's an ultimate core/essential identity to women that would have the effect of constraining them
			- category of women is socially constructed fiction
			- engages in deconstructing that fiction composed of particular social/cultural understandings
		- feminist critics of postmodernism
			- concerned if the category of women is indeterminate, then how can an alternative world order with a different role for women be suggested?
			- if critical discourse has the subversive effect of undermining concepts and creating conceptual disarray, what'll be ther eplacement?
- consensus among feminism
	- lack of women in IR and perspectives they represent in a male-dominant field with masculinist understandings taken as uncritically givens
