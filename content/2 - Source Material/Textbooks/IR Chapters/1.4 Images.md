#### 1.4.0 Images
- **[[Realism]]:** classical and neorealism/structural realism
	- states are the principal/most important actors on the international political stage and represent the key unit of analysis
	- states are viewed as unitary actors that behave in a generally rational or purposive manner
	- national security issues are primary research focus
	- **power** and **[[balance of power]]** are key concepts in analyses
- **[[Liberalism]]:** neoliberal institutionalism; presents a pluralist view of the world that is composed not just of states and their institutions, but also of multiple nonstate actors to include international and NGOs, individuals, groups
	- the state is disaggregated into its component parts and is continually subjected to outside influences
	- political-economic issues are primary research focus
- **[[Economic Structuralism]]:** identifies economic classes and other material structures, places a broader emphasis on multiple mechanisms of postcolonial dominance that maintain the so-called Global South in a subordinate status
	- all actors must be viewed within the context of an overarching global structure
	- defining characteristic of the global structure is **capitalist nature** (must be viewed in a historical context)
- **[[The English School]]:** politics occur in an international society not only realist, materialist understandings of power and [[balance of power]], but also the impacts of rules, norms, and institutions
	- theorists draw from realist, liberal, and constructivist traditions
	- **via media:** middle path
		- between realism and liberalism, between realism and idealism
- Qualifications and Clarifications
	1. Images of IR identified could be viewed as forms of/bases for interpretive understandings
	2. Images should be viewed as **ideal or pure types** in that each emphasizes what several seemingly diverse theoretical approaches have in common
	3. Images are **NOT** mutually exclusive in all respects
	4. There's a certain amount of [[conceptual eclecticism]] by scholars in the field... but the main focus is primarily on ideas, trends, generalized images, and interpretive understandings of IR; work of particular authors are secondary
	5. Images tend to focus more on what is studied than on how to conduct such studies
- The four images identified are NOT theories of IR. They represent general perspectives on IR out of which particular theories may develop.
