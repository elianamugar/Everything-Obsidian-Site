#### 11.1.0 Intellectual Precursors and Influences
- [[Plato]] (427 – 347 BCE)
	- elevated role of women alongside men in idealism [[Republic]]
	- [[Aristotle]] did not afford women the same equal standing
- [[Aristophanes]] (446 – 386 BCE), playwright
	- portrayed women in [[Lysistrata by Aristophanes]] oriented toward peace, less prone to resort to warlike activities than men, powerful (often decisive) actors in their own right
	- [[Assemblywomen by Aristophanes]]
		- women assume control of politics and establishing a communal, [[egalitarian]] society
		- oracle of Delphi always a woman
			- belief in goddesses who could impose themselves on world events
- [[Machiavelli]] (1469 – 1527)
	- women matter and can be decisive, trumping decisions and actions of men
	- portrays [[Fortuna]] metaphorically as a powerful woman and can reverse the most powerful men
- [[Mary Astell]] (1666 – 1731)
	- early feminist writer, articulated a "core liberal feminist belief that men and women are equally capable of reason, and that therefore they should be equally educated in its use" #quote 
		- core ideas of recent radical feminism
			- "man (whether as sexual predator or tyrannous husband) is the natural enemy of woman"
			- "women must be liberated from the need to please men"
- Mary [[Wollstonecraft]] (1759 – 1797)
	- [[Vindication of the Rights of Women (1792) by Mary Wollstonecraft]]
		- refutes claim "that women were less capable of reason than men"
		- argues "men and women are equally possessed of reason, they must be equally educated in its use" (in line with [[Mary Astell]])
	- education of women would enable them to exercise their reason and realize their inner virtues as fellow humans
- [[John Stuart Mill]] (1806 – 1873), [[Utilitarianism]]
	- [[Subjection of Women (1861) by John Stuart Mill]]
		- against subordinate and abusive conditions women had to endure
		- equalized position of women, assuring access to education and employment
			- full protection of law and political rights as fellow citizens and full participants in society
- [[Marx]] (1818 – 1883)
- [[Engels]] (1820 – 1895)
	- [[The Origin of the Family, Private Property and the State by Friedrich Engels]]
	- [[The Condition of the Working Class in England by Friedrich Engels]]
	- "the world historical defeat of the female sex" as "the man took command in the home also; the woman was degraded and reduced to servitude; she became the slave of his lust and a mere instrument for the production of children" #quote 
	- liberation of women comes with [[revolution]] that frees them from bondage of being treated as private property controlled by men
	- abuse of women and men in workplace in early [[Industrialization]] [[Capitalism]]
- [[Maria Stewart]] (1803 – 1879), [[Elizabeth Cady Stanton]] (1815 – 1902), [[Susan B. Anthony]] (1820 – 1906)
	- 20th c. [[suffrage]] movement
	- 1919: [[America]] women receive right to vote
- [[Post-WWII]] feminist writings and associated movements put in place the intellectual foundation for feminist scholarship and to transform the conditions experienced and roles played by women in a global project
- feminism in popular literature
	- challenges exclusionary policies toward women, unequal treatment, patterns of male dominance
	- substantial impact on a global scale concerning human rights with regard to equal treatment and empowerment of women, allowing them same opportunities that have traditionally been reserved for men
	- empowering women will give them means to limit family size voluntarily
		- reducing population growth rates to economically sustainable levels
	- counters predominantly male [[misogyny]] (restricts legally a woman's choice on reproductive health)
		- argument: women have the right to make their own decisions about their bodies
			- carrying a fetus to term
			- rape, incest, mother's health
	- women are more prone to approaching issues of peace and conflict resolution
