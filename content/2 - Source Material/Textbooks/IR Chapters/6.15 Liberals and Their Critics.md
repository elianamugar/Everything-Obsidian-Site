#### 6.15.1 Anarchy
- role of [[anarchy]] and [[security dilemma]] in [[Liberalism]]
	- overemphasized in explaining [[IR]]
	- argues that worst aspects can be overcome through collaboration of int'l orgs, institutions, regimes
- [[Realism]]'s counter
	- any analysis of world politics must begin with [[anarchy]] structure of system being taken into account
	- how can they realistically assess possibilities of cooperation and peace between states unless role of [[anarchy]] in creating suspicion and distrust is recognized?
	- if this is ignored/reduced of its importance, thinking can lead to [[utopian]] with little relation to reality
	- states often have fundamentally different interests
		- drive for material gains makes conflict inevitable and part of the landscape of [[IR]]
- [[Liberalism]]'s response
	- placing too much emphasis on [[security dilemma]] disregards any change from status quo
	- viewing world as simply competition and conflict due to mistrust among states is a distortion of reality and a self-fulfilling prophecy
		- if a state acts on suspicion and distrust, it'll produce similar responses in others and confirm one's initial suspicions
- history of world politics includes conflict and **collaboration**
	- studying instances when [[security dilemma]] has been overcome is as important as when it's contributed to onset of [[War]]
		- [[Liberalism]] claims they have conceptual tools to do so
			- [[integration]], [[interdependence]], [[neoliberal institutionalism]], [[regime theory]], [[democratic peace theory]], role of ideas accounting for actor preferences
#### 6.15.2 Theory-Building
- [[Realism]]'s critique on [[Liberalism]]
	- highly descriptive and lacking theoretical content
		- [[Globalization]] term as a popular buzzword than a theoretically useful concept
	- [[Realism]] describes world in greater detail so descriptive accuracy increases at the expense of developing narrow theories of [[IR]] that can explain patterns of behavior
		- theories should be as simple as possible
		- understanding increases by moving away from real world, not closer to it
			- seems counterintuitive, but ambitious theories aim at producing valid generalizations by viewing entire forest, not individual trees
	- by faithfully cataloging complexities of the world, [[Liberalism]] is in danger of remaining in the realm of only describing things versus explaining why things happen the way they do
- [[Liberalism]]'s rebuttal
	1. [[neoliberal institutionalism]] rebuts charge of description at expense of theoretical parsimony
		- [[Keohane]] and colleagues are theoretical counterpoint to [[neorealism]]'s approaches to [[IR]]
		- both research agendas employ [[rational-actor assumption]] and positivist standards of evidence
	2. [[structural realism]], [[neorealism]] obsessed with parsimony
		- [[classical realism]] ([[Morgenthau]], [[Wolfers]]) examines role of multitude of factors at various levels of analysis
		- classical realists seek to understand reciprocal interactive effects of state and int'l system
	3. [[Liberalism]] research has offered more theories and testable hypotheses than any other image of int'l politics
		- contributes to ultimate social science goal of cumulative knowledge
		- varying liberal research agendas coalesce around unifying theme of pacifying effects of [[democracy]], [[economic interdependence]], int'l institutions
			- these topics reinforce each other
			- diversity of approaches under [[Liberalism]] isn't neat and streamlined compared to [[Realism]]'s focus on issues of conflict among states
		- [[Liberalism]] image consists of many different actors operating within and across state boundaries
			- adds to complexity
			- theories of [[IR]] should aspire to deal with such complexity and not pretend it doesn't exist
				- how adequate are theories that fail to deal with and explain many of the changes that occurred in the nature of world politics over the past half-century?
		- while [[Realism]] is a useful "first cut" at understanding world politics, too much is left out
			- role of individuals, institutions, transnational relations, domestic politics, ideas
#### 6.15.3 The Democratic Peace
- early 1990s: demise of [[USSR]]
	- Francis [[Fukuyama]]'s [[End of History by Francis Fukuyama]]
		- [[Liberalism]] [[democracy]] has no serious ideological rival
			- it's "the end point of mankind's ideological evolution"
			- it's the "final form of human gov't"
	- [[democracy]] was gaining followers in [[Eastern Europe]] and [[Third World]] countries
- [[Realism]] was skeptical of lasting effects of expanded zone of [[democracy]]'s peace in new [[Europe]]
	- [[Balkan wars]]
	- [[Africa]]'s state conflicts
	- [[India]]-[[Pakistan]] tensions
	- rise of [[China]] as a global economic, political, military power
- [[Liberalism]]'s response
	- democratic zone of peace in [[Western Europe]] isn't a small achievement
		- debatable how much the spread of [[democracy]] contributes to explanation of this situation versus [[economic interdependence]] provided by membership of [[EU]]
	- [[Realism]]'s predictions of incessant drive for domination and "built-in systemic incentives" for armed conflict haven't threatened [[Western Europe]]'s peace
	- major threat: [[Russia]] (illiberal)
	- [[Global South]] is an appropriate test bed for [[democratic peace theory]]
		- characterized as a zone of conflict where internal conflicts and weak states seem the norm than interstate conflict
- global perspective, backsliding of [[democracy]]
	- V-Dem Institute: "The level of democracy enjoyed by the average global citizen in 2021 is down to 1989 levels. The last 30 years of democratic advances are now eradicated. [[dictatorship]]s are on the rise and harbor 70% of the world population – 5.4 billion people." #quote 
	- [[democratic]] score of [[America]] has dropped since 2015
	- by logic of [[democratic peace theory]], such developments don't bode will in terms of global conflict
#### 6.15.4 Voluntarism
- critique of [[Realism]]
	- excessively pessimistic concerning human condition and ability of individuals to control int'l events and forces
- critique of [[Liberalism]]
	- heavy reliance on assumption of [[voluntarism]], effective free will
- emphasis on agency over structure ([[voluntarism]]), key to [[Liberalism]] theory
	- int'l harmony can only be achieved if only leaders want it
		- simple matter of human volition
		- desiring cooperation instead of competition
	- transformation of nature of world politics is seen as desirable and attainable
		- "bad and ignorant leaders" or "bad governments" stand in the way
			- if they can be educated or removed, the world will be a better place
	- influence of [[Liberalism]] [[Philosophy]] is evident
- [[Realism]]'s evaluation of [[Liberalism]]
	- this view of int'l change and how it can be achieved ignores constraints placed on all leaders and states by the [[anarchy]] nature of the int'l system, drive for *relative gains*, and [[balance of power]]
	- [[Realism]] argues that people are aggressive with tendency toward warlike behavior
		- it's part of human nature to behave so
- emphasis on [[voluntarism]] isn't always acknowledged in [[Liberalism]]
	- it's not a necessary function of philosophical commitment to belief in free will that can influence outcomes
	- maybe because focus of analysis happens to be factors operating at domestic level of analysis
		- involves study of actual institutions, individual actors, and collective belief systems held by policy makers
- when studying real individuals in real institutions, it's obvious that agency matters
- world politics takes on a human face, resulting in a reliance on the assumption of [[voluntarism]]
