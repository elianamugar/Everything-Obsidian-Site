#### 9.12.0 Constructivist Affinities in the Broader IR Field
- [[constructivism]]'s similarity with [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]]
	- broad tents within which numerous perspectives and theories can be found
		- constructivism in [[critical theory]], [[postmodernism]], [[feminism]]
		- in [[The English School]] 
			- focus on rules and law in [[international society]], emphasis on [[historical sociology]]
				- historical sociology: how ideational factors emerged over time
					- fits constructivist's understandings of how structures come to be
	- with [[Liberalism]]
		- emphasizes role of ideas, learning, and shared expectations in the construction of [[regime theory]]
	- with [[classical realism]]
		- voluntarist take on matters such as historical development of [[balance of power]]
			- [[Morgenthau]]: "The confidence in the stability of the modern state system... derives... not from the balance of power, but rather from a number of elements, intellectual and moral in nature, upon which both the balance of power and the stability of the modern state repose." #quote 
			- 19th c. balance of power was embedded in moral and intellectual climate that resulted in "temperate and undecisive contests"
			- [[Thucydides]]: emphasis on power of rhetoric to shift Athenian conception of interest
- constructivism's separation from [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]]
	- [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]]'s emphasis on asocial actors primarily concerned with material interests (opposite of constructivism)
- [[constructivism]] not as prevalent/well-developed in [[Economic Structuralism]]
	- [[Gramsci]] develops concept of *historical* and *ideological* bloc
		- obstacle to social change that maintains a pattern of dominance in society (or potentially globally)
		- blocs are social constructions that economic structuralists see as serving dominant class interests that go beyond boundaries of particular states and their societies
