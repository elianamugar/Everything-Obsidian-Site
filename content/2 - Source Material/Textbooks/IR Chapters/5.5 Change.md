#### 5.5.0 Change
- realists stress continuity of [[IR]]
- insights of [[Thucydides]] relevant to today
- modern history
	- 15th – 16th c.: [[balance of power]] 
		- either as a pursued policy or a recurrent, expected outcome from interactions of states using power to pursue their own interests
- understanding evolution (change) of int'l system and predicting its future is a research goal in [[IR]]
	- methods: 
		- quantitative measures to historical data
		- discerning cycles of nat'l power and their relation to outbreak of war
#### 5.5.1 Power Transition
- Robert [[Gilpin]] (1930 – 2018)
	- [[War and Change in World Politics by Robert Gilpin]]
		- develops framework for thinking about [[hegemonic power]] [[War]]
		- belief: possible to identify recurrent patterns, common elements, general tendencies in major turning points in int'l [[History]]
	- [[offensive realism]]
		- argument: int'l political change due to political actors trying to change the int'l system to advance their own interests
			- "a state will attempt to change the int'l system if the expected benefits exceed the expected costs" #quote 
				- if there's an expected net gain
		- various periods of history marked by equilibrium
			- 1815: [[Congress of Vienna]]
		- and disequilibrium
- as long as system can adjust to demands of its states, stability is maintained
	- what accounts for change and undermining of status quo?
		- key factor: tendency for powers of member states to change at different rates in int'l system
			- due to political, economic, technological developments
			- differential growth in power of various states in system causes fundamental redistribution of power in system
- what's been the principal mechanism of change throughout history?
	- [[War]]
		- wars determine which states will govern the system
		- peace settlement after war codifies new status quo
	- equilibrium reflects new distribution of power in int'l system until the differential growth in the power of states leads to another attempt to change the system
- [[power transition theory]]
	- like [[balance of power theory]], a system-level theory
	- both theories: realists claim the distribution of power among states is key to understanding [[IR]]
	- [[power transition theory]] scholars
		- more likely to call attention to change in int'l system
		- int'l system is hierarchically ordered
			- most powerful state dominates rest
				- classification: satisfied or dissatisfied with ordering of system
		- argues [[War]] is more likely when states are relatively equal
			- particularly when differential growth in two states' economies brings a challenger close to displacing the reigning [[hegemonic power]]
	- [[balance of power theory]] scholars
		- less likely to call attention to change in int'l system
		- emphasis on stability whether in [[bipolar distribution of power]] or [[multipolar]] world
		- argues equality of power leads to stability
- currently experiencing a lull before a global cataclysm?
	- given emphasis on importance of [[War]] in changing structure of system
	- [[Graham Allison]]
		- raises possibility of global cataclysm
		- fear/deep concerns in [[America]] about rise in power of [[China]] could lead to war
			- similar to [[Thucydides]]'s argument that fear in [[Ancient World]] [[Sparta]] about the rise of [[Athens]] was the underlying cause of [[Peloponnesian War]]
- how power transitions take place is important
	- affects duration, severity, consequences of [[War]]
	- when one state is in process of overtaking the power position of another, likelihood of war increases
	- [[War]] least likely when we find ways and means of satisfying/minimizing dissatisfaction among challengers and defenders of existing power positions
		- dissatisfied challengers and defenders are the condition that makes the outbreak of war most likely
	- [[power transition theory]] scholars call for managing alliances, int'l organizations, distribution of nuclear weapons, and crises wherever and whenever they emerge
		- does NOT adopt a [[laissez-faire]] approach
- [[power transition theory]] on [[War]]
	- criticized on historical, empirical, conceptual grounds
		- true of all work that attempts to explain important issues of war and peace
	- ongoing shifts in distribution of world power and interest in implications of rising powers ([[China]], [[India]], [[Russia]])
