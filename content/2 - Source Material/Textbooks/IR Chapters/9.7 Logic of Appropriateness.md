#### 9.7.0 Logic of Appropriateness
- *logic of appropriateness* – identities, rules, norms come together
- distinction between consequence-based and rule-based actions in IR
	- consequence-based: driven by actors rationally rank-ordering their preferences/interests while being aware other actors do the same
		- actions of actors (individuals, orgs, states) are driven by calculation of consequences measured against prior preferences
			- [[neorealism]], [[neoliberalism]]
		- missing from *logic of consequences*: appreciation of how norms, rules, shared understandings of legitimate behavior can shape actors' behaviors
- *logic of appropriateness* assumes human actors follow norms and rules that associate particular identities with particular situations
	- action is associated more with identities informed by norms and rules rather than narrow understandings of self-interest
		- [[James G. March]] & [[Johan P. Olsen]]: "As a cognitive matter, appropriate action is action that is essential to a particular conception of self." #quote 
	- ethical matter: appropriate action is action that's virtuous
		- foreign policy: the application of rules associated with particular identities to particular situations
- IR's identity perspective
	- sees actors as acting in accordance with legitimated rules and norms that are socially constructed
	- IR portrayed as a community of rule followers tied to one another through intersubjective understandings, sociocultural ties, sense of belonging
- if identities are associated with and influenced by current standards of "appropriateness," then how does change in terms of int'l norms come about?
	1. when norm "entrepreneurs" emerge who can create alt norms or frames of reference
		- challenge: when promoting a new norm, it's done within current standards of appropriateness defined by existing norms
			- to overcome constraint imposed by existing normative framework, norm entrepreneurs and activists may have to act explicitly inappropriately
				- e.g. suffragettes positing alt norms as to what constituted women's interests (chaining themselves to fences, hunger strikes, damage property, refusing to pay taxes)
				- e.g. Greenpeace, Amnesty Int'l, Transparency Int'l, transnational advocacy networks and those within the UN/other int'l orgs
