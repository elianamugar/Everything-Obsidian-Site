#### 8.6.1 From System to International Society
- [[Realism]]: emphasizes continuity of IR throughout history
	- insights from [[Thucydides]] relevant today as in 5th c. BCE
- [[Liberalism]]: change is possible, focused on role of institutions, regimes, norms since [[Post-WWII]]
- [[Economic Structuralism]]: [[normative]] commitment to change, cognizant of restraints resulting from [[capitalist world-system]]
- [[The English School]]: work in context of [[Realism]], [[rationalism]], [[revolutionism]] frameworks
	- sensitivity and recognition to possibilities and limitations of change in IR and world politics
	- application of [[historical sociology]] approach to development of [[international society]]
	- [[Martin Wight]] –> [[Adam Watson]]
		- conducts in-depth studies of historical development of system of states
		- adopted distinction between system of states and a society of states from [[Hedley Bull]]
			- system of states: an impersonal network of pressures and interests that binds states together and operates outside the will of members
			- society of states: a set of common rules, institutions, codes of conduct, values to which states agree to be bound
	- [[Realism]] on system of states
		- issue of power and struggle for power among states
	- interest in societal aspects of systems
		- emphasis on how authority is developed and how practitioners contributed to development and monitoring of rules of the game
		- how to understand the current int'l system and extent to which it represents [[international society]] unless we understand how it came into being
		- historical assessment can provide insight into how current system may change in the future
- [[The Evolution of International Society by Adam Watson]]
	- investigates beyond [[Europe]]
		- ancient state systems: Sumer, Assyria, [[Persia]], Classical [[Greece]], [[Macedonian Empire]], [[India]], [[China]], [[Rome]], [[Byzantine]], [[Islam]]
	- investigates development of [[Europe]]'s [[international society]] through case studies
		- [[medieval]] Europe, [[Renaissance]] [[Italy]], [[Habsburg]] empire, rise of modern Westphalian commonwealth states, [[Napoleon]] empire
	- each system varies along a continuum of [[hegemony]] or dominance by one political entity rather than a system characterized by relative autonomy/decentralization of power and authority
	- case studies provide insight on [[The English School]]'s concern w/conditions for int'l order
		- how did these systems regulate their political entities?
		- how did societal aspects of the system develop over time?
		- can we learn from their trial and error?
	- [[Adam Watson]]'s analysis
		- issue with [[Realism]] conception of IR being characterized by a repetitive condition of anarchy through recorded history
		- claims that relations among states can be viewed through lens of international societies regulated by rules and practices
			- based on the result of actual experience rather than [[Idealism]] of what the world should be
		- places current int'l system in historical context
			- raises question of how permanent the current int'l system is and how it might change in the future
- [[The English School]]'s interest in continuity and change in IR
	- interest in the relation between int'l order and aspiration for human justice
		- younger scholars have responded to changes in IR since [[post-Cold War]]
		- [[Adam Watson]] examined expansion of [[international society]]
		- others studied phenomenon of failed states and resultant human cost
- investigation into contrasting views of what constitutes [[international society]] from POV of non-Westerners
- some scholars joined [[Liberalism]] and [[constructivism]] in examining changing relations among state [[sovereignty]], human rights, norms of humanitarian intervention
- research topics with less attention in [[The English School]]
	- European [[integration]], int'l political economy, global environmental politics
#### 8.6.2 From International Society to World Society
- the renaissance of [[The English School]]
	- early 1980s: calls for its disbandment
	- [[Barry Buzan]] resurrects and reformulates [[The English School]] approach to IR
		- engages in extensive critiques of [[The English School]]
			- exemplified by [[Martin Wight]], [[Hedley Bull]], [[Herbert Butterfield]]
		- notes internal debates, inconsistencies, conceptual and empirical voids
		- credits rise of [[constructivism]] for reinvigorating [[The English School]]'s important yet underdeveloped emphasis on social/societal dimension of IR
		- [[The English School]] consists of several complementary elements that aren't explicit in first generation of theorizing
			- [[The English School]] as a set of ideas to be found in the minds of statesmen and political theorists
			- [[The English School]] as a set of externally imposed concepts that define material and social structures of the int'l system
				- Buzan's main concern
				- key goal of addressing conceptually weak concept of [[world society]]
- traditional discussion within [[The English School]]
	- limited debate between [[pluralism]] vs [[solidarism]] conception of [[international society]]
		- concern to [[Hedley Bull]]
		- centered on actual and potential extent of shared norms, rules, institutions, and int'l law within [[international society]]
			- [[pluralism]]: states have relatively little in common other than the calculations of interest
				- thin morality in which states can agree to a framework of int'l order that allows for mutually advantageous cooperation
				- world of enlightened self-interest; states most concerned with [[security]] and maintaining order under [[anarchy]]
				- practical policies emphasize mutual recognition of [[sovereignty]], [[diplomacy]], and maintaining non-intervention principle (domestic affairs of states)
					- [[Realism]] influence
			- [[solidarism]]: views the world in cosmopolitan terms, [[international society]] has a relatively high degree of shared norms, rules, institutions among states
				- thick morality with the capacity even to enforce [[universalism]] ethics
				- ideas of individual rights and extent to which community of humankind exists
					- [[Kant]] influence
		- dichotomy or spectrum, traditional debate conducted *within* [[international society]] framework rather than [[Realism]]'s int'l system or [[Kant]]-style [[world society]]
- [[Barry Buzan]]'s contribution to reformulating the [[The English School]]
	- argument: the [[world society]] concept is more accurately composed of a set of *[[normative]]* goals for theorists, but conceptually and analytically remained at the margins
	- wants to correct this by examining [[world society]] from perspective of norms and ideas as forms of social structure
		- interest in [[constructivism]]
	- interest in a theory about norms vs. [[normative]] theory
		- closes gap between [[The English School]]'s view of theory as simply a way to organize and structure questions, concepts, and categories systematically and the [[Positivism]] emphasis on causal explanations
	- argues that a conceptually robust concept of [[world society]] could become the best approach to coming to grips with the phenomenon of [[Globalization]]
		- general IR theory challenge
	- biggest weakness in [[The English School]]
		- relation between [[international society]] and [[world society]]
			- also where the biggest theoretical AND practical gains can be made
			- working out relation between order and a more cosmopolitan culture in [[international society]]
				- unfinished in [[Hedley Bull]]'s work
	- [[The English School]] revised theory has potential to improve how [[Globalization]] is conceptualized
		- only if [[world society]] can be developed as a coherent theoretical concept
	- argues the School's triad concepts captures key aspects of [[Globalization]] to include simultaneous existence of state and non-state systems to include transnational actors (e.g. NGOs)
		- development of [[world society]] concept can help us understand potential and obstacles for transitioning to post-Westphalian world politics
			- [[Adam Watson]]'s examination of historical relation and transition between systems of states and international socities
				- [[Hobbes]] and [[Grotius]], [[Realism]] and [[rationalism]]
	- unless weakness of [[world society]] is a pillar of School thought is overcome, further progress within the School is constrained
		- [[constructivism]] insights and its approach to social relations a useful tool
- Buzan's process to rethink [[The English School]] in an era of [[Globalization]]
	- retool the int'l system, [[international society]], and [[world society]] triad
	- reconstruct the [[pluralism]]-[[solidarism]] debate
	- think through the analytical and [[normative]] implications of the [[world society]] concept
	- examine the concept of institutions that is the underpinning of order in IR
- Buzan compares and contrasts the School's approach to institutions. to the ways [[Realism]], [[liberal institutionalism]], and [[regime theory]] scholars deal with the concept
