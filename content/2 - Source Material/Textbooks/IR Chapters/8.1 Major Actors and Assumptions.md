#### 8.1.0 Major Actors and Assumptions
- assumption 1: world can be understood as an int'l or [[Anarchic]] society in which both states and non-state actors operate
- emphasis on concept of *society*
	- [[Realism]] doesn't pair *society* and [[Anarchic]]
- what is an *[[international society]]*?
	- "A group of states (or, more generally, a group of independent political communities) which not merely form a system, in the sense that the behavior of each is a necessary factor in the calculations of the others, but also have established by dialogue and consent common rules and institutions for the conduct of their relations, and recognize their common interest in maintaining these arrangements." #quote 
	- skepticism of "because the int'l system lacks a common power to keep citizens in awe, we cannot speak of an [[international society]] but rather only a system of states"
- assumption 2: concept of *order* in the [[Anarchic]]al society plays an important theoretical role
	- order results from power and [[balance of power]], also from acceptance of rules and institutional arrangements that are in the enlightened, rational self-interest of states and other actors
	- similar to [[classical realism]]
		- [[The English School]] understands importance in int'l affairs of power (material component) and ideas, values, norms
- reliance on tradition of int'l law
	- [[Realism]], [[Liberalism]], [[neoliberal institutionalism]] use concept of [[rationalism]] as an underlying assumption that contributes to development of ungenerous theories
	- [[The English School]]'s [[rationalism]]
		- [[Grotius]]
		- rules, laws, institutional arrangements states have established to provide some degree of order to an [[Anarchic]] [[international society]]
		- structure associated with broadly conceived rule-based institutional framework
			- contrasts [[Realism]]'s emphasis on defining structure in terms of *polarity* or distribution of capabilities
- assumption 3: importance of [[Kant]]'s ethical and moral understandings
	- balanced by pragmatic view of [[Anarchic]] society as one in which considerations of power and interest remain important
	- [[world society]] concept for this [[Kant]]/[[revolution]]/[[utopian]] strain of thought
		- realizing a universal [[cosmopolitanism]] and transforming the world into a society based on norms with broad moral acceptance
		- different from [[Liberalism]] view of world/global society
			- [[Liberalism]] [[world society]] goes beyond int'l or interstate relations to encompass a complex array of state, non-state, transnational actors that engage with each other globally
	- concept of [[international society]] reflects [[Grotius]]'s [[rationalism]] influence central to [[The English School]]
	- concept of [[world society]] reflects [[Kant]]'s [[revolution]] influence central to [[The English School]]'s revolutionist branch
- avoids a narrow perspective on world politics
	- [[Martin Wight]] (1913 – 1972)
		- founder of [[The English School]]
		- [[Realism]] (int'l system), [[rationalism]] ([[international society]]), and [[revolution]] ([[world society]]) are important to understanding world politics
	- int'l system, [[international society]], and [[world society]] can all exist simultaneously as understandings of reality
		- subject to analysis
