#### 9.0 Constructivist Understandings
- also known as [[social constructivism]]
- top 3 perspectives on int'l politics
	- other 2: [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]]
- doesn't claim to offer a global vision of IR (similar to the other images)
- offers an approach to interpretive understanding that already had an enormous impact on theorizing in IR
- gradual rise in importance in IR
	- unforeseen events in 1980s and 1990s
	- [[Cold War]]
	- IR theorists failed to anticipate the peaceful end of the [[USSR]] and the collapse of its empire
		- [[Realism]]'s [[power transition theory]]
			- might've expected an eventual clash between superpowers ([[America]] and [[USSR]])
			- didn't expect internal collapse of [[USSR]]
				- traditional enemy of West was gone
		- there should've been greater theoretical anticipation of this momentous process
	- noticeable decline in state [[sovereignty]]
	- military intervention and [[normative]] justification on humanitarian grounds became evident
	- [[Globalization]] rose doubts about explanatory power of existing theories and approaches
		- intensification of economic, political, social, cultural relations across borders
- [[Economic Structuralism]] theories that relied on [[Marxism]] concepts
	- suffered in academia when [[Liberalism]]'s [[democracy]] and [[Capitalism]] precepts held sway globally
- 1980s: [[constructivism]]'s critique of [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]]
	- commonality between the 3: interpretive understanding of observed phenomena
		- opening the subjective dimensions of knowing and creating the world
- [[social constructivism]]'s core
	- relates to the *intersubjective* dimension of human action to include knowledge and reality
		- assumption that objects of our knowledge aren't independent of our interpretations
	- interested in key concepts of norms, rules, identities and how they affect the conception of ourselves and how we relate to the world
	- seen as middle ground between other conceptual approaches to IR
		- [[Positivism]]: material-based images in [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]] theories
			- emphasize explanations based on natural science criteria
			- explanation of actors' actions
				- [[rational-actor assumption]]: assumed that rational choices are made among alternative by evaluating their likely consequences in terms of objectives being sought
			- ideas may matter, but they're secondary to materially oriented interests
			- constructivists use empirical data and embrace [[Positivism]] scientific standards for testing hypotheses
				- conception of what constitutes explanation differs
			- [[The English School]] has closest affinity to [[constructivism]]
				- emphasis on norms and importance of history and its application to the concept of society to IR
		- radical [[postmodernism]], [[post-structuralism]], [[feminism]] interpretivists
			- sociology of knowledge – ideas matter
			- science is power disguised as knowledge
			- explanation in [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]] sense is an impossibility
			- may be closer to [[constructivism]] due to emphasis on power of ideas and importance of interpretive understandings
