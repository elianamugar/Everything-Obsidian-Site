#### 12.0 Green Theory and Environmental Understandings
- an interpretive understanding. of how the global environment impacts all humans
	- extinction of species and collapsing of fishing stocks as warming oceans accelerate with global climate change
	- warming planet melts ice caps and causes sea levels to rise, problematic for islands and sea coasts
	- extremes in weather patterns (tornadoes, hurricanes, cyclones, typhoons)
	- warming planet dries land, plants suffer –> food security challenge
	- droughts indirectly cause wildfires
- speed at which climate change occurs leaves little time to adapt to altered circumstances
	- ongoing carbon emissions are major cause of global climate change
		- industrial production depends on 
			- carbon fuels
			- burning/cutting forests that take in CO2 from the atmosphere
			- melting tundra that contains carbon
			- massive agricultural use of fertilizers
			- flatulence of cattle and other animals raised for food
			- rotting food and other organic materials in garbage dumps
		- carbon emissions create "greenhouse"/heat-containing effect
			- heat form sun enters the atmosphere by day
			- heat is trapped by carbon dioxide and methane in the upper atmosphere
			- reduces cooling that nighttime normally would affect
- 1960s: literature on global environment
	- focus on economic development at any cost was subject to criticism
- [[Silent Spring by Rachel Carlson]]
	- wake-up call on loss of species and adverse impact on humans due to widespread use of pesticides
	- focus: insensitivity to the environmental impact of economic development
		- pollution of land, air, waterways, oceans
		- policy makers believed concern about environment was more domestic than a global matter calling for collective action
		- recent discourse in IR re: environment
- global climate change as #1 int'l [[security]] concern
	- global warming and its consequences over time are an existential threat to life on Earth
		- 1960s concern of chemicals on land, waterways, air over major cities
	- a global concern
		- challenges to achieving globally effective policies are daunting
- major problem with global environmental policies
	- fundamental asymmetry between states who have contributed to most of the global warming problem ([[First World]]) and those that suffered the most ([[Third World]])
		- e.g. impact on [[Africa]]
			- global warming contributes to food and nutrition insecurity
	- countries in [[Global South]] have been seeking financial compensation from [[First World]] countries
		- since 19th c. [[Industrial Revolution]], principal cause of present-day global climate change
- climate change originally viewed as an int'l security issue that might compound into existing political and socioeconomic challenges
	- migration induced by droughts/typhoons
		- can cause political instability
		- states may become involved in wars over scarce resources (e.g. water)
	- fragile states intersect with environmental vulnerability
		- climate change and environmental degradation as threat multipliers
			- increases domestic and transnational security risks
- [[Red Cross]]
	- links climate change and environmental degradation to conflict and violence ([[War]]) in the [[Global South]]
- [[United Nations]]
	- competition over scarce resources is responsible for 40% of civil wars and conflicts over last 6 decades
- Thomas [[Malthus]], late 18th c.
	- warned that population growth would outstrip natural resources
- [[America]] [[DoD]]
	- climate change is a threat to US nat'l security
	- melting in [[Arctic]] Ocean
		- int'l competition for fishing, oil, gas, other mineral resources with both allies ([[Canada]]) and adversaries ([[Russia]])
		- primary responsibility for 12-mile territorial waters off of [[Alaska]] rests with US Coast Guard, deep-sea waters beyond that limit with US Navy
	- while traditional military threats and actions (e.g. 2022 [[Russia-Ukraine War]]) are deliberate actions that can be pinpointed to a specific date and actors, environmental challenges are long term and slow to develop
		- actual impacts subject to debate
			- wide range of state and non-state actors
- overview of chapter
	- how various perspectives on IR theory have viewed/ignored environmental issues
		- [[Realism]], [[Liberalism]], [[The English School]]
			- seeks to incorporate environmental issues into their respective worldviews
		- [[Economic Structuralism]]
		- interpretive understandings discussed in previous 3 chapters
	- green theory has a strong affinity/basis in [[constructivism]], [[critical theory]], [[feminism]]
