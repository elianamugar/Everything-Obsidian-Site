#### 6.1.0 Major Actors and Assumptions
- [[Realism]] and their primary interest
	- power and balance of power
		- their explanations of political and economic competition, [[War]], and other conflicts prevalent in [[IR]]
	- int'l system is starting point for analysis
	- factors at unit/state-societal level of analysis is secondary importance
- [[Liberalism]] and their primary interest
	- explaining conditions under which int'l cooperation, collaboration, or [[multilateralism]] becomes possible
	- factors at unit/state-societal level is starting point for analysis
	- int'l system is secondary importance
		- opposite of [[Realism]]
- [[Liberalism]]'s image
	- all-inclusive
		- states, int'l and nongov'tal orgs and cross-cutting networks that connect them
	- focuses on:
		- [[democratic peace theory]]
		- [[integration]]
		- [[interdependence]]
		- [[regime theory]]
		- [[neoliberal institutionalism]]
		- ways and means of [[global governance]]
	- 5 key assumptions:
		1. states and non-state transnational actors are important entities in world politic
			- int'l orgs can be independent actors in their own right
			- other nongov'tal, transnational orgs (multinational corporations, MNCs), and human rights and environmental groups play important roles in world politics
			- individuals *can* have a significant impact
			- [[pluralism]] image – multiple kinds of state and non-state actors play substantial roles in world politics
			- prefer *world*/*global* rather than *int'l* politics
				- *int'l* tends to privilege the state over int'l and nongov'tal orgs
				- *int'l* politics is a euphemism for interstate politics (suited to [[Realism]])
			- analytical challenge for [[Liberalism]]: how, to what extent, and under what circumstances do these diverse actors influence world politics?
		2. economic/other forms of [[interdependence]] (interconnectedness) among state and non-state actors have a pacifying effect/moderates state behavior
			- discussion of [[Globalization]]
				- [[Liberalism]] sees states, int'l orgs, nongov'tal orgs, MNCs, groups, individuals overlapping coalitions/networks
				- growth of transnational networks oriented around common strategies/goals epitomize rapid expansion of "sovereignty-free" actors
					- [[global civil society]]
				- terrorist and criminal orgs viewed as dark side of [[Globalization]]
		3. agenda of int'l politics is extensive – rejects notion that agenda of int'l politics is dominated by only military-security issues
			- distinction between *high politics* and *low politics* FALSELY DRAWN
			- economic, social, environmental issues also matter
				- can sometimes be understood as [[security]] issues (sometimes more than military-related security matters)
		4. "inside-out" view that examines how factors at state-society and individual levels of analysis affects [[IR]] and outcomes
			- opposed to [[structural realism]]'s "top-down" view on how [[anarchy]] and distribution of capabilities affect state behavior
			- [[democratic peace theory]]: how political culture, values, domestic political structures influence prospects for int'l peace
			- role of perception, small-group behavior, decision-making processes
				- particular policies enhance bureaucratic power of one org at expense of another/state as a whole
				- decision-making processes associated with coalition/counter-coalition building/bargaining/compromising may not yield best/optimal decision for particular state
		5. key analytical task: discover under what conditions int'l collaboration (if not peace) might be achieved
			- role of int'l orgs is major focus
				- [[regional integration]] and [[interdependence]]
				- task: go beyond description and achieve explanation
					- e.g. [[neoliberal institutionalism]] and [[structural realism]] utilize [[rational-actor assumption]] to help generate testable hypotheses on how int'l orgs can affect states' calculations of interests
			- calculations of interest/utility (gains and losses) can also be affected by misunderstanding/misperceptions on part of state decision makers
				- incomplete information, bias, uncertainty about cause-and-effect relations related to policy options under consideration
- [[regime theory]]: collaboration is possible where principle, norms, and actors' expectations converge on a particular issue area
	- [[Liberalism]] takes [[Game Theory]] from [[positive-sum]] perspective, size of pie can be increased
	- *absolute gains* (all can win) are opposed to [[Realism]]'s assumption of *relative gains* that supposedly drive interstate competition
		- when one gains or loses disproportionately more than others
		- when one's gain is another's loss
		- a [[zero-sum]] outcome
- [[integration]], [[interdependence]], [[regime theory]], [[neoliberal institutionalism]] all examine possibilities of upgrading common interest to include impact of non-material factors (ideas and norms)
	- some liberals incorporate [[social constructivism]] understandings within their work
- [[Liberalism]]: human agents matter, takes into account how human agents relate to material/ideational factors that may facilitate/constrain their conduct
	- with agency, states/int'l and nongov'tal orgs may be actors
		- prone to look within state/other institutions to find agency at human level of individuals and small groups
	- cognizant of impact of system-level influences
		- challenges [[Realism]] assumption of [[rational-actor assumption]]
			- [[Liberalism]] uses this as a methodological starting point to understand role of int'l orgs and regimes in enhancing collaboration among states
- liberal approach to theory
	- building separate islands of theory
		- each explaining some things but not others
	- eventual goal of connecting them with more general theory of int'l politics
	- [[voluntarism]]
		- compared to [[structural realism]] and [[Economic Structuralism]]
	- [[Liberalism]] cautiously optimistic that int'l collaboration/partial [[global governance]] is achievable
		- [[Globalization]] is not without its costs
		- explaining logic and circumstances under which this can be achieved is major theoretical and empirical challenge
