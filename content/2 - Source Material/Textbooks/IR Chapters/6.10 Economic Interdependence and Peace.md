#### 6.10.0 Economic Interdependence and Peace
- Richard [[Rosecrance]]'s [[The Rising of the Trading State by Richard Rosecrance]]
	- 18th - 19th c. argument: associating increased trade among states with peace
		- [[4. The Rise of the State and Modern Political Thought]]
	- contrasts [[Realism]] military-political world with [[Liberalism]] trading world
		- utilizing [[Game Theory]] to highlight respective constant-sum/[[variable-sum]] characters
	- rise of a new "trading world" offers possibility of escaping major int'l conflicts and establishing new patterns of cooperation among states
		- benefits of trade exceed those of military competition and territory aggrandizement
			- e.g. [[Japan]]
- territorial acquisition may harm ability of a state to increase its national wealth
	- [[Rosecrance]] suggests states will calculate out of self-interest that an open int'l trading system will allow them to find a productive niche in world commerce's structure
		- doesn't deny that traditional military competition will continue
	- since 1945: threat of all-out nuclear war made major wars less likely to occur
- since 1945: prevalence of trading option increases peaceful possibilities among states that were lacking in the 19th c.
	- 1930s when competitive economic policies helped drive world into depression
- as [[economic interdependence]] spreads, economic development through trade and foreign investment becomes **self-reinforcing process** and integral part to state strategy
	- [[Rosecrance]]: expands theoretical focus, including **trade** and **commerce** in strategic understandings
		- not just confining strategy to military sector
- [[Realism]] skeptical to peace-through[[economic interdependence]]
	- [[Kenneth Waltz]] and [[Mearsheimer]]
		- late 19th c.: [[Western]] trading system had extremely high levels of [[interdependence]], but didn't prevent [[WWI]]
			- nat'l security concerns trumped economic interests
	- if states feel threatened, they'll take whatever military action is required, despite costs of breaking economic ties
		- especially if an aggressive great power attempts to upset territorial status quo and embarks on military conquests
- how shifts in the int'l economy can affect int'l security relations
	- [[Globalization]]
		- [[Stephen Brooks]]
			- impact of the [[Globalization]] of production on great power security relations and security relations among states
			- argues historically unprecedented development left out of debate concerning hypothesized pacification of interstate relations through int'l trade
				- geographic dispersion of production by MNCs
					- where and how these MNCs organize their int'l production activities are key integrating force of **global commerce**, not necessarily trade
- shift from 1970s study on MNCs that focus on extraction of raw materials from exploited [[Global South]] states
	- [[Stephen Brooks]] study of [[Globalization]] of production examines broader int'l security implications
		- uses independent variable ([[Globalization]] of production) and examines how it could influence security calculations
			- instead of focusing on dependent variable to explain a particular case (e.g. outbreak of [[WWI]]) or general class of events (e.g. interstate wars)
- [[Stephen Brooks]]'s 3 major means by which int'l economy can influence security
	- changing capabilities
		- whether [[Globalization]] of production has changed parameters of weapons production
		- is self-sufficiency still possible?
			- autarkic, go-it-alone arms production in decline
			- to remain on cutting edge of military technology requires engagement in production of weaponry beyond one's borders
	- incentives
		- has the geographic dispersion of MNC production reduce the benefits of physical conquest of other states?
			- economic benefits of military conquest are greatly reduced
	- nature of actors
		- has dispersed MNC production positively influenced the prospects for [[regional integration]]?
			- it has, even where traditional security rivals have existed
			- maybe not the primary cause of the integration, but can help deepen it
- [[Stephen Brooks]]'s conclusion
	- influence of [[Globalization]] of production is most evident in terms of great powers relations
	- cognizant of [[democratic peace theory]], but his evidence strengthens argument that int'l commerce acts as a force for peace among great powers
		- rest of the world, differential impact
	- security implications of [[Globalization]] of production are a mixed bag between great powers and developing countries
		- negative among countries of [[Global South]]
