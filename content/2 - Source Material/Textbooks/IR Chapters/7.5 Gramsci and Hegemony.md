#### 7.5.0 Gramsci and Hegemony
- Antonio [[Gramsci]] (1891 – 1937)
	- [[Italy]], [[Marxism]]
	- departs from hardline [[Marxism]]-[[Lenin]] formulation
	- offers volitional approach to theory and practice, appealing to [[Economic Structuralism]]
	- [[Prison Notebooks by Antonio Gramsci]]
		- written during [[Italy]]'s [[fascism]] under [[Mussolini]]
		- emphasis on political [[voluntarism]]
			- roots from [[Machiavelli]]
				- understood importance of practical action
		- key concept: historical and ideological bloc (obstacle to social change)
			- maintains pattern of dominance in society or globally
			- social constructions that serve dominant class interests
			- *historic bloc*: instrument of hegemony
				- decisive
				- composed of structures (objective) and superstructures (subjective)
		- argument: the hegemonic position relies on consent more than coercion
			- dominant classes provide a social vision that's supposedly in the interests of all
				- vision reflected in pop culture, education, literature, political parties
			- if subordinate social groups buy into this *vision*, the ruling classes won't have to rely on coercion to stay in power
			- [[Gramsci]] hopes this dominant ideology/social vision can be challenged by elements of civil society by articulating a counter-hegemonic vision that would open the way for a [[post-capitalism]] future
- [[Robert Cox]]
	- influenced by [[Gramsci]]
	- work on social forces and [[hegemony]]
	- relating ideas to global economic and political structures to avoid the limitations of state-centric [[IR]] theory
	- [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]] underestimate the powerful and expansive nature of [[world hegemony]]
		- at it'l or systems level, [[hegemony]] isn't just an order among states, it's an order within a world-economy with a dominant mode of production that penetrates all countries
		- [[hegemony]] evident in complex social and political relations that connect social classes of different countries
		- [[world hegemony]] is a combination of social structure, economic structure, political structure
			- expressed in universal norms, institutions, other mechanics that constitute general rules of behaviors for states and transnational, civil society actors
				- supports the dominant capitalist mode of production
	- contrasts [[Liberalism]] view
		- int'l orgs are a mechanism through which universal norms of [[world hegemony]] are expressed
- [[Craig Murphy]]
	- influenced by [[Gramsci]]
	- North-South historical bloc composed of
		- "Atlantic"/"Trilateral" ruling class
		- some of the subordinate classes within advanced industrial states
		- rising governing class in dependent [[Global South]] that maintain a collective position of dominance over those that are subordinate to them
	- bloc has many interrelated reinforcing faces (economic, political, culture) that facilitate maintenance of this dominance
	- sees ideas and values institutionalized in int'l orgs as designed to serve identifiable interests in a [[capitalist world-system]]
