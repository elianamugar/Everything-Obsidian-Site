#### 11.2.0 Major Assumptions
1. *gender* as the major category of analysis to highlight women's perspectives on social issues and research
	- gender is a set of socially and culturally constructed characteristics that are associated with what it means in any culture to be masculine or feminine
		- masculinity
			- power and forceful activity
			- [[rationalism]] often cold to human concerns
			- self-empowered autonomy
			- assumption of leadership in public roles
		- femininity
			- less assertive/aggressive behavior
			- willful dependence/interdependence in nurturing relationships with others
			- sensitivity to emotional aspects of issues
			- focus on the private realm
	- gender categories dependent upon one another for their meaning, permeates public and private life
		- gendered identity/self is defined in relation to (an)other
		- society reinforces the idea to be a "real man" means not to display "femininity"
	- [[Epistemology]]
		- feminists pursue empirical research
		- strict positivist dichotomies are rejected by feminists
			- adopt [[constructivism]] approach to work
				- emphasizes how knowledge is shaped by culture, history, context
		- feminists can be found among [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]]
			- critics of scholars within any of the IR images who marginalize gender as an interpretive lens
	- gender permeates social life
		- profound and largely unnoticed effects on the actions of states, int'l orgs, transnational actors
		- feminist scholarship seeking to develop a research agenda to trace and explain these effects
2. gender is particularly important as a primary way to signify relationships of power in domestic realm, foreign policy, and IR
	- when we privilege masculinity, women socially and legally can be cast into a subordinate status
	- gender hierarchies perpetuate unequal role expectations
		- contributes to inequalities between men and women in IR
	- emphasizes social relations as the key unit of analysis
		- interest in causes and consequences of unequal power relationships between men and women
		- unequal power relations exist through time, across cultures, at all levels of analysis
	- different perspective on power from previous images
	- issue with gender-neutral concepts in int'l system
		- argument: virtually the entire Western philosophical tradition ignore feminine perspectives, masculine bias
3. many contemporary IR theory feminists are dedicated to emancipatory goal of achieving equality for women via the elimination of unequal gender relations
	- emphasis on how, e.g. military conflict among and within states directly affects the lives of the dispossessed (women in particular)
		- doesn't just base their analysis on abstract speculation of how [[anarchy]] might influence the behavior of rational, unitary states
	- states are just as likely to threaten the security of women if one empirically examines system level of analysis, state, societal, local levels
		- far from states being viewed as security provider
		- suspicious of the view that levels of analysis demarcate a clear division between the int'l system and state-society
			- int'l system characterized as [[anarchy]]
			- state-society characterized as community
	- gender as a category of analysis
		- old assumptions about security and new assumptions about who benefits from [[Globalization]] can be examined in a new, more humane light
