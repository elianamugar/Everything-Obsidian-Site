#### 8.4.0 Genesis of the English School
- [[America]]'s beginnings
	- 1954: [[Dean Rusk]] (1909 – 1994) and [[Kenneth Thompson]] (1921 – 2013) establish American Committee of academic and policy-oriented [[Realism]] scholars with sponsorship from Rockefeller Foundation in Columbia University (New York)
		- [[Dean Rusk]] later becomes Secretary of State in [[JFK]] and [[Johnson]] administrations
		- [[Kenneth Thompson]] was a student and colleague of [[Morgenthau]]
		- [[William T. R. Fox]] (1912 – 1988)
		- Hans J. [[Morgenthau]] (1904 – 1980)
		- Reinhold [[Niebuhr]] (1892 – 1971)
		- [[Paul Nitze]] (1907 – 2004)
		- Arnold [[Wolfers]] (1892 – 1968)
		- [[Kenneth Waltz]] (1924 – 2013)
			- [[William T. R. Fox]]'s student
		- purpose: develop a theory to comprehend, explain, guide the study of [[IR]] and the formulation of foreign policy
			- deep divisions among these [[Realism]] scholars on theory and policy
			- American Committee didn't last long
- [[The English School]]'s beginnings
	- 1954: [[Kenneth Thompson]] reaches out to [[Herbert Butterfield]] (1900 – 1979), colleague and historian at Cambridge University to form a parallel group
		- [[Herbert Butterfield]] turns to [[Desmond Williams]] (1921 – 1987), diplomatic historian
	- 1958: [[Martin Wight]] at LSE invited to join them to form the British Committee in Cambridge
		- [[Adam Watson]] (1914 – 2007), British Foreign Office
		- [[William Armstrong]] (1915 – 1980), Treasury
		- [[Michael Howard]] (1922 – 2019), military historian from Oxford
		- [[Donald Mackinnon]] (1913 – 1994), Wight's protégé
		- [[Hedley Bull]], from [[Australia]]
		- [[R. J. Vincent]] (1943 – 1990)
	- Two prominent scholars weren't invited to join
		- [[E. H. Carr]]
			- presence would become too dominant a force in the group
		- [[F. H. Hinsley]] (1918 – 1998)
			- [[Adam Watson]]: he saw "Europe, and the world, as more of an [[anarchy]] and less of a society than [[Hedley Bull]] or myself"
	- [[Hedley Bull]] and [[R. J. Vincent]] interested in strategic studies
		- deployed [[Martin Wight]]'s 3 traditions to engage with classical theorists
			- [[Realism]] of [[Machiavelli]] or [[Hobbes]]
			- [[rationalism]] of [[Grotius]]
			- [[revolutionism]] of [[Kant]]
		- shared a theoretical investigation into the nature of order and justice in [[international society]]
- [[The English School]]'s central understanding
	- [[IR]] and world politics viewed as a society rather than a system
		- system view was prominent in [[America]]
	- system of states
		- lacks bonds, common norms, cohesion found in [[international society]]
	- how a system of states can be transformed into an [[international society]]
	- how the [[international society]] can slip back into a system of states
- [[The Anarchical Society – A Study of Order in World Politics by Hedley Bull]]
	- [[Hedley Bull]]: where order comes from in such an [[Anarchic]] [[international society]]
		- is it from operation of power and [[balance of power]] ([[Hobbes]]'s [[Realism]])
		- Is it from rules or laws that have been constructed in [[international society]] ([[Grotius]]'s [[rationalism]])
		- is it from moral principle that comes to be accepted in a progressive improvement of states and their relations with other states ([[Kant]]'s [[Idealism]])
	- [[Hedley Bull]]: rules/norms are a source of order in [[international society]] ([[Grotius]]'s [[Internationalism]])
		- beyond [[Hobbes]]'s [[Realism]]
		- exemplifies rule-oriented construction in the *via media* between [[Hobbes]]'s [[Realism]] of power and [[balance of power]] and [[Kant]]'s [[Idealism]] of universal moral principle
