#### 9.5.0 Agents
- constitutive logic in [[constructivism]]'s ontological assumption
	- agents may be states
	- non-state actors include individuals or groups, social movements, corporations, int'l orgs, NGOs, or classes
		- potential to influence the creation of int'l norms, identities, behavior of states
		- states can impact non-state agents
- structure (social relations and shared meanings) can have a constitutive effect on actors
	- can encourage actors/agents to redefine their interests and identities in an ongoing socialization process
	- [[constructivism]]: interest in how ideational structures shape the way actors define themselves and relate to other actors
	- [[neorealism]] and [[neoliberalism]]: interest and identities constant to isolate the causal factors related to power among states and the dynamics within and between int'l institutions
- actors/agents have impact on structures and how they're altered and produced
	- key point: agents (actors) and structures mutually constitute one another
		- structures aren't objects that simply influence actors in a unidirectional manner
		- agents can change structures and escape from situations that encourage and replicate
			- e.g. conflictual practices ([[War]])
		- reciprocal relation between agency and structure
			- [[Hobbes]]: int'l system of perpetual war of all against all
			- [[Thucydides]]: interstate war
		- not necessarily an inevitable state of affairs endlessly replicated through the ages, but rather a socially constructed structure developed over time
	- *mutual constitution* logic
		- states can change the rules of the game
			- can escape into Lockean or Kantian culture of agreed rules or accepted norms to guide the conduct of int'l politics
			- a community of peaceful states can degenerate into a [[Hobbes]] world marked by conflict
				- change isn't always positive
