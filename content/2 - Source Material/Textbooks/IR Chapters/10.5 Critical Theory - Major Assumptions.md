#### 10.5.0 Critical Theory – Major Assumptions
1. study of IR should be about emancipatory politics
	- geared towards social and political transformation
		- first step: scrutiny of current understanding of int'l politics to understand and explain existing realities of IR and how they develop over time
	- to avoid [[Idealism]] (what the world *should* be), IR scholars must explain and criticize current political order in terms of principles embedded in political institutions and cultural practices
		- work done on comparative [[historical sociology]] of states illustrates use of empirical evidence
	- [[emancipation]]
		- [[Enlightenment]], work by [[Kant]]
			- interested in how competitive power relations among states could be transformed into a more cosmopolitan order of perpetual peace
		- negative conception of freedom that emphasizes removing repressive constraints/relations of domination
			- interest in relation between power and freedom
		- [[Marx]] (1818 – 1883)
			- critical theory draws from his analysis of human inequality and [[normative]] goal of eliminating [[exploitation]]
		- [[Frankfurt School]]
			- Theodor [[Adorno]] (1903 – 1969), Max [[Horkheimer]] (1895 – 1973), Herbert [[Marcuse]] (1898 – 1979)
			- turned Marxist critique of political economy into critique of ideology
			- [[Habermas]]
				- takes Marx's assertion seriously that heretofore philosophers had only interpreted the world, but the point was to change it
	- development of critical theory – diverse philosophical influences
		- escaping from ideological constraints ([[Rousseau]])
		- searching for universal moral principles with the universality of application ([[Kant]])
		- identifying the oppression of class/socioeconomic structures ([[Marx]])
		- understanding role of human [[Psychology]] in relationships of dominance ([[Freud]])
		- rejecting [[determinism]] in favor of [[Gramsci]]-style [[Marxism]]
			- adopts [[normative]], but practical approach to challenging and overthrowing structures of domination
		- normative and ethical concerns should be embedded in theories of IR
			- what is the good and just society?
	- systemic investigation of the existing order
		- criticism
			- supports practical political theory that can map routes to societal and political transformation
		- the good society is a just and democratic order that should be extended beyond the state to the int'l domain in the creation of a cosmopolitan community
	- critical theorists on [[voluntarism]] extreme end
		- sees great transformative potential residing in those able to see through ideological and other ideational masks that obscure unjust, exploitative realities
	- [[Andrew Linklater]] 
		- "triple transformation"
			- required to undermine the relationships among [[sovereignty]], territory, and national conceptions of citizenship
		- these transformations involve widespread recognition that
			1. certain moral, political, legal principles need to be universalized
			2. material inequality must be reduced
			3. there's need for respect for ethnic, cultural, gender differences
2. relation between knowledge and interest
	- knowledge-seeking is inherently political
		- detached theorizing is an impossibility and a sham
	- [[Robert Cox]]: "Theory is always for someone and for some purpose"
		- theories embedded in social and political life
		- critical theory examines purposes served by particular theories
			- critical theorists believe the beliefs held by many positivists necessarily bias their truth claims and may well be part of global ideological schemes to legitimate particular world orders
				- alleged agenda domination
					- may be convenient to advance ideologies masquerading as scientifically-based theories
			- unmask deceptions, probe for deeper understandings, expose class/elite interests in the ideologies/alleged theories
3. scrutinized works of [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]]
	- [[Thucydides]]'s concern with language and practices ([[History of the Peloponnesian War by Thucydides]]) can be viewed as beginning of critical theory
		- individuals are conscious initiators of the described events
	- [[Machiavelli]] as an interpretive theorist
		- sensitive to historical context of political action
	- critical theory POV: realism and liberalism claim to be problem-solving technical approaches to IR
		- built-in bias toward stability and maintaining status quo of int'l politics
		- provides answers on how to manage IR and keep int'l system within bounds and avoid disruption
			- not revolutionaries
		- realism and liberalism goal isn't to transform IR for the betterment of the vast majority of humanity
	- critical theory's major goal: to uncover underlying power and other motives these theories allegedly advance
		- critique towards realism and [[neorealism]] whose theories knowingly/unknowingly give legitimacy to states and relations among them
		- not really theories (to critical theorists), but rather cover stories
			- ideologies serving state, class, elite interests masquerading as theories
	- criticism to positivist understanding of IR
		- [[Richard Ashley]] on [[Kenneth Waltz]]'s system-level structural explanation for the behaviors observed among states
			- influence of [[Habermas]]'s critical theory
			- "poverty of neorealism"
			- "What emerges is a positivist structuralism that treats the given order as the natural order, limits rather than expands political discourse, negates or trivializes the significance of variety across time and place, subordinates all practice to an interest in control, bows to the ideal of a social power beyond responsibility, and thereby deprives political interaction of those practical capacities which make social learning and creative change possible. What emerges is an ideology that anticipates, legitimizes, and orients a totalitarian project of global proportions: the rationalization of global politics." #quote 
	- criticism to [[neoliberalism]] (emphasis on maintaining int'l stability at times of int'l economic unrest)
		- [[Robert Cox]]: neoliberalism is situated between the system of states and [[capitalist world-system]], providing insight on how the two can coexist
- critical theory separate from [[postmodernism]]
	- critical theory retain strict methodological criteria to guide their work
		- not a complete rejection of science or positivism
	- but there's some overlap with postmodernism
