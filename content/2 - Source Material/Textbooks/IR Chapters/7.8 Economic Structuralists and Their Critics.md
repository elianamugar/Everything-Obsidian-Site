#### 7.8.0 Economic Structuralists and Their Critics
- [[Economic Structuralism]] literature
	- subject to criticism by [[IR]] specialists and comparative politics, or ignored
	- [[postcolonialism]] literature rarely referenced in mainstream [[IR]] journals
	- harsh criticism of [[dependency]] theorists and [[Wallerstein]]
		- reflects divergent ontological and epistemological issues reflected in [[Positivism]]-[[postmodernism]] divide
		- ideological preference
#### 7.8.1 The Question of Causality
- critics question whether [[dependency]] creates and sustains underdevelopment or if it's the lesser level of development that leads to a situation of [[dependency]]
- no agreement on causality
	- whether dependency is the *cause* or *effect* of underdevelopment
#### 7.8.2 Reliance on Economics
- critics argue some [[Economic Structuralism]] reduced the operation of the int'l system down to the process of capital accumulation and related dynamics
	- what about other non-economic explanations of [[imperialism]] and relations among states?
		- political and strategic motives equally or even more important?
	- how can one account for 19th c. [[Europe]] states scrambling for economically low-value pieces of terrain?
		- what was the economic motive?
	- if the competitive interstate is derived from capitalist mode of production, how does one explain similar competitive behavior among political units in pre-capitalist eras before 15th c.?
		- e.g. [[Thucydides]]'s discussion of [[Peloponnesian War]] that lends support to validity of [[anarchy]] and [[security dilemma]]
			- suggests autonomy of political realm and distinctly political dynamic involving competition among sovereign units well before the emergency of a [[capitalist world-system]]
- critics claim the economic variable can't carry the great explanatory weight assigned to it
	- insights from the contemplation of [[IR]] over the centuries shouldn't be ignored
	- [[structural realism]] argues that it's the int'l political-security system that largely determines the int'l economic system, not the other way around
#### 7.8.3 System Dominance
- critics say there's an excessive reliance on int'l economic factors in explaining poverty and [[dependency]] in [[Third World]]
- critics say domestic variables at the societal level of analysis are downplayed
- critics argue that [[dependency]] theory is too sweeping in its claims, blaming virtually every [[Third World]] political, economic, social problem on [[First World]]
	- lack of economic growth, social unrest, repressive gov'ts are all due to [[First World]]
- critics see structure as occupying too central a role, marginalizing agency in a *system-dominant* theoretical enterprise
#### 7.8.4 Theoretical Rigidity
- criticism of economics and *system dominance* as bases for causality
- individual cases are examined solely in terms of general theoretical constructs (e.g. [[capitalist world-system]])
	- a society's experiences are reduced to 1 or 2 concepts
	- major political, economic, social changes all fall under general explanatory logic of [[dependency]]
	- rather than modifying theories in light of empirical evidence or questions raised by case studies, [[Economic Structuralism]] uses cases studies only when they provide evidence to support the line of argument
		- no tension between theory and findings
		- little questioning of framework
		- unwillingness to consider alternative hypotheses
- criticisms are also leveled at [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]]
#### 7.8.5 Accounting for Anomalies
- [[Economic Structuralism]] has trouble accounting for non-Western countries that have been relatively successful economically (despite ups and downs)
	- [[Taiwan]], [[Brazil]], [[Singapore]], [[South Korea]]
	- [[Japan]], [[China]]
		- greatest success stories of any non-[[Europe]], non-[[North America]] country
- what has allowed these countries to escape abject poverty or make amazing strides forward?
	- neither are examples of autonomous development
	- benefited greatly from being enmeshed in [[capitalist world-system]]
- response: no theory or approach can be expected to explain everything
	- good theorizing points out and accounts for commonalities and what particular cases have in common
	- anomalies are expected and don't detract from utility of the theory if it can be adequately explained why a unique case doesn't fit the general pattern
- critics' rebuttal: [[Economic Structuralism]] ([[Wallerstein]]) simply group all anomalies under the concept of *semi-periphery*, a theoretically and empirically poorly defined concept
- ignored literature
	- what of the insights of authors who argue there's certain advantages to backwardness when a state is trying to catch up economically with more advanced states?
	- what of the work by scholars who emphasize importance of different types of state structures, political and social coalitions, shifting alliances in accounting for differential [[modernization]] success of various countries?
	- why is it ignored
		- [[Economic Structuralism]] refuses to give due consideration to domestic factors that aren't the result of capitalist dynamics
#### 7.8.6 Defining Alternatives and Science as Ideology
- argument: some [[Economic Structuralism]] has done a poor job in defining reasonable alternative world futures/strategies for states in [[Global South]] to pursue
	- e.g. what's meant by the call for autonomous development?
		- is this goal feasible?
		- how can this goal be achieved?
		- would redistributive policies of gov't be combined with political repression and abuse of power?
- critics: value preferences infuse [[Economic Structuralism]]
	- [[Economic Structuralism]] isn't apologetic for their [[normative]] commitment to fundamental changes in relations between North and South
		- such works are considered "more true because they assume that, by discerning which are the historical agents capable of propelling a process of transformation... these analyses thus grasp the meaning of historical movement and help to negate a given order of domination." #quote 
#### 7.8.6 Responses
- why does [[Economic Structuralism]] literature receive a great deal of criticism?
	1. criticism from scholars in mainstream [[North America]] social science
		- analyses based on [[Marxism]] insights are generally viewed with distrust in [[North America]] universities
			- leads to dismissal of [[Economic Structuralism]] literature
		- though some [[Economic Structuralism]] work is characterized as ideological, [[Economic structuralists]] say the criticisms are more on value preferences of the reviewer
			- ideological biases wrapped in cloak of supposedly objective criticisms
	2. [[dependency]] literature particularly is insufficiently empirical and fails to marshal evidence based on the canons of [[Positivism]] science
		- where is the data?
		- claim: empirical testing of selective hypotheses from [[dependency]] literature indicates these hypotheses don't hold up
		- rebuttal: that claim is based on assumption that [[rationalism]] or [[Positivism]] methods are the only means to comprehend reality
			- alternative epistemological premises/assumptions exist to question the value of formal hypothesis testing
				- often-exclusive focus on what can be measured, counted, added
		- even from [[Positivism]] approach to knowledge, most of [[world-system theory]] literature is empirical, cross-national focus
		- broader [[postcolonialism]] literature doesn't engage in statistical analysis
			- relies heavily on detailed case studies, in-depth archival research
- [[Economic Structuralism]] isn't mainstream
	- [[Realism]], [[Liberalism]], [[constructivism]] perspectives dominate [[IR]] literature
	- [[Economic Structuralism]] isn't unimportant or undeserving of attention
		- its contribution to increased understanding of the world around us shouldn't be seen only as a function of its degree of popularity at a specific point in time
		- provides very different, challenging, provocative perspective on world politics
- dominant scientific paradigms/research programs of a particular period tend to decay
	- what's considered to be heretical or at the fringes of normal science at one time may become the prevailing orthodoxy
- at minimum, [[Economic Structuralism]] perspective should encourage [[IR]] students to analyze [[Realism]], [[Liberalism]], and [[The English School]]'s [[rationalism]] images and assumptions on which they're based **critically**
- at maximum, [[Economic Structuralism]] provides challenging hypotheses, insights concerning dynamics and development of [[IR]] and world politics that still constitute an important image
