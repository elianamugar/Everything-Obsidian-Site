#### 10.7.0 Postmodernism – Major Assumptions
- human construction that relies on perception and cognitive processes influenced by prior understandings and meanings
	- influences what we see, choose to see, and mechanisms/methods we employ
	- language usage reflects embedded set of values that are an integral part of any culture
		- found in narratives people employ to depict understandings of their observations and experiences in the world around them
		- postmodernists engage in linguistic deconstruction of what's been said/written
			- employs discursive practices that emphasize reasoned argument
- postmodernist assumptions
	1. intimate connection between power and knowledge in analysis of IR
		- [[critical theory]], [[feminism]]
		- following arguments of [[Foucault]]
			- production of knowledge is a political process that has a mutually supportive relation to power
				- true in all aspects of political life where power is exercised (and IR)
				- not a [[Realism]] emphasis on material basis of power, but a focus on how actors and commentators attempt to impose authoritative interpretations on events
					- e.g. [[Cold War]], post-9/11 attacks
				- reality is structured by language
					- development of linguistic discourses results in a structure/system consisting of knowledge, subjects, objects
						- e.g. development of the concept [[sovereignty]] and associated terms and assumptions
							- heart of postmodern work in IR
							- state, [[anarchy]], borders, [[security]], human identities
	2. in terms of methodology, some follow [[Nietzsche]]'s lead in tracing over time the genealogy and significance of power-knowledge relations and such dominant discourses in IR as [[sovereignty]] and [[anarchy]]
		- knowledge is always conditioned by a particular time and place
		- understanding how particular interpretations of the past continue to guide current thinking and behavior also involves highlighting what's been excluded from historical narratives
			- [[Foucault]]: history is not about the uncovering of facts and building up a composite picture of the past, but rather exposing "the endlessly repeated play of dominations"
			- Foucault's studies of sanity, sexuality, punishment
				- explores how concepts we use commonly developed/were socially constructed over time
					- challenges generally accepted meanings these concepts purport to convey and power-based human relations they sustain
		- postmodernists following [[Derrida]]'s approach
			- sought both text and subtexts to deconstruct the meanings embedded in what we say/write/act
			- the need to understand the world as a whole
			- how different interpretations represent and constitute the world (an ontological position)
			- leads to [[post-structuralism]]
				- going beyond the accepted symbols/established structures that channel our understandings
				- stems from reaction to [[structuralism]] in [[France]] philosophical thought and [[semiotics]] (in [[anthropology]])
				- against any attempt to unify the social sciences with a single structuralist methodology based on identifying linguistic/cultural signs and differences
				- [[Lyotard]] and followers
					- rejects grand metanarratives employed to explain all the world in scientific terms
				- also against [[balance of power]] and other metanarratives in IR that masquerade as if they were scientifically-based theoretical explanations
	3. highlight competing historical perspectives, narratives, trajectories
		- following [[Nietzsche]]
			- no single historical truth, but rather multiple ones
				- no standard to judge them as no objective standard for truth exists
		- [[Kuhn]] and [[Toulmin]] use of paradigms/lenses that influence scientific work
			- assumes a real, discoverable objective reality exists, but we adopt different lenses that highlight and interpret "facts" in different ways
				- postmodernists believe these perspectives actually constitute the "real world"
			- basic ontological assumption: the identification of a historical narrative isn't simply the interpretation of a series of actions, but rather how "reality" is conferred upon events
		- postmodernists reject the idea that there's a knowable, single truth to be discovered
			- also rejects the idea that the only way to gain knowledge is through a positivist methodology
				- application restricted to conventional approaches favored by [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]]
			- e.g. lead on textual analysis and interpretation of art and theater
				- [[Christine Sylvester]] shows how IR influences missions of museums –> how visitors experience and interpret that art that's displayed
	4. [[normative]] commitment to idea that sovereign state is not the only means by which to organize political and social life
		- language of IR emphasizes states in a condition of [[anarchy]] reinforces the current exclusionary paradigm that precludes alternative forms of political and social organization
		- territorialization of political identity justifies political discourse and policies that affirm right of state officials to brutalize their subjects and restrict possibility of expanding [[democracy]] values
		- postmodernist issue with ontological perspective of [[Realism]] and [[Liberalism]]
			- privileges the state as the unit of analysis
				- makes it an ontological given for IR theorizing
		- in postmodernist understandings, states don't simply use force in an instrumental, means-ends calculation to achieve certain objectives
			- the role of violence is important even in the origins and constitution of the state itself
- postmodernists deconstruct the words and phrases we use
	- looks for underlying meanings/subtexts in our communications/narratives we adopt to depict our understandings
	- we are subjective creatures
		- humans are the source of knowledge we have about the world around us
		- our own identities are formed by the way we come to understand the world around us
			- the self is defined subjectively by each of us in relation to (an)other
