#### 1.2.1 Explanation and Prediction
- **Theory:** a way of making the world or some part of it more intelligible or better understood
	- Explanation from the positivist perspective involves establishing the phenomenon it explains as something that was to be expected in the circumstances in which it occurred.
	- **Carl G. Hempel:** "requirement of explanatory relevance"
		- Information is explanatory only if it "affords good grounds for believing that the phenomenon to be explained does, or did, indeed occur. This condition must be met if we are to be entitled to say: 'That explains it – the phenomenon in question was indeed to be expected under the circumstances."
	- **Laws:** a set of regularities or patterns that explain certain happenings at particular times.
		- Determined through **hypotheses**: proposition relating two or more variables (If *A*, then *B*)
			- e.g. If states engage in arms races, then the likelihood of war increases. (empirical test)
			- e.g. The stronger the leading sea power's relative capability position, the less likely it is that other great powers will balance against it. (most likely, least likely)
- **Generalizing/Covering-law Approach:** the primary research strategy that entails invoking laws in a scientific explanation.
	- Realists and liberals are rooted in this tradition, seeking covering laws of war, deterrence, cooperation, and economic integration.
- **Jack Snyder:** Why did the Cold War end peacefully?
	- Expansionists myths + timing of industrialization = understanding the type of collapse experienced by the USSR
- **Kenneth Waltz:** Theories explain laws.
	- A power-based structure of the international system that explains the behavior of states as the system's principal actors.
	- Testing a theory:
		- infer hypothesis from it
		- subject the hypotheses to experimental or observational tests
		- use the definitions of terms found in the theory being tested
		- eliminate or control perturbing variables not included in the theory under test
		- devise a number of distinct and demanding tests
		- if a test is not passed, ask whether the theory flunks completely needs repair and restatement, or requires a narrowing of the scope of its explanatory claims
- Reconstructive positivist strategy - no attempt is made to place the phenomenon under investigation into a larger class
	- The event is explained as the expected endpoint of a concrete historical sequence, not as an instance of category A, B, or C
- **William Wohlforth:** Why did the Cold War end peacefully?
	- Details the sequence of events leading up to the collapse of the USSR
		- The behavior to be explained emerges from this analysis and historical reconstruction
- **Theory is necessary and unavoidable when it comes to explaining and attempting to foresee or predict future outcomes.**
- **Theory** (new definition)**:** an intellectual construct composed of a set of interrelated propositions that help one to identify or select facts and interpret them, thus facilitating explanation and prediction concerning the regularities and recurrences or repetitions of observed phenomena.
	- Interest in cultural tendencies
	- "Fitting pieces into a larger whole makes theory-building analogous to puzzle-solving."
		- War poses a most important puzzle for IR theorists
		- **Why does the phenomenon persist even though wars are extremely costly in terms of lives and treasure lost?**
#### 1.2.2 Abstraction and Application
- Policy-relevant theorists may have explicit purposes that stem from the value preferences of the theorists (reducing the likelihood of war, curbing arms races)
- Theory is a way to become engaged in an increasingly globalized world that goes beyond today's headlines
- Theory can help cut through the blizzard of information we're faced with daily
- **Waltz:**
	- "from theory all else follows"
	- "theory explains and may at times anticipate or predict outcomes"
	- "a political theory, if it is any good, not only explains international outcomes, but also provides clues to situations and actions that may produce more of the desired and fewer of the undesired ones"
- There is nothing so practical as a good theory.
#### 1.2.3 Levels of Analysis
- **Levels of Analysis:** individual or group, state and society, "system" as a whole
- **Units of Analysis:** states, organization, individuals or groups, classes, other entities
- **Dependent Variable:** what one is trying to explain/study (e.g. outbreak of war)
- **Independent Variables:** factors we suspect as being causally related to what we're trying to explain
- **Waltz:** an exploration into the causes of war
	- **Individual:** Was the cause of war (dependent variable) to be found in the nature of individuals? (Are humans innately aggressive?) 
	- **State and Society:** Or in the nature of states and societies? (Are some types of states more aggressive than others?) 
	- **International:** Or in the nature of the international system of states? (Is anarchy a "permissive" cause of war, there being no obstacle to the use of force by sovereign states in a world without central governance?)
- **David J. Singer:** one's choice of a particular level of analysis determines what one will and will not see.
	- the international system
		- distribution of power among states, geography, technology, and other factors
		- the capitalist world system - economic structuralists
		- international/world society composed of rules, norms, states, nonstate actors - English School
	- the state
		- treated as a unified actor
		- domestic/national society - [[democratic]], [[authoritarian]], etc.
	- groups
		- bureaucratic politics and group dynamics
		- the domain of social psychology
	- individuals
		- psychology, perception, belief systems
- Various levels used to explain the foreign policy behavior of states (dependent variable)
- *Agent-structure* problem - difficulties of developing a theory that successfully meets both demands
	- Two claims about social life
		1. human agency is the only moving force behind the actions, events, and outcomes of the social world
		2. human agency can be realized only in concrete historical circumstances that condition the possibilities for action and influence its course
	- Karl Marx: "People make history, but not in conditions of their own choosing." ([[Marxism]])
	- The claims impose two demands:
		1. acknowledge and account for the power of agents
		2. they recognize the causal relevance of exogenous or "structural factors" (conditions of action as decision makers understand them)
