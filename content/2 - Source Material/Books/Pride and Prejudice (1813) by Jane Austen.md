# Thoughts
I will flesh out my thoughts after I let the book linger in my head a little longer, but I thought it was alright and very ahead of its time! Jane Austen deserves all the laurels she's gotten. I do think the story dragged around the middle-end for me, but that might have to do with the fact that I know how the ending goes. I'm sure the stakes were high back in the day to keep people on their toes, but for me, I couldn't give a damn about the stakes. I think this tends to happen when I read most YA books, where the stakes feel like they're important, when they're really not. So, what I gathered is that *Pride and Prejudice* was probably the YA novel of the 1800s! There are some topics that I do plan on verbally talking about, then I will transfer them to some coherent notes. I will just type out what I wrote out on my talking sheet:

- Why did I read Pride and Prejudice?
	- To be honest, I was looking for a lighter "girl" book to read during my breaks from reading The Brothers Karamazov by Fyodor Dostoevsky and, although I usually read a contemporary to take a break from a classic, something in me really wanted to read either *Pride and Prejudice* or *Wuthering Heights*. Since the 2005 film adaptation is leaving Netflix at the end of this month and there's a new 2026 limited series adaptation coming to Netflix, I thought it was fitting to finally read it now.
- Appreciation for the contemporary novel
- British polite society
	- lends itself to British comedy regarding restraint + suppression of language, leading the characters to be forced into subtext
	- observations on British economics
- focus on duty + honor + manners as respectable
- opinions on Jane Austen
