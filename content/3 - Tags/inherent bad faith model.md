A specific theory in political psychology describing how a decision-maker views their opponent's actions as entirely malicious, filtering all information through this belief.

a political psychology theory developed by Ole Holsti, often associated with John Foster Dulles, which posits that an opponent is inherently hostile and untrustworthy. All contrary evidence is dismissed as propaganda or tricks, reinforcing a rigid belief system where the opponent's "good" actions are seen as deceptive and "bad" actions as true intentions
