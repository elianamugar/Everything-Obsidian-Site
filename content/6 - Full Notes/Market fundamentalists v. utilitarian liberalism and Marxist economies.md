# Market fundamentalists v. utilitarian liberalism and Marxist economies

In a sense, [[market fundamentalists]] believe that if the wealth is accumulated at the “top of the chain,” then that wealth will trickle down to the rest of society. Utilitarian liberalism gives too much power and trust to the rest of the people since wealth is scattered around society and not concentrated into one group. It creates a lack of control over where the money gets distributed. Marxism altogether doesn’t have an inherent controller for the money (besides the government). The workers can pretend to work and the government can pretend to give money, causing many issues within the economy. There’s no one to keep each other in check, which is what market fundamentalists argue.

In order for market fundamentalism to work, there has to be trust at the “top of the chain” to actually distribute their wealth. But if the top of the chain (ex: the 1%) don’t distribute their wealth evenly, the wealth stays at the top and never trickles down, essentially creating a bigger gap of wealth between the richest person and the poorest person. Yet, market fundamentalists believe their approach will be better at providing the greatest happiness because the laws of supply and demand will happen _naturally_. And if these laws of supply and demand occur naturally, then it will _naturally_ trickle down to society, and contribute to the greatest happiness for the greatest number of people. But ultimately, this idea was similar to the idea of “[[Social Darwinism]]” (survival of the fittest). Market fundamentalists believed that if there are the haves and have nots in a society, then the have nots will have to fend for themselves, because that was determined _naturally_.
