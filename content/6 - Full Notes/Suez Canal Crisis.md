# Suez Canal Crisis

In the late 1860s, a canal was built through Egypt to connect the Mediterranean and the Red Seas. This was efficient for most traders--they wouldn’t have to go αround them, they could just cut through the Indian Ocean. As a result, the canal became eye-candy for most western nations for trade. The Suez Canal was a private company controlled by UK and French shareholders, so that every ship that passed through, they would have to pay a toll. In short, the shareholders profited. At this point in time, Egypt was a UK colony.

In 1952, the King of Egypt was overthrown by the military, led by Gamal Abdel Nasser. The King was unpopular due to his weakness and his puppetry by the British. Nasser, as the new leader of Egypt, establishes a military dictatorship which the Egyptians support. Nasser’s goal: get the UK out of Egypt, claim Egyptian independence, and claim the Suez Canal under Egyptian territory. As part of this goal, Nasser’s ideology comes to fruition: rid the Arab world of outside western powers.

On May 14, 1948, Israel was established as a country and after WWII, European Jews were in Israel. Palestinian Arabs also lived in the area, so this seemed like part of the Arab world was being threatened by European invaders. Nasser provides support to the Palestinian Arabs, which threatens Israel.

In September 1954, the French-Algerian War broke out. After years of being ruled by the French minority, the Algerian Arabs (majority) attacked the French. The French have already faced many devastating losses to Hitler in WWII and the Indochinese colonies, so the French can’t face humiliation again.
