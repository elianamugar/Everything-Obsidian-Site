# Metropolis and Brazil - the cynical realities and their future implications

Both _Metropolis_ and _Brazil_ are pessimistic towards industrial work culture, but _Metropolis_ is more idealistic about a hopeful future that does not let machinery run society’s course, while _Brazil_ believes living a future life outside of mechanical work is merely fantasy.

Directed by Fritz Lang in 1927, _Metropolis_ is a film about Freder, who lives above the underground world of workers and whose father is in-charge of those workers. Although against his father's wishes, Freder decides to help the workers by maintaining a machine in place of an exhausted worker. He realizes how difficult the task is, then sees the mechanical monster consuming workers, symbolic of people needing the machines and fueling the machines to uphold society. As people become more fixated on technological innovation, the film argues that people quickly lose touch with their own humanity. This not only occurs in the underground world, but also in the posh upperworld. When Maria, a teacher, gets cloned by the robot, the clone seduces the men in the upper-class entertainment room. The men lust for Maria in an animal-like way. Then, as the story progresses to the rising action, the men fight over each other in a primal state for the beautiful, but fake, Maria. This represents the people losing their humanity by becoming slaves to their desires, which the machines are designed to produce. Both groups have lost their humanity in service of machines, despite being on different ends of the social pyramid.

Then, at the end, Freder serves as the “mediator” to unite his father and Grot, a worker, after everyone destroys the machines—the common enemy. The ending caption reads, “THE MEDIATOR BETWEEN HEAD AND HANDS MUST BE THE HEART!” and everyone is happy. This is a very optimistic ending, symbolic of the unity of the masters and workers of the machine, the head and the hands. This shows that in working together, everyone in the social hierarchy can achieve a future that is not reliant on machinery and work. Instead, they can work together and strive for a society that focuses on the betterment of people rather than industrialism. Lang’s nostalgia for a machineless society is depicted through his interpretation of the future because he decides to unite the workers with the upper-class, rather than people with technology, which would be a bleaker message than Lang’s intended one.

In another film about industrialism, directed by Terry Gilliam in 1985, _Brazil_ explores bureaucratic and mechanical daily life through protagonist Sam Lowry, an unambitious bureaucrat working at the Ministry of Information who is infatuated with a woman in his dreams. In Lowry’s first dream, he flies over an open land but is suddenly bombarded by buildings rising from the ground—his first enemy. Then, through other dream sequences, Lowry fights enemies of whimsical bosses and soulless people, representative of those working in the bureaucratic system. In one scene, Lowry slays the giant samurai boss, a metaphor for technology. When Lowry reveals himself to be the samurai, it shows Lowry’s submission to industrialism and that he is fighting himself. He struggles to decide whether or not he should stay in the mechanical reality or flee, leaving Lowry trapped in a repetitive lifestyle where he simply goes through the motions with minimal hope to live in his fantasy world. By seeing industrialism as villains, the film emphasizes that it is evil for society. 

In an action-packed scene at the end, Lowry escapes from the Ministry of Information with the woman of his dreams, but it is revealed that this is only a dream. Although Lowry believes he can control his own destiny to prove to himself that the bureaucracy and his job don’t control him, it is all a farce. Doctor Jack Lint, the one who performs the lobotomy on Sam Lowry, says he is a “lost cause” in a delusional and blissful insanity. This symbolizes that no one can escape the mechanical way of life, let alone an industrial society. Ultimately, _Brazil_ establishes that an optimistic future of happiness is unobtainable and that society should be pessimistic about the industrial future. Gilliam, like Lang, is nostalgic for a simpler time and wants to return to a life not dictated by technology. Yet, Gilliam is more cynical, practically stating that the future reality cannot head backwards into the past. Technology will play a role in the future, and it cannot be destroyed, so Gilliam establishes that it is only a dream to believe that there will be a happy ending to industrialism and work culture.

Both _Metropolis_ and _Brazil_ display negativity towards industrialism and believe technology can destroy humanity if society does not heed to its problems and take precautions against its mentally destructive lifestyle. The films depict the common cynical aspect of industrialism, such as becoming automatons and living their lives as simply cogs in a machine. Also, both films are nostalgic for a time when industrial technology did not exist because of how much it deadens and numbs society. Although _Metropolis_ and _Brazil_ criticize the perfunctory way of life during their time periods, they differ in their future outlooks. In _Metropolis_, there is hope for people to unite, despite the technology, to improve on society for future generations. This is depicted by the destruction of the machinery, the resolve of people in differing social statuses, and the general happiness of the people. On the other hand, _Brazil_ the message of the film’s ending is that hopes and dreams are futile and unattainable in a society that requires workers to commit their lives to the job—nothing more, nothing less. Nostalgia plays a role in both the movies, pertinent to the directors’ time periods, but they both take different approaches in how that affects the future. Lang believes that society can succeed if people focus on humanity and use technology as a tool, rather than let technology control them. In contrast, Gilliam believes humanity is doomed to live in the inevitable mechanical lifestyle and a conformed society is imminent.
