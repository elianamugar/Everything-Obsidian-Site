# The Captain as Job

Job (in the Book of Job) is a good and righteous man who suffers unbearable tragedies. The book he is in addresses the problem of the suffering of the innocent.

In The Brothers Karamazov, we see the captain losing his son (the sick Ilusha) and he cries out "If I forget thee, Jerusalem, may my tongue..." ([[The Brothers Karamazov (1880) by Fyodor Dostoevsky#510]]). This is taken directly from Psalm 137.5-6, when the Israelites lament during their Babylonian exile, yearning for the return of Zion (Jerusalem). When the captain/Ilusha's dad laments for his son, after Ilusha tells his father to find another boy to replace him, he cries out the infamous line from Psalm 137.5-6, meaning "if I forget all that is precious to me, if I let anything take its place, then..." he would rather have his tongue cut.

The captain is described by Dostoevsky as an innocent man who has done nothing wrong, yet suffers at the loss of his child. Through this, Dostoevsky parallels the captain with Job, bringing forth the metaphysical problems that have functioned at an analytical or anecdotal level, whether from philosophy or the Bible.
