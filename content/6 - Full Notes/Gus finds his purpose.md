# Gus finds his purpose

Along the way to Montana, Gus found his purpose — marry Clara. Originally, his purpose to leaving Lonesome Dove was to make Call happy. In other words, Gus left Lonesome Dove for Call's own purpose. Therefore, Call's purpose became Gus's purpose. After several near-death experiences and witnessing the deaths of his fellow Hat Creek Cattle Company members, Gus realizes he should go back and find his Clara, the one who got away. 

Gus goes through an interesting character arc. He is the epitome of [[Existentialism]]. While he acknowledges the harsh and often meaningless nature of the frontier world, he doesn't believe life is devoid of meaning. He argues that humans must *create* their own meaning through friendship, pleasure (part of his [[hedonism]]), and living passionately. So once he finally reaches his breakthrough and discovers *a* purpose to call his own – he sets out to find his purpose, to marry Clara.

It's important to emphasize that Gus found *his* own purpose, rather than find *a* purpose (which was to make Call happy). Once he found it, he went for it. And though he went for it, ultimately he gets rejected by Clara. He becomes purposeless again, still harping on the fact that he wishes he got married. When discussing whether they'll see Indians in Montana, they have this conversation: 

> "I guess we'll meet them soon enough," Call said.
  "If not too soon. They may come biling out of them hills and wipe us out any day. Then we'd have enough meat to last the winter. They'd be rich Indians, and we'd be dead fools."
  "Fools for doing what?" Call said. "This country's looking better all the time."
  "Fools for living the lives we've lived," Augustus said.
  "I've enjoyed mine," Call said. "What was wrong with yours?"
  "I should have married again," Augustus said.

Gus reaches Montana with Call and the rest of the crew and he dies there. That ending fits him. He had his purpose, which was to marry Clara. He didn't fulfill it and so he fulfilled Call's purpose. And once that was done, he finished his mission in life, which was to finish strong and be free.
