# The modern discourse of "doing it for the aesthetic"

In *The Drama* (2016), which I talk more about in-depth [here](obsidian://open?vault=Everything&file=6%20-%20Full%20Notes%2FDo%20your%20thoughts%20or%20actions%20define%20you%3F) and [here](obsidian://open?vault=Everything&file=6%20-%20Full%20Notes%2FDoes%20the%20purpose%20of%20your%20actions%20define%20how%20legitimate%20your%20actions%20are%3F), Emma explains to Charlie why she had the initial idea to do a school mass shooting: for the aesthetic. I talked a bit already about how "doing it for the aesthetic" in Emma's case was more of a grander message of belonging to a community, whereas her previous state was this terrible alienation in [this mini-essay](obsidian://open?vault=Everything&file=6%20-%20Full%20Notes%2FBlack-and-white%20dichotomy%20for%20nuanced%20situations). But I think this topic of aestheticism in today's age is closely linked to [[Capitalism]].

Many video essays have popped up on my YouTube homepage feed where people discuss "doing it for the aesthetic" leading to overconsumption, but I think shopping for a certain aesthetic is just one level to it. Aesthetics can show itself in actions. The most common example I can think of is *smoking*. Personally, I cringe every time a dear friend or someone close to me mentions *smoking* just because *it looks cool*. It's terribly vain and, personally, I don't think it makes them look cool, but anyway. There's lots of things considered "bad", in this case, bad for one's health, but people do it anyway. Same with drinking or doing drugs. There's an aesthetic which makes people for-go the negative effects.

In *The Drama* (2016), the film explores this cringe idea of "aesthetic" versus reality, outlined in the scene of young Emma having to re-record her message in the assumption that she went through with the shooting. A user on YouTube commented on the situation perfectly:

> @margarethansen7490: \[It] \[f]eels like the “slapstick” parts are meant to make people glamorize the aesthetic less, show how cringe and silly it really is, but still showcases the devastating impacts when that aesthetic is taken to its full conclusion. It takes the gas out of how horrible the shooters are, since the “glory” and infamy is all those idiotic kids want.

In this day and age with the rise of social media among my generation, the people I'm surrounded by have become a bit more vain. And my impression upon everyone else in my generation is that they're even more vain. It's sad to watch people crumble before my eyes because [[Capitalism]] is working its voodoo magic. Of course, I'm all for people feeling beautiful, but I think there's some twisted pleasure that this capitalist system has programmed us to feel when we get that dopamine hit and has taken advantage of people's vanity and curated this idea of grouped aesthetics to have people *want* to fit in. Maybe more than a want, more of an *urgent need*.
