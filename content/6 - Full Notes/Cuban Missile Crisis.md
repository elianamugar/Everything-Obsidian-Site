# Cuban Missile Crisis

During JFK’s presidency, he tried to roll back communism through the [[Bay of Pigs]]. In 1959, Cuba fell to Fidel Castro who overthrew the pro-American regime. Castro’s allies were communist, but Castro himself wasn’t communist yet. Eventually Castro became communist and Eisenhower cut off trade with Cuba, asking Dulles (head of the CIA) to repeat the situation in Guatemala--train guerrilla forces in Cuba to overthrow the government (a secret). In 1960, Castro reached out for Khrushchev’s help and Khrushchev did. During the 1960 election, JFK condemned Eisenhower for doing nothing to rid Castro (which was false, it was just a secret), so when JFK won, JFK had to continue Eisenhower’s plan. From mid-April to May 1961, CIA ships and the Cuban guerrilla army landed in the Bay of Pigs. The JFK administration assumes that the Cuban people don’t want communism, so once the guerrilla army lands, the Cuban people will unite with the army to overthrow the communist regime. In actuality, Castro was popular and Cubans supported the Castro regime. As a result, the Bay of Pigs was a disaster: the Cuban Air Force was barely damaged and Cubans rallied Castro. JFK is humiliated and Khrushchev perceives JFK as weak and moronic. So, in June 1961 at the [[Vienna Summit]], Khrushchev pressured JFK to leave West Berlin.

From June-Aug 1961, many East Berliners fled to West Berlin because they were worried about what would happen after the Vienna Summit. JFK supports this heavily, but this damages Khrushchev since East Germany was losing its young, educated people of the future. In August 1961, the leadership of East Germany pressured Khrushchev to shut the border between the 2 Berlins to stop the migration. On August 13, 1961, the barbed wiring of the Berlin Wall started being placed. JFK can’t do anything to tear it down because it’s on the East Berlin line, so the barbed wire eventually becomes concrete. In October 1961, there’s a kerfuffle between the East German guards and Americans. The Americans don’t want to hand over their papers to the East German guards or else that would give East Germany recognition. Tensions escalate, JFK orders tanks, and so does Khrushchev. By December 1961, Soviet guards came to check the passports. As a result, Mao condemns Khrushchev for being weak and asserts himself as leader of the communist world. This shows the Sino-Soviet tensions amongst the communist world. Also, within his own party, Khrushchev is being condemned  for his weakness. To save his skin (life or death situation), Khrushchev looks to Cuba to place missiles and close the US-USSR missile gap, which the USSR was losing in. After all, to Khrushchev’s reasoning, if the US has missiles in Turkey, why can’t the USSR place missiles in Cuba? The difference was that the US openly placed missiles in Turkey while Khrushchev kept the missile operation in Cuba a secret, which signaled the possible deployment of USSR missiles.

On October 14, 1962, US U-2 planes flew over Cuba and spot missiles. On October 15, JFK was informed of the missiles, which led to the beginning of the Cuban Missile Crisis. JFK doesn’t reveal this to the US public and wants to understand the options first. One thing was certain--JFK couldn’t allow the missiles to remain in Cuba because of his failures and humiliation with the Bay of Pigs and the Vienna Summit. JFK has to draw the line and establish that Khrushchev has limits as to where he can place his missiles. JFK’s Flexible Response options were 1) do nothing (which would make JFK look weak), 2) take out the missiles with a surgical strike (which was difficult) and 3) set up a blockade around Cuba. The best option was to set up a blockade. The problem with “blockade” was that it was an act of war, so the JFK administration renamed it to “quarantine”. On October 22, 1962, JFK revealed to the US public of the existence of USSR nuclear missiles off the coast of Florida. One USSR submarine heard the speech. On this submarine, the captain wanted to deploy the missiles. Luckily, Vasily Arkhipov (commander) vetoed the decision because of his past experiences with the K-19 nuclear accident, so the missiles weren’t deployed. On October 26, Khrushchev sent a message to the US State Department. Khrushchev says he’s willing to make a trade: if JFK publicly announces a hands-off policy against Cuba and publicly pledge to not overthrow Castro, then Khrushchev will remove the missiles from Cuba. But then Khrushchev sends a second message on October 27, 1962: if JFK followed the former request AND took out US missiles in Turkey, then Khrushchev will remove the missiles from Cuba. These 2 different deals were confusing and increased tensions during the Crisis. JFK was hesitant because trading missiles were dangerous--there was nothing to guarantee that Khrushchev wouldn’t do this gain. Yet, JFK’s advisor said that he has to take the risk: publicly offer Khrushchev the Castro pledge and hands-off policy promise but privately offer to take out the US missiles from Turkey (as long as Khrushchev upholds to keep the secret that the missile removal from Turkey was related to the Cuban Missile Crisis, or else the US will place the missiles back in Turkey). Khrushchev accepted.

On October 28, 1962, the Cuban Missile Crisis ended, making JFK look like a hero and Khrushchev the loser. JFK wasn’t proud of it--he was one mistake away from the brink of nuclear war. The biggest issue from the Cuban Missile Crisis was Khrushchev’s loss of control, which was a danger that JFK was unaware of. The JFK administration wasn’t aware of the command system the USSR had in place for missiles. They weren’t aware that Khrushchev doesn’t have control over missiles once it leaves the USSR. Khrushchev lost control of missiles on the ground of Cuba to Cuban generals AND submarine missiles to the captain, commander, and crew of the submarines.
