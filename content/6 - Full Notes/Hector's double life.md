# Hector's double life
​
Before the Trojan war breaks out, Hector, a Trojan soldier, visits his home to see his wife and son for possibly the last time. During this vulnerable moment, Hector unravels himself, revealing deeper feelings that make up his character.

​To the Trojans, Hector has always been seen as an almighty fighter. He’s been praised as “the lone defense of Troy” (478), but throughout the text, we see a deeper, more sensitive side to him. From the reading, Hector is revealed as a dedicated father and husband. When describing Hector’s interaction with his family before he leaves to fight the war, Homer writes, “Great man of war breaking into a broad smile, gaze fixed on his son, in silence” (479-480). From the line, it’s clear that he has an emotional attachment to his son, almost in complete awe and admiration. 

Although he’s depicted as a warrior and family man, his underlying character of being a prideful fighter comes from his obligation to battle for his state in the act of patriotism. After Andromache, his wife, begs him to stay, Hector replies, “But I would die of shame to face the men of Tray and the Trojan women trailing their long robes if I would shrink from battle now a coward” (523-525). Hector sounds somewhat egotistical, accompanied by pride and arrogance, as he feels like he has more of a responsibility to his state rather than his family. This is not the case—he simply submits to fate.

Towards the end of the passage, Hector reassures to Andromache that no one has ever escaped fate, so if he dies, so be it. Then, he continues to tell Andromache that she, too, has to continue on with her life (577-588). Hector’s facade of being calm for his family changes when he delivers his adrenaline-powered statement: “Come, now for attack!... Zeus will ever let us raise the winebowl of freedom high in our halls, high to the gods of cloud and sky who live forever- once we drive these Argives geared for battle out of Troy!” (620-631). From these two lines, Hector unravels the two roles he plays: a family man and a prized fighter. We sense Hector’s deep devotion to his wife, his son, and his brother, and ultimately decides to commit to a bigger cause—saving his nation.

Hector’s quick transformation uncovers his character. He’s a man who is attempting to balance his home life and life on the battlefield for his nation, who has a burden on his shoulders, and has to end up submitting to a higher power, or fate, in order to keep his sanity. So, it is revealed that Hector is a complex character stuck in a tragedy of having to fight for his nation and leaving his family because he feels obligated to save his brother Paris.
