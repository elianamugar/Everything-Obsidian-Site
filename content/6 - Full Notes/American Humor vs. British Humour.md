# American Humor vs. British Humour
I prefer British humor to American humor, but why is that?

British Reddit user "h0m3r" describes their opinion on the overarching cultural difference of *sincerity*. They say: "In the US, straight talking is very important and double entendre in our speech – \[British people are] much less direct and it manifests in comedy." They go on to list three ways:
1. "British humour uses **irony** a lot more than American, in the same way \[British people] use irony and also understatement in \[their] everyday speech."

I think this is true, given my research into how the history of British society and restrictive English etiquette has pervaded its way into modern-day British comedy.

2. "Many \[British] classic comedy characters are *losers*. People who are pathetic, nasty or sad. I think \[British people] relate to comedy that's based on imperfection. In the US, I think generally their comedy characters are supposed to be *likeable*, even if they're not heroic, and \[British people] don't find that so important.... I think it comes from the British tendency to *self-deprecate* which again comes from a less direct way of acting in society – \[British people] downplay \[their] successes."

I think [[Fleabag (2016) by Phoebe Waller-Bridge]] is a key example of this. The main character, Fleabag, does not seem like someone the audience should like and that's the *exact* reason why the whole show is comedic. We see her trials and tribulations as she represents the epitome of humanity's struggle to figure out the meaning of life and purpose.

3. "...British people really pride themselves on their sense of humour and it seems less culturally important in the US to be funny. I think this comes again from the fact that we use humour rather than talking directly to each other – \[British people] banter with friends rather than compliment them, for example."

This point I'm not really sure about as I haven't really been witness to it. I'm leaning towards disagreeing with this point because, like everything else in America, comedy is a big money machine here. Comedy shows are very big here and I've heard many anecdotes about comedians trying to make it big in America, so from personal experience, it's hard to say that humor is less culturally important in the US than the UK.

I think the reason why I consume more British comedies is because the nuances, wittiness, and sarcasm that emerges through indirect communication (very prevalent in British humor) appeals more to me than the direct forwardness of American humor.
