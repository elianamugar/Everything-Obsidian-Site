# Kolya contradicts his Socialist beliefs

At the young age of thirteen, Kolya tells his friend Smurov (who he deems as less intelligent) that he is a Socialist: When Smurov innocently asks, "What is a Socialist?" Kolya replies: "That's when all are equal and all have property in common, there are no marriages, and everyone has any religion and laws he likes best." ([[The Brothers Karamazov (1880) by Fyodor Dostoevsky#480]])

This is great and all, until he converses with Alyosha, saying: "Do you know, what I like best is that you treat me like an equal. But we are not equals, no, we are not. You are better!" ([[The Brothers Karamazov (1880) by Fyodor Dostoevsky#507]]) If anything, through Kolya, this demonstrates that Alyosha is more of a Socialist than himself! What's funny is this idea that Kolya is trying so desperately to be identified as something else that's more mature and older rather than his mere age of thirteen.

Kolya contradicts himself, which represents his crisis of identity – something he's so desperately trying to pin down himself so that other people can take him seriously as a person (mature, maybe?) instead of just a kid.
