# Peace with honor in the Vietnam War

Note: SV(A) = South Vietnam (Army); NV(A) = North Vietnam (Army)

​The definition of a “peace with honor” was to 1) guarantee an independent, non-communist South Vietnam and 2) guarantee that every NVA soldier leaves SV. The 3 methods Nixon and Kissinger established were Triangulation, Vietnamization, and cutting the Ho Chi Minh trail. Triangulation was the US attempt at taking advantage of the Sino-Soviet tensions in the communist world to remove NV pressure on SV, which would help solve the 2nd part of the peace with honor. Vietnamization was the process of withdrawing US troops from SV, while still sending supplies so that SV could do the majority of the fighting instead of the US, which would help the 1st part of the peace with honor. If there was a strong SV, then the NV can’t defeat SV, so NV would be forced to leave, which also solved the 2nd part of peace with honor. Cutting the Ho Chi Minh Trail would cut off NVA supplies in SV, which would force the NVA to leave South Vietnam and enforce the 2nd part of peace with honor.

​In order to get the NVA out of SV, Nixon and Kissinger looked at why and how NV was able to stay in SV. The answer was the Ho Chi Minh trail, in which supplies would be sent down from communist powers to sustain the NV forces. In April 1970, Nixon and Kissinger attempted to cut off the Ho Chi Minh Trail by secretly sending US and SV troops into Cambodia. The [[Tonkin Gulf Resolutions]], which guaranteed presidential blanket power to do whatever they needed to do to protect SV, didn’t mention anything about Laos and Cambodia, so this was very illegal. Because NV had spies, they already knew of the Cambodia invasion. As a response, the NVA stayed away from the trails. The US and SVA couldn’t attack the NVA on the trails, but still destroyed supply depots and damaged the trails. When the US public found out about Cambodia, Nixon was condemned by Congress and had to withdraw US troops after 8 weeks. In June 1970, the Cambodia invasion was over. Cambodians were infuriated with the Cambodian military dictatorship because they felt vulnerable, so support grew for the [[Khmer Rouge]], a communist guerrilla group. As a result, the Khmer Rouge got supplies and support from the USSR and China through the Ho Chi Minh Trails. Now, the Ho Chi Minh trail has to go through Laos, which led to the heavier use of Laotian portions of the trail.

​Nixon and Kissinger now have to cut off the trails in Laos. In February 1971, Nixon tested out his Vietnamization idea by letting the SVA invade Laos with US support to avoid trouble with Congress and the US public. This tests if SV is ready to be independent and to see if SV can defend itself. In a secret operation, the SVA headed to Laos, but this scheme was discovered (again) by NV spies. In March 1971, the SVA lost to NV forces, proving that Vietnamization was a failure. On top of that, the cutting of the Ho Chi Minh Trails was a failure. 2 out of the 3 methods were failing. SV couldn’t stand on its own, which doesn’t solve the 1st part of peace with honor and the Ho Chi Minh Trail is still open, which doesn’t help with the 2nd part of peace with honor. As a result of the Laos invasion, Laotians couldn’t trust their government to protect them, so support grew for the [[Pathet Lao]], a communist guerrilla group. With the situation in Vietnam worsening, Nixon and Kissinger looked to Triangulation to try and resolve it.

​Nixon and Kissinger’s awareness of Sino-Soviet tensions allowed them to benefit from it. China and the USSR had tensions amongst each other, showing how the split in the communist world really was. Nixon and Kissinger’s idea was that, if the US showed support toward one communist side, the other communist side would feel pressure. The issue here was that there were no US-China relations, so the US had to work on building that. By some chance in April 1971, ping-pong diplomacy developed after a friendly ping-pong match was established in China between a Chinese and American table tennis player. In late 1971, the US withdrew its veto of communist China joining the UN Security Council, replacing Taiwan. In February 1972, Nixon arrived in China and established the Shanghai Communiqué between the US and China. Although Mao was uncomfortable with the idea, Chinese foreign minister Zhou Enlai pushed for US-Chinese relations. The question at this event surrounded the situation of what to do with Taiwan. Nixon clarified that the reunification of “one China” must be peaceful, but nowhere in the Chinese version of the Shanghai Communiqué is it established that the reunification must be peaceful, only in the US version. In a drastic move, the US recognized communist China as the legitimate one, which caused Brezhnev of the USSR to worry--exactly what Nixon and Kissinger were counting on. Through these series of events, US-China relations started to grow.

​After the US-China visit, Brezhnev approached Nixon and Kissinger, inviting Nixon to Moscow. Brezhnev and Nixon would open up about nuclear discussions. In May 1972, Nixon visited Moscow and finalized the details of the SALT I agreement (temporary 5 year agreement to limit how many missiles each side can have) and an ABM treaty (imposed limits to how many defense systems against missiles each side can have). During this visit, Nixon pushd Brezhnev that US-China relations were getting better because China was cooperating with pressuring NV to leave, pushing Triangulation. Because Brezhnev doesn’t want a US-China alliance (too much pressure for Brezhnev), Nixon suggested that if Brezhnev helped pressure NV for negotiations to end the Vietnam War, then the US wouldn’t gang up on the USSR and the US won’t band together with China. At this moment in time, Triangulation worked--US-USSR relations improved, which worried Zhou Enlai. Now, China felt pressured to respond, so China alsopressured NV for negotiations to end the Vietnam War. Triangulation was very successful. Because of Nixon’s visits to the USSR and China, Cold War tensions dropped, which led to the detente period. This was a piece of Triangulation which Nixon and Kissinger anticipated if Triangulation was successful.

​In Spring 1972, US troops were still in Vietnam. Nixon’s original platform for his first term presidency to get out of Vietnam had to extend into his second term. One thing that Nixon and Kissinger overlooked was that the Sino-Soviet competition’s winning prize was NV because NV had fought the US for victory. As of now, NV was the hero of the communist world. When China and the USSR pressure NV, NV felt pressured. As a result, NV counter-triangulated: NV threatened USSR by saying there will be a strong NV-Chinese allyship and NV threatened China by saying there would be a strong NV-USSR allyship. As a result, the USSR and China don’t cut their supplies, which cannot guarantee Nixon’s peace of honor. Triangulation ultimately failed because it was used against the US. Now, all 3 methods were seen as failures.

​In January 1973, the [[Paris Peace Accords]] took place. In the treaty, US troops, air power, support, etc. would leave Vietnam in the next few months, active fighting would cease, there would be elections for a new SV government, and a prisoner exchange would happen between NV and SV. Nothing in the treaty mentioned NV troops leaving SV--NV troops were allowed to stay where they were in SV. The idea was that the NVA would withdraw from SV after the elections, but it wasn’t an NV requirement to do so. As a result, this wasn’t a peace of honor--SV wouldn’t be independent and the NV troops were still in SV. Nixon and Kissinger were accused of not keeping their promise. After the US withdrew their forces from SV, NV took over SV with the Ho Chi Minh Campaign in December 1974. In April 1975, under the NV communist country, both halves unified after the fall of Saigon (SV’s capital). Soon after, in April 1975 and December 1975, Cambodia became communist to the Khmer Rouge (Chinese ally) and Laos became communist to the Pathet Lao (Soviet ally). This was the ultimate failure of the peace of honor--Nixon and Kissinger couldn’t uphold both parts of the peace of honor.
