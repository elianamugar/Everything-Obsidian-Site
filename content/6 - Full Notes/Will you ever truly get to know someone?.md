# Will you ever truly get to know someone?

In The Drama (2026), Emma and Charlie are a week away from their wedding. Leading up to it, Charlie learns something shocking about Emma: she almost committed a mass shooting. How all of this unfolded could've been done in private, but alas, it wasn't. Charlie spirals and doesn't quite know how to broach the subject, or even talk about it with Emma, who shuts down every attempt in having the conversation with Charlie. Regardless of the communication flaws, this begs the question: will you ever *truly* get to know someone? 

My short answer: no. You will never *truly* walk the shoes of someone else. You can only approximate how someone else has lived their life, but you didn't exactly live *their* life. No matter how much Emma explains to Charlie that she is not the same as that little kid who planned to commit a mass shooting, Charlie can't wrap his head around it. For one, Emma is a millennial American black woman. Being American comes with its set of desensitization to American gun culture. Being a black woman growing up as one of the few in school has its own alienation issues. What would Charlie, a millennial British white man know about Emma's experiences? Emma doesn't need to justify *why* she almost did it, but Charlie needs to know, something that is truly unreachable for Charlie. They're in an interracial marriage for God's sake! You'd think Charlie would understand the nuances of doing so, but alas, he is like a deer in headlights with these situations.

But I don't think you need to *truly* get to know someone to love them. I think Charlie got a bit caught up in society's opinions rather than his own feelings and had a break down trying to reconcile with both. Understanding someone and knowing they do good things seems enough. I think people's gut instincts are stronger in these situations than one might think. Charlie walks back to an empty house after the wedding, bloodied nose and all, and realizes how he's fucked up – he *does* love Emma and her absence makes him miserable. The chaos was unnecessary. So, does it truly matter if you will never truly get to know someone? I don't think so. As the saying goes: if you know, you know.
