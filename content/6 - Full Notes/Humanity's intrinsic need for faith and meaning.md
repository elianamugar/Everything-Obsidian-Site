# Humanity's intrinsic need for faith and meaning

When Kolya and Smurov hangout, the narrator says that Kolya knows Smurov worships him. But who did Kolya worship? It certainly wasn't doctors, peasants, or God.

Kolya calls Alyosha "a prophet" by the end of their initial meeting ([[The Brothers Karamazov (1880) by Fyodor Dostoevsky#507]]). Kolya falls in love with Alyosha and worships him. Alyosha, himself, clearly worships God and deeply admires Father Zossima. Ivan contrasts Alyosha, who struggles with faith and rejects God. The way Dostoevsky depicts Alyosha with unwavering faith and being happy versus Ivan in despair with a sense of meaninglessness tells his belief that humanity *needs* faith and *needs* meaning.

Dostoevsky wrote down three depictions of worship:
1. Smurov worshipping Kolya,
2. Kolya worshipping Alyosha,
3. Alyosha worshipping God/Father Zossima,
and he Ivan as a depiction of what happens when one lacks worship.

Through the examples mentioned and the contrasting perspectives of Alyosha and Ivan, Dostevsky explores the concept of worship, highlighting the human need for faith and meaning – ultimately leading to a sense of identity and purpose.
