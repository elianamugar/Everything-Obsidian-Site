# System equilibrium in IR and Physics

In the 1800s, many scientists observed the [[Law of Conservation of Energy]], which states that energy cannot be created, nor destroyed, only transformed. This law pervades the sciences: physics, chemistry, thermodynamics, biology, engineering... you name it. Another field it surprisingly applies to is a *social science*: international relations. 

In international relations, there is a school of thought known as [[Realism]], which focuses on the competitive and conflictual side of global [[Politics]]. One theory they constantly flesh out is the [[balance of power theory]], in which actors (being states) in an international system (which is inherently [[anarchy]]) will always attempt to maintain a [[balance of power]]. For example, if one state gains power, then another state will try to offset that power by either creating a counter-alliance or [[War]]. Of course, this is an over-simplification of the many internal conflicts within [[Realism]] as there are many realists who debate how the exact mechanics of this [[balance of power]] is maintained, but that is the gist. 

To be more specific, one realist scholar, by the name of Robert [[Gilpin]] (1930 – 2018) emphasized in his work that in the international system, there is a tendency for powers of member states to change at different rates in terms of political, economic, and technological developments. He claims this differential growth in power of various states in the international system causes a **fundamental redistribution of power** in the system. This puts on full display that same principle of the [[Law of Conservation of Energy]]: energy cannot be created, nor destroyed, only transformed. In this case: power cannot be created, nor destroyed, only transformed. Much like balancing a chemical equation where the number of each element is equal on both sides, or a mathematical equation where the input must equal the output – within a closed system (or in the case of the [[Realism]] realm of international relations, an *international system*), the total energy/power remains constant, where energy/power is balanced by other states. The power of states is always shifting, but the total international system will tend towards an equilibrium state. Once that balance is offset by *something*, the states in the system will work to maintain that, whether through [[War]] or peace, conflict or cooperation.

It is through this principle of the [[balance of power]] that I'm reminded of the [[Law of Conservation of Energy]]: energy cannot be created, nor destroyed, only transformed. This is applicable to [[Realism]]'s theory of [[balance of power]] in which the energy, which would be *power* in this case, cannot be created, nor destroyed, only transformed. It's this maintenance of equilibrium in both the sciences and the social science of international relations that makes the [[Law of Conservation of Energy]] so intriguing.
