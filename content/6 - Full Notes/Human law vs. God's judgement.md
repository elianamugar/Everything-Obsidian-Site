# Human law vs. God's judgement

In Book 11, Chapter 8 ("The Third and Last Interview with Smerdyakov"), Ivan confronts Smerdyakov, discussing who is the true murderer of Fyodor Karamazov. This is the conversation they have ([[The Brothers Karamazov (1880) by Fyodor Dostoevsky#565]]):

>Ivan: Do you know, I am afraid that you are a dream, a phantom sitting before me.
>Smerdyakov: There's no phantom here, but only us two... And one other. He is here, that third one, between us.
>Ivan: Who is he? Who is here? What third person?
>Smerdyakov: That third is God Himself, Providence. He is the third One beside us now. Only don't look for Him, you won't find Him.

Through this conversation, we see logical Ivan try and grapple with who murdered his father. Smerdyakov argues that Ivan willed it, even though Smerdyakov was the mechanism, therefore Ivan is guilty. But Smerdyakov means that Ivan is guilty in God's eyes, maybe not so much in their law. In law, Smerdyakov would be the guilty one, but Smerdyakov points to a much higher authority.

Dostoevsky explores whether man's law matters considering God will judge everyone in the end.
