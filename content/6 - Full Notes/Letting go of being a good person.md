# Letting go of being a good person

On my YouTube homepage, I stumbled upon [this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xuMcuD2aMCA), which is also the title of this mini-essay. Just the title alone triggered what I've been exploring since watching *The Drama* (2016) – what does it mean to be a good person? 

In the video, Goobie talks about finally understanding what Billy Joel's song *Only the Good Die Young*, truly means. I admit I haven't listened to the song myself, but maybe I'll get around to it. When he first listened to the song, Goobie didn't really understand why that is the case. Why *do* the good die young? The way he interprets it now is that *good people* have to sacrifice being good to themselves and put others before self, to a detrimental effect of the self.

He continues to flesh this out saying, he doesn't mean he will be a *bad person* – everyone is *good* and *bad*. He subliminally establishes that *good* and *bad* is an identity thrust upon someone based on the judgment of others. This reminds me of a concept we learned early on in [[Linguistics]] where [[Identity]] is based on others judgment of you. When you're alone in your room with no external judgment, you have no identity (sort of like [[Schrodinger]]'s cat). And so the *internal* pressure to be a *good person*, that is, the *internal* pressure to be *good* in the eyes of others, is a detrimental cyclical nature that destroys your being.

So, Goobie decides he will let go of the pressure to be a *good person*. He will just *be* without worrying of the external judgment. It reminds me of this interview where David Corenswet is asked in an interview (pre-*Superman*) if his character is trying to be a good person. This must've been regarding *Hollywood* or *Pearl* press junket. I can't find the original interview anywhere, but in paraphrasing, he said something along the lines of how it's impossible to be a *good* person, like there's no real definition to being a *good person*, but rather *doing* good things is all you really have control over. Doing good things is more objective (though not completely, I'll be honest), but I think I imagine picking up a piece of litter on the ground every day, no matter how small, and that's a good thing that'll make a difference, rather than that making me a *good person*.

So, yeah, let's get rid of this grandiose idea of being a *good person*. It's a tall order to ask for and I think with the current day's obsession with being morally superior and diagnosing how to be morally good it's all getting out of hand. Do good things. Just be.
