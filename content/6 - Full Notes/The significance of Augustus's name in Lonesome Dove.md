# The significance of Augustus's name in Lonesome Dove

In the opening pages of *Lonesome Dove*, I'm introduced to Augustus, who becomes informally known as Gus in the book. When reading the name "Augustus", immediately what came to mind was [[Augustus Caesar]], after all I had recently read [[Augustus (1972) by John Williams]]. Augustus Caesar, who was known as Octavius Caesar before becoming the first emperor of the [[Roman Empire]], starting the successful period known as the [[Pax Romana]]. He was adopted by [[Julius Caesar]] who last known leader (more so a dictator and military leader) of the [[Roman Republic]] after he was assassinated by Roman senators ([[the Ides of March]]). Augustus Caesar was known as a private, modest, and introspective leader, embracing [[Stoicism]] for his leadership style.

In contradiction, Gus is an overly chatty character, so much so that the quieter members of the Hat Creek Cattle Company don't want to be left alone with him and would prefer the silence. But in a similar way, Gus was seen as a great leader, a leader who seemed to be immortal and everyone felt safer with him around. Funnily enough, Gus is also an intellectual (which also contributes to his chattiness and philosophizing), having been educated in Tennessee and almost had higher prospects into a noble profession, but decided to quit while he was ahead and become a Texas Ranger. He does have charm, much like [[Augustus Caesar]] did, but he also had a laziness to him, which [[Augustus Caesar]] did not.

At the end of the book, Gus dies in a noble battle. It was an impressive standoff, one against all. The wounds keep him long enough to survive and ride into Miles City to get looked at by a doctor, but he dies to the poisonous arrows. This reminds me of [[Augustus Caesar]]'s end. Like Gus, he dies. Both their deaths circle back to the themes of [[mortality]] in the book. Though [[Augustus Caesar]] died of natural causes and old age and Augustus McCrae died to arrow wounds and battle scars, they both *died*. They both had an end, like all humans have done, like all humans do, and like all humans will eventually have.
