# Be yourself

Kolya, a thirteen-year-old boy, is determined to be defined as something more than his age. He wants to be identified by beliefs (he claims he's a Socialist because he read about it and the concept made sense to him). But, being the young boy he is, he struggles with finding that identity and pretends that he is steadfast in what he believes in. Alyosha breaks that down.

Kolya confides in Alyosha, telling him he's unhappy. Kolya then asks Alyosha, "Am I being very ridiculous now?" ([[The Brothers Karamazov (1880) by Fyodor Dostoevsky#506]]) To which Alyosha replies:
>"Don't think about that, don't think of it at all! And what does ridiculous mean? Isn't everyone constantly being or seeming ridiculous? Besides, nearly all clever people are afraid of being ridiculous, and that makes them unhappy. I am surprised that you should feel ridiculous so early, though nowadays little children have begun to suffer from it. It's almost a sort of insanity. The devil has taken the form of that vanity and entered into the whole generation. It's the devil. You are like everyone else. That is, like very many others. Only you must not be like everybody else, that's all." ([[The Brothers Karamazov (1880) by Fyodor Dostoevsky#506]])

Kolya further asks, "Even if everyone is like that?" ([[The Brothers Karamazov (1880) by Fyodor Dostoevsky#506]])

And Alyosha reassures him:
>"Yes, even if everyone is like that. You be the only one no like that. You really are not like everyone else; you are not ashamed to confess to something bad and ridiculous. And who today will admit so much? No one. People have even ceased to feel the impulse of self-criticism. Don't be like everyone else, even if you are the only one." ([[The Brothers Karamazov (1880) by Fyodor Dostoevsky#506]])

Though it's tempting to want to fit in, to find an identity, the best thing one can do for themselves is to not follow the masses.
