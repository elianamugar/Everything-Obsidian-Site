# Similarities of Gus and Jude

In *A Little Life*, Jude is a Sisyphean character who goes through the trials and tribulations of his life, an uphill battle. In *Lonesome Dove*, Gus (short for Augustus) is a laidback ex-Texas Ranger and nihilistic leader of a cattle herd company.

When Jude and Willem (his best friend and life partner) discuss the topic of happiness, they have the following dialogue:
>Are you happy? He once asked Jude (they must have been drunk).
>**I don’t think happiness is for me**, Jude had said at last, **as if Willem had been offering him a dish he didn’t want to eat**. But it’s for you, Willem. ([[A Little Life (2015) by Hanya Yanagihara#264]])

Gus and Call (his ex-Texas Ranger and cattle herd company partner) also discuss happiness, when Gus recalls a time with his "the one who got away" – Clara:
>"When was you happiest, Call?" Augustus asked.
>"Happiest about what?" Call asked.
>"Just about being a live human being, free on the earth," Augustus said.
>"Well, it's hard to single out any one particular time," Call said.
>"**It ain't for me,**" Augustus said. "I was happiest right back there by that little creek. I fell short of the mark and lost the woman, but the times were sweet."

The way Gus and Jude both describe happiness as something that's "not for them" exudes the similarities they have as nihilistic characters. The only reason they keep living is for the other people around them. Jude lives on his life, suffering, for Willem's happiness and Gus drives the cattle to Montana for Call's happiness. 

It is interesting to note that Gus's character is usually jovial and optimistic, so this conversation is a bit of a shock to me as the reader. Maybe it's a change of character for Gus, or perhaps a lapse in change of character.

At this point in time, I don't know how Gus's story will end, but Jude's ends in a tragedy where he finally takes one selfish action to live life the way he wants to – which is to take his own life away. By that model, I predict Gus will end in a similar fate.
