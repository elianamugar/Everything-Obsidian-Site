# Existence through thought

Disney's *Coco* (2017)  covers the concept of The Day of the Dead (*el día de los muertos*) where you remember your loved ones that have passed into the afterlife because the core belief is that if you don't remember your loved ones, they will stop existing forever, as if they have never existed at all.

In Sally Rooney's *Intermezzo* (2024), Ivan has a discussion with Margaret (his lover) about his father's death, saying:
> The person who's gone has no reality anymore, except in thoughts. And once they're gone from thoughts, they actually are completely gone. If I don't think about him, literally, I'm ending his existence. ([[Intermezzo (2024) by Sally Rooney#383]])

When I read this quote, it reminded me of *Coco*, about keeping people in our "thoughts and prayers" as people usually say – to keep their existence alive. The concept of the afterlife and existence, though it spans from Mexican culture, has landed into this Irish story and I thought it was cool to see this concept span across different cultures. It shows humans share similarities, even across continents.
