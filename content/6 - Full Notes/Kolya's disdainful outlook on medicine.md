# Kolya's disdained outlook on medicine

When Kolya hangs out with his friend Smurov (who he deems as less intelligent), they discuss Doctor Herzenstube – the doctor assigned to Ilusha. Kolya says that doctors are "fakes," following that up with "I don't believe in medicine. It's useless ([[The Brothers Karamazov (1880) by Fyodor Dostoevsky#479]])."  Then, at the beginning of book 10, chapter 6 when Ilusha's illness is nearing his death, Kolya exclaims: "\[Doctors] are fakes! Medicine's a fraud!" ([[The Brothers Karamazov (1880) by Fyodor Dostoevsky#502]]).

Alyosha then asks Kolya if he believes in God. Kolya's reply is indifferent ("I've nothing against God. Of course, God is only a hypothesis, but... I admit that He is needed... for the order of the universe and all that... And that if there were no God He would have to be invented.")([[The Brothers Karamazov (1880) by Fyodor Dostoevsky#503]]). As Ilusha's illness worsens and worsens, Ilusha's father (the captain) asks the doctor if there's anything he can do to fix it. The doctor replies, "I can't help it, I am not God!... It's not in my hands ([[The Brothers Karamazov (1880) by Fyodor Dostoevsky#508]])."

Kolya's hatred towards medicine plays hand-in-hand with the idea of doctors playing God with life. Though it's not true that doctors are equivalent to God, I think Kolya dislikes the idea that people deem doctors as equivalent to God, equivalent to determining the outcome of people's lives, when in fact, doctors do not have that power.
