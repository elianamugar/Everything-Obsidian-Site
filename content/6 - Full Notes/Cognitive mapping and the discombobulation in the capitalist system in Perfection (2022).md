# Cognitive mapping and the discombobulation in the capitalist system in Perfection (2022)

In Vincenzo Latronico's *Perfection* (2022), Anna and Tom are two Italian natives who explore the ex-pat life in Berlin, Germany. The reader goes through a journey of observation, almost studying Anna and Tom's psychology and emotions through a lens similar to a BBC animal nature documentary with Latronico's descriptive verbiage. Anna and Tom are a couple that never felt like they belonged to their home base in Italy and set out on a journey to try and create their own existence, to define their self-worth. With this sentiment, they finally felt they had "made it" when they moved to Berlin.

> It was a life \[Anna and Tom] had created for themselves, building difference upon difference until it encapsulated the real them, with a freedom they would never have had back at home. ([[Perfection (2022) by Vincenzo Latronico#39]])

This quote stuck out to me because I recently watched a YouTube video by Shannon Kim that discusses Frederic Jameson's "cognitive mapping" theoretical-aesthetic framework that describes an individual's ability to conceptually visualize and comprehend their place within the vast, complex, and unrepresentable totality of global, late-stage capitalism. To be exact, this is what Kim has to say on the topic:

> One of the defining conditions of living inside postmodern capitalism is that you cannot actually see or visualize the structure that you're living because the vastness of the global economic systems that occupy are so structurally complexified to the point that it's so abstract to actually be perceived, which makes the individual constantly disoriented and detached from any sense of personal locality or geography in a way.... \[Jameson] refers to the capitalist system as a grid and as a visual logic of a system that flattens everything into equivalent units and into very specific objects that is able to make everything exchangeable and everything to be commodified and disorient the individual and make everything tradeable in the capitalist system.

So when Anna and Tom think they've "made it" by moving to Berlin and that they've created the life they wanted for themselves, it's truly just a facade. When sifting under the system that is currently dominated by capitalism, is it truly possible to "create" this life for yourself? That's a question subconsciously posed to the readers, but a question that doesn't Anna and Tom's minds in this particular moment. In the system of capitalism, everything is curated for you, and if there are options, there are only limited options. Someone with $0 can have the option of any food in the world, but ultimately cannot buy any of them because the limiting factor is their $0. For Anna and Tom, they could have stayed or gone, and to leave Italy was another web of choices in itself, but ultimately they stayed in the EU because, subtextually, it is the most reasonable limiting choice. They can just go home if times get hard in Berlin. It's not as easy if you move outside the EU zone.

I also recently wrote a mini-essay on [[Identity]] in regards to linguistics and international relations, but it also applies here. In a concept of analyzing linguistic identity, two key principles apply here: **partialness** and **relationality**. To recap, partialness means no one can be 100% of their identity at all times and relationality means the emergence of a person's identity is relative to the identities of those they interact with. 

In the case of Anna and Tom, what if 100% of your identity is spread too thin (partiality)? Throughout the book, they're constantly juggling being a native Italian, an English-speaker, a so-so German speaker, a wannabe translator for refugees, an activist, a digital nomad, an ex-pat, a tourist, etc. and they never really stick the landing. For periods of time, they feel like they've settled on an identity, but that changes (which is normal as identity is seen as dynamic), but what if that change is too often and they become all of these identities spread too thin at one time? Then do they really have an identity at all?

On the point of relationality, what if Anna and Tom's interactions with the world around them are all artificial to the point where everyone's interactions are essentially the same. In effect, everyone would emerge... the same? Is that the same as having no identity? Is that the same as not interacting with anything, which means having no identity? Because on the point of relationality, being alone and not interacting with anything is equivalent to not having an identity because you have no relative identities to interact with. 

This goes back to Kim's point: "One of the defining conditions of living inside postmodern capitalism is that you cannot actually see or visualize the structure that you're living because the vastness of the global economic systems that occupy are so structurally complexified to the point that it's so abstract to actually be perceived, **which makes the individual constantly disoriented and detached from any sense of personal locality or geography in a way.**" In the case of Anna and Tom, capitalism is so vast to them (and us as the readers) that *we* don't know how much it controls us. Just like Anna and Tom, we're constantly in a state of disorientation and we feel like we don't belong to anything. All our interactions are artificially curated in such a way to make us believe that our interactions are spontaneous and natural, but in reality, it's all under this *system* that has given us the illusion of vastness and the illusion of choice.

*Perfection* (2022) really ties together these ideas of "cognitive mapping", capitalism, the age old dilemma of finding a sense of belonging, and the modern dilemma of millennial malaise – a generation-wide phenomenon of chronic fatigue, existential disillusionment, and burnout. It stems from the cognitive dissonance o being raised with optimistic "follow your dreams" messaging, only to enter adulthood during the 2008 financial crisis, a global pandemic, and a heavily inflated cost-of-living landscape. This now bleeds into the up-and-coming Gen Z malaise which is now characterized by a collective sense of [[Nihilism]].
