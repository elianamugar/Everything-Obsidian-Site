# Iran-Contra Affair

During Reagan’s campaign run for presidency in 1980, his administration got in touch with Iran since Iran was holding the 52 hostages. The message to Iran was to not release the hostages until Reagan was elected. On Inauguration Day, the hostages were released. After winning, the Reagan administration brings up the idea with the Iranian leadership of selling US weapons to Iran. Iran needs US weapons desperately to fight Iraq (which was a US ally), so they agree even though Reagan condemns Iran throughout his campaign run; the Reagan administration fulfills the promise. The US became heavily involved with the Iran-Iraq war by supporting both sides: Iran was supported secretly and Iraq was supported publicly. In order to sell weapons through Iran, a public US enemy but a private ally, the US would have to use Israel as a back channel to secretly sell weapons to Iran. Because of the Camp David Accords, Israel was allowed to buy/resupply weapons from the US. This was how the US would sell weapons to Iran--through Israel.

​Meanwhile, Lebanon was a country that played a role in the Iran-Contra affair. During the 1970s through the 1980s, Lebanese society was shattered after a multi-sided civil war destroyed it. As a result, the Lebanese government lost control and ultimately led to a non-functional government. Because of the lack of control in Lebanon, terrorist organizations set up in Lebanon to attack Israel, since Lebanon was geographically close to Israel. Some of these terrorist organizations sitting in Lebanon were supplied by Iran with money, weapons, and advisors which were supplied by Israel by the US. In short, Israel played a part in handing over weapons to terrorist organizations which were ultimately against Israel--Israel was somewhat playing a part in attacking itself.

​In 1981, terrorist organizations in Lebanon started taking US hostages from the American University in Beirut. By 1985, it was revealed that 7 US hostages were taken by terrorist organizations in Lebanon. Reagan wants to release the hostages, but doesn’t want to negotiate with the terrorists for fear of more hostages being taken. But because the Carter presidency was publicly destroyed by the Iran Hostage Crisis, Reagan feared that this Lebanese hostage crisis would shatter his own presidency. Later in 1985, the families of the hostages visited Reagan, pleading for him to release the hostages. As a result, Reagan approached his advisors to organize a way to release the hostages. The advisors remembered the private selling of weapons as a connection to Iran: if the US privately offers to sell weapons to Iran through Israel (the back channel), then the US gets the hostages. Some advisors opposed this idea because Iran is the ultimate enemy due to terrorism--US had embargoes and no trade relations with Iran, so this would be a foul US move. Reagan didn’t care too much about the logistics of this situation, he just wanted to release the hostages. So, in 1985, Israel secretly sold US weapons to Iran as the US publicly resupplied Israel. While Israel sells weapons to Iran, the US profits from the deals, but it’s dirty money so the US has to store it in a secret Swiss bank account.

​From the 1930s to late 1970s, Nicaragua was ruled by a single family of dictators. In the late 1970s, this family was overthrown by the communist-backed Sandinistas. For a while, the US has rapport with the dictator family, but they were condemned by the Carter presidency because of their inhumaneness. After the successful Sandinistas overthrowing, Carter doesn’t get involved and Reagan perceives this as Carter’s weakness--something Reagan targets during his campaign run. In Nicaragua, there was a group of rebels against the communist Sandinistas called the Contras. Reagan pressures Carter on the campaign trail to support the Contras and to fight communism, rather than being soft on communism. In a sense, Reagan was strong on the roll back of communism. Carter strongly disagrees with Reagan because the Contras are unpopular, brutal drug runners who transport drugs from South America to the US. After Carter loses the presidency to Reagan, Reagan funds the Contras. After investigation, Congress realized that Carter was correct about the Contras--they were unpopular, brutal drug runners. In the 1980s, Congress officially stated that the US will cut off all aid to the Contras rebels. This angers Reagan, so he tries to find any and every possible way to fund the Contras.

​In 1985, with the US secretly storing money in the secret Swiss bank from the capital gained from the secret US-Iran weapons deals, the NSC realized that the US could use this secret money to fund the Contras rebels by flying them into Nicaragua. This was very illegal since Congress officially passed a law to not aid the Contras rebels. In October 1986, the Sandinista government shot down an airplane flying over Nicaragua illegally, revealing that a CIA pilot was part of providing weapons to Contras rebels. This exposed the big scandal of Reagan and the whole Iran-Contra affair exploded within the US public.

​In 1987, Gorbachev reached out to Reagan (again) to propose a reduction treaty of missiles. This was quite a radical missile treaty--both sides would cut all medium and intermediate range nuclear missiles. After Reagan’s history of rejecting Gorbachev’s proposals of missile reduction treaties, Reagan finally agrees because 1) SDI wasn’t actually feasible (it would just lead to an arms race between the US and USSR), so Reagan didn’t have to keep defending it and 2) he needed to save his presidency. As a result, at the end of 1987, Reagan and Gorbachev signed the [[Intermediate Nuclear Force Treaty]] to get rid of all medium and intermediate range nuclear missions which had the potential of hitting Europe at the Washington Summit. The signing signified that Gorbachev and Reagan were open to peace, leading towards the end of the [[Cold War]] and establishing that US-USSR relations were growing.
