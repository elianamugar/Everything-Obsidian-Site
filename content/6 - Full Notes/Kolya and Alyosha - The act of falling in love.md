# Kolya and Alyosha - The act of falling in love

Kolya initially fears the idea of meeting Alyosha Karamazov, but by the end of their meeting, Kolya comes to worship him.

Kolya is nervous about meeting Alyosha, but in reality he's been looking forward to it. He wants to "show his independence" – in other words, Kolya wants to impress Alyosha. In front of the other boys, Kolya was cool and he, himself, thought he was complete. Kolya then goes through "the fall," realizing all along he had been missing something to worship. After some difficult discussions and Kolya's identity and beliefs wavering, he respected Alyosha's efforts to speak to him as a whole person.

Kolya tells Alyosha "Do you know, Karamazov, our talk has been like a declaration of love\[?] ([[The Brothers Karamazov (1880) by Fyodor Dostoevsky#507]])." Kolya then tells Alyosha that he is a prophet. Kolya's point of breaking, his confession, happens when he says, "I loved you awfully. I've been loving and dreaming of you."

Kolya and Alyosha's meeting demonstrates *the fall* required to love.
