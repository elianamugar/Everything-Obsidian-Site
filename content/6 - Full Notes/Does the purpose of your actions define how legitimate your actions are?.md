# Does the purpose of your actions define how legitimate your actions are?

In *The Drama* (2026), four friends meet up for a wine tasting in preparation of Emma and Charlie's wedding. Rachel starts the inciting action with a question: *What's the worst thing you've ever done?* Everyone goes around answering (more in detail in [this mini-essay](obsidian://open?vault=Everything&file=6%20-%20Full%20Notes%2FDo%20your%20thoughts%20or%20actions%20define%20you%3F)) and everyone finds you, Emma *almost* committed a school mass shooting. But she didn't.

This is the crux of the film. All the surrounding characters grapple with this fact, that Emma *almost* committed a school mass shooting, but only Charlie truly knows *why* she didn't commit the school mass shooting. It was because there was a nearby mall mass shooting that happened before she planned to do it. A classmate of hers died in that mall mass shooting. The school held a grief counseling session and that changed her way of life. On a classroom discussion of mass shootings, a classmate answers that it's men's faults. They're the ones doing it. Emma rebuts: no, there have been female mass shootings. The classmate counters: Not in America. Emma (and the audience) can only rebut in thought: no, *she* would've been the one to commit a mass shooting. But Emma's words of "inspiration" inspired another a classmate, who just so happens to be the anti-gun school club leader, and he invites Emma to join their club. Emma joins and becomes a vocal spokesperson on anti-gun laws and even gets arrested at a student protest.

Charlie tries to grapple with the over-simplified reason on why she didn't commit a mass shooting: because there was one before her. It truly is an over-simplified reason because yes, the event itself may have been the catalyst, but the following events *changed* her for the better. The truth is that, yes, mall shooting *did* happen. And after that, she *did* the anti-gun protests. She *did* change and that's evident in the *actions* she took course. The action Emma *didn't* take course was doing a school mass shooting, which is important.

What I'm getting at here is that Charlie seems to think that the purpose behind *why* Emma *didn't* do a mass school shooting doesn't justify her changing from *bad* to *good*. There's many situations why purpose matters, but also why it *doesn't matter*. For example, if you only give compliments simply because it makes *you* feel good, does that make you a bad person because you're being selfish? No! Putting out compliments in the world is an overall *good thing* for society. Purpose is a great guiding direction on how to live life, but certainly it's a case-to-case basis on when it applies and when it helps. Hell, sometimes I'm asked *Why did you do \[insert action]*? And sometimes my answer is, I don't know, I just felt like it! Sometimes I throw away my trash because it feels like the right thing to do. Sometimes people do bad things because it feels like the right thing to do. At the end of the day, purpose is a guiding principle, but not *the* guiding principle.

No, the purpose of your actions define how legitimate your actions are. Purpose is abstract. Actions are concrete. One leaves an imprint on history and other people, the other is internal, within the recesses of the mind and soul.
